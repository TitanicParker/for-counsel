# Case Overview

> **Status:** counsel-facing working case map. This page separates primary fact, reported fact, inference, unknown matters, legal propositions and questions requiring expert evidence. It is not legal or medical advice.

## 1. The issue in one paragraph

Liam entered Neurology in 2017 with a severe parkinsonian syndrome and a disabling bilateral foot/lower-limb problem. The surviving clinical record itself retained uncertainty about the relationship of the feet to the neurological syndrome. Podiatry nevertheless became an operative foot pathway. The accessible documentary corpus does not presently demonstrate the reasoning step by which that unresolved neurological question was established as sufficiently separate or resolved to justify treating the podiatry disposition itself as proof of neurological closure.

**Qualification.** This is a documentary/evidential proposition. It does not by itself prove that podiatry was clinically inappropriate, that Parkinson's disease was a negligent diagnosis, that levodopa should have been commenced in 2017, or that any clinician acted dishonestly.

The organising question is therefore not “was Parkinson's wrong?” It is: **what does the source record actually establish about the unresolved foot/lower-limb problem, how did podiatry become operative, and what independent evidence later validated — or did not yet demonstrably validate — that transition?**

## 2. The secure factual chronology

### 2017 entry and neurological assessment

- The 29 June 2017 GP referral records an unusual festinant/stiff gait, **bilateral foot issues**, difficulty initiating movement, stiffness/increased tone in upper and lower limbs, brisk reflexes and functional deterioration. [Primary source: GP referral](docs/source-records/gp-referral.html).
- The 2017 Neurology material records substantial parkinsonism, an unusually young/atypical presentation, broader differential thinking, abnormal lower-limb/gait features described in dystonic terms, and deliberate deferral of dopaminergic treatment. [Primary source: discharge summary](docs/source-records/discharge-summary.html) · [2017 notes](docs/source-records/neurology-notes-2017.html).
- The extended discharge record records uncertainty about whether the feet were separate/neuropathic and states that their relationship to the neurological syndrome was **“Not clear.”** Podiatry nevertheless became the practical foot pathway. [Primary source: discharge summary](docs/source-records/discharge-summary.html).
- Parkinson's had become an operative diagnostic/administrative state before the later DaTSCAN result. The chronology must be assessed from the information available at each point; the later scan cannot be back-projected into the earlier state.
- The 13 July 2017 DaTSCAN was abnormal and independently supports a presynaptic dopaminergic/parkinsonian disorder. It does **not** by itself establish the cause of the foot symptoms or prove that podiatry was the correct neurological resolution. [Primary source: 2017 notes / DaTSCAN material](docs/source-records/neurology-notes-2017.html).

### Longitudinal record

The foot/lower-limb problem did not disappear from Neurology. Later records document recurrent foot symptoms and later dystonia/OFF/toe-curling material; the 2023 record records a “misalignment” about the explanatory frame and records that the earliest symptoms were in the feet. [Primary source: 2023 notes](docs/source-records/neurology-notes-2023.html).

A 2025 Neurology record **records** the management-history statement that podiatry “makes things worse in the foot.” That is evidence that the statement was recorded; it should not be converted into an independent clinician finding that podiatry caused deterioration unless the full source expressly supports that conclusion. [Primary source: 2025 notes](docs/source-records/neurology-notes-2025.html).

Later dopaminergic treatment, including Sinemet/Sinemet CR, materially modified the foot symptoms. The later response is relevant to continuity, materiality and later reassessment; it is not hindsight proof that 2017 care was negligent. [Primary source: 2025 notes](docs/source-records/neurology-notes-2025.html) · [consultant complaint response](docs/source-records/official-rebuttal.html).

### 2025 governance and data-protection sequence

- By **19 March 2025**, Liam was expressly attributing prolonged harm to the historical diagnostic/treatment pathway. This is a serious adverse date-of-knowledge fact for any broad historical clinical claim.
- Article 16 rectification/completion issues were expressly raised in 2025.
- Liam expressly invoked Article 18 on **4 August 2025**, including QPS and DPO addresses and specifically referring to the SIMT process.
- A PAR concerning care from July 2017 to March 2025 existed, was presented to SIMT on **27 August 2025**, and a multidisciplinary SIMT concluded **“no deficit in care.”** The outcome refers to risk factors associated with starting Sinemet in a young person, alternative diagnoses, and first Sinemet in 2021.
- The complete PAR/source architecture is not presently available in this repository.

## 3. The documentary hinge

The narrow documentary sequence is:

**presenting bilateral feet**
→ **abnormal neurological/lower-limb findings**
→ **relationship “Not clear”**
→ **podiatry disposition**
→ **no presently demonstrated reasoning bridge establishing neurological closure**
→ **longitudinal persistence of the same problem**.

The fact of a podiatry disposition is secure. What the accessible record does **not** prove by itself is that the neurological relationship had been established as separate, that podiatry constituted neurological resolution, or that the patient accepted that transition.

“Temporal compression” is useful analytical shorthand for the risk of reading the later DaTSCAN into an earlier decision state. It is not a legal doctrine.

## 4. What remains reliable

The reliability issue is proposition-specific. It is **not** a claim that the entire medical record is unreliable.

Secure or highly supported matters include:

- substantial parkinsonian syndrome;
- bilateral foot issues forming part of the presenting picture;
- young/atypical features and broader differential thinking;
- abnormal lower-limb/dystonic-looking features;
- deliberate deferral of dopaminergic treatment;
- the recorded rationale for that deferral;
- an operative Parkinson's certification/diagnostic state;
- abnormal later DaTSCAN;
- longitudinal continuation of the foot problem;
- later dopaminergic responsiveness;
- existence of Article 16/18 challenges;
- existence of PAR/SIMT and its “no deficit in care” conclusion.

## 5. What the record cannot presently prove by itself

The following are **not safe for unqualified reliance** without independent evidence:

- that the feet were established as separate in 2017;
- that podiatry itself proves neurological resolution;
- that Liam accepted the transition to podiatry;
- that absence of a recorded protest proves no protest occurred;
- that “good understanding” proves global agreement with diagnosis/discharge;
- that later repetition automatically validates the original transition;
- that SIMT automatically constitutes independent factual corroboration of the disputed hinge.

The “good understanding” wording should be kept in its local treatment-counselling context unless a primary source supports a broader meaning.

## 6. Why the later foot history matters

Later evidence is relevant because it demonstrates **continuity, materiality, later reassessment and possible clinical consequence**. It is not used here as hindsight proof of 2017 negligence.

The later record includes recurrent foot symptoms; toe-curling/dystonia/OFF material; the 2023 “misalignment”; later acknowledgement that the feet were early symptoms; the 2025 note recording that podiatry made things worse in the foot; and later medication response, including Sinemet/Sinemet CR response.

The defensible inference is limited: the original foot problem remained clinically consequential and continued to require neurological interpretation. Whether that implies a 2017 breach, and whether earlier treatment would have altered outcome, require independent expert evidence.

## 7. Decision provenance

The presently safe formulation is:

> The material strongly supports a consultative/collective element in the levodopa-deferral decision. It does not presently establish that Counihan directly treated Liam, exercised final authority, acted dishonestly or concealed his involvement.

The provenance material includes Monaghan, the contemporaneous “Myself and Tim” wording, Healy correspondence, and later Monaghan identification of Counihan advice. “Tim = Counihan” should not be treated as self-proving from the original wording alone unless the primary provenance is independently fixed.

The question for disclosure is the exact role of each participant: who attended, who advised, who decided, and what source record records that authority.

## 8. Dependency and later institutional authority

**Multiple documents do not necessarily mean multiple independent evidential origins.**

A chain such as:

`2017 treating record → Monaghan 2025 → PAR → SIMT → QPS 2026`

may be one evidential lineage if later stages simply repeat or adopt the original proposition.

Conversely:

`GP observations + neurological examination + DaTSCAN + later independent clinical observations`

are genuinely multiple evidential origins for a parkinsonian syndrome.

The relevant question is therefore not how many documents repeat a proposition, but **where each proposition ultimately comes from and whether a later stage independently tested it**.

Terms such as “dependency severance”, “recursive validation”, “institutional inheritance” and “impeached hinge” are project analytical shorthand only. They are not Irish legal doctrines.

## 10. GDPR

The data-protection issue is separate from clinical negligence.

### Article 15 / access

Access/search issues may matter to locating witness, trainee, provenance and governance records. A negative search result does not itself prove that an encounter or protest did not occur.

### Article 16 / rectification and completion

Article 16 should not be framed as a right to rewrite a historical diagnosis. The safer formulation, consistent with DPC guidance, is:

- historical clinical opinions can remain as historical opinions;
- objectively inaccurate facts may be rectified;
- incomplete personal data may potentially be completed, including by supplementary statement;
- the present question is whether the current dataset is sufficiently complete and qualified for its continuing purposes.

### Article 18 / restriction

Established: Liam expressly invoked Article 18 on 4 August 2025; QPS and DPO addresses were included; the request specifically referred to SIMT; PAR preparation continued and SIMT occurred later that month.

Unknown: whether restriction was accepted, implemented, refused, qualified or lifted; or whether later processing occurred under an Article 18(2) basis.

Accordingly, the present issue is not an established allegation of unlawful SIMT processing. It is:

> **What operational effect, if any, did the Article 18 challenge have on the disputed data state used in the PAR/SIMT process, and can the controller demonstrate it?**

### Accountability

Articles 5, 12, 15, 16, 18, 19 and 24 may be relevant to accuracy, completion, restriction, communications and demonstrability. Any compensation claim under Article 82 separately requires the elements required by the applicable law; infringement alone should not be presented as sufficient.

## 11. Limitation

The limitation section must be adverse-fact complete.

Documentary incompleteness does **not** automatically postpone limitation. The correct proposition is:

> **Documentary incompleteness may be relevant to the factual question of when a particular material fact was actually known or reasonably ascertainable under the statutory date-of-knowledge test.**

The major adverse fact is that by **19 March 2025** Liam was already expressly attributing prolonged harm to the historic clinical/treatment pathway. That date should be treated as a serious defendant-side knowledge date for the broad historical clinical claim.

Later discoveries must be analysed individually as one of the following:

- a genuinely new material fact;
- provenance evidence;
- a new later institutional act;
- a new data-protection infringement;
- or merely better proof of an older claim.

Fraud or concealment should not be used to carry the limitation case. Section 71 requires substantially stronger, actor-specific evidence than presently exists.

## 12. Clinical negligence questions for an expert

The documentary case does not establish breach or causation. An independent movement-disorders expert should be asked to reconstruct the **2017 point-in-time** clinical state without assuming that the podiatry disposition itself proved neurological resolution.

Questions include:

1. Given severe parkinsonism plus a disabling bilateral foot/lower-limb problem with abnormal/dystonic features and an expressly unclear relationship to the neurological syndrome, what investigation, classification and management would a reasonable neurologist have undertaken in July 2017?
2. Could podiatry reasonably have been adjunctive care while the neurological relationship remained unresolved? If so, what follow-up or documentation would ordinarily be expected?
3. Was the levodopa-deferral strategy within a responsible body of neurological practice for a patient of Liam's age and presentation at that time?
4. Would a therapeutic dopaminergic trial, movement-disorders opinion or other investigation reasonably have been indicated, and when?
5. What, if anything, can later dystonia/OFF features and later Sinemet response legitimately imply about the 2017 state without hindsight error?
6. If any breach is identified, what injury and counterfactual outcome can be attributed to it?

The governing professional-standard question should be assessed under Irish clinical-negligence principles, including the Dunne line of authority, by counsel and the appropriate expert.

## 14. Five controlling evidence gaps

Unless primary material is located elsewhere, the present priority gaps are:

1. **Complete PAR + source schedule/evidence matrix/attachments** — determines what factual materials and independent review, if any, supported the SIMT conclusion.
2. **SIMT agenda/minutes/circulation pack** — determines what was supplied, discussed, questioned and resolved on 27 August 2025.
3. **Native EHR discharge/version chronology** — determines creation/finalisation/addendum timing and what version existed at each relevant 2017 point.
4. **Trainee/witness/teaching evidence** — determines whether the reported bedside protest can be independently corroborated or materially tested.
5. **Article 18 DPO/QPS operational trail** — determines whether restriction was accepted, implemented, refused, qualified, lifted or processed under a stated basis before SIMT.

A further high-value provenance source is the complete Complex Case Meeting / Monaghan-Counihan-Healy communication record.

## 15. Case if the protest is never corroborated

The core documentary case does **not** depend entirely on proving the protest.

Without it, the sustainable minimum is:

**GP referral**
+ **bilateral foot-centred presenting problem**
+ **abnormal neurological/lower-limb findings**
+ **relationship “Not clear”**
+ **podiatry disposition**
+ **no presently demonstrated reasoning bridge proving neurological closure**
+ **longitudinal persistence of the same problem**
+ **later dystonia/OFF/toe-curling material**
+ **later neurological treatment response**
+ **2023 recorded misalignment**
+ **2025 note recording adverse podiatry experience**.

That supports a proposition-specific documentary challenge. It still does not establish clinical negligence without expert evidence.

## 16. Case if the protest is corroborated

The reported protest is Liam's later evidence. It is materially relevant because it allegedly occurred at the exact state-transition point. Independent corroboration has not yet been located.

If corroborated by a witness, teaching/trainee record or reliable contemporaneous source, the incremental effect would be that the transition was not merely undocumented or unclear but **affirmatively disputed at the time**. That would strengthen the case that the discharge account was incomplete as to material patient disagreement and would further weaken any attempt to use “good understanding” or silence as proof of global acceptance.

It would not by itself establish negligence, dishonesty or causation.

## 17. What the case does not presently prove

The present materials do **not** safely prove:

- that Parkinson's disease was a negligent or baseless diagnosis;
- that the severe parkinsonian syndrome was not real;
- that podiatry was necessarily inappropriate or could not have been reasonable adjunctive care;
- that levodopa should necessarily have been commenced in 2017;
- that later Sinemet response proves historical breach or causation;
- that all foot symptoms had one neurological cause;
- that the bedside protest occurred;
- that “good understanding” proves more than the context in which it appears;
- that Counihan directly treated Liam, controlled the decision or acted dishonestly;
- that SIMT failed to perform an independent review;
- that the unseen PAR lacks independent revalidation;
- that Article 18 was unlawfully ignored or that SIMT processing was unlawful;
- that documentary incompleteness automatically postpones limitation;
- fraud, collusion, cover-up, dishonest concealment or a section 71 postponement case.

Certain provenance and later-representation questions remain open. **Fraud, reckless concealment and s.71 postponement would require substantially stronger actor-specific evidence than presently exists.**
