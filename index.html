Counsel-facing orientation

A narrow, source-provenanced case

The organising issue is the documentary transition from an unresolved bilateral foot/lower-limb problem to an operative podiatry pathway — not a hindsight claim that Parkinson's disease was simply the wrong diagnosis.

Controlling formulation

The question the record must answer

Liam entered Neurology in 2017 with a severe parkinsonian syndrome and a disabling bilateral foot/lower-limb problem. The surviving clinical record retained uncertainty about the relationship of the feet to the neurological syndrome. Podiatry nevertheless became an operative foot pathway. The accessible documentary corpus does not presently demonstrate the reasoning step by which that unresolved neurological question was established as sufficiently separate or resolved to justify treating the podiatry disposition itself as proof of neurological closure.

Qualification. This is a documentary/evidential proposition. It does not by itself prove that podiatry was clinically inappropriate, that Parkinson's disease was a negligent diagnosis, that levodopa should have been commenced in 2017, or that any clinician acted dishonestly.

Reliability boundary

What is secure, and what is not

The case does not ask counsel to reject the whole medical record. Strong parkinsonism, the abnormal later DaTSCAN, deliberate treatment deferral, the recorded rationale, the longitudinal foot history, later dopaminergic responsiveness, the Article 16/18 challenges and the PAR/SIMT outcome all remain material evidence.

The record does not, by repetition alone, establish that the feet were separate in 2017, that podiatry itself was neurological resolution, that Liam accepted that transition, that silence disproves the reported protest, or that a later institutional conclusion is necessarily an independent evidential origin.
