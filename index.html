The clinical case in one paragraph

Liam was 39 at first Neurology presentation, with possible symptoms extending roughly 13 years. Galway recognised severe young-onset Parkinsonism, entered the PRKN/PARK2 diagnostic architecture and adopted a deliberate levodopa-deferral strategy because of genuine long-term treatment-risk concerns. During the same admission the feet contained real structural pathology but the neurological relationship remained unresolved. A 6 July GP record says Podiatry would mark the case priority “as Parkinson's.” On 12 September 2017 Prof Daniel Healy wrote that Liam probably had PARK II and described relatively low-dose dopamine replacement as compatible with that condition while warning about dyskinesia from over-use. By 2018 Galway recorded suspected PARK2/PRKN while affirmatively continuing levodopa deferral. Later records increasingly treated foot phenomena as parkinsonian, OFF-related or dopamine-responsive. In February 2026 St Vincent's confirmed compound-heterozygous pathogenic Parkin variants causing Liam's Parkinsonism and dystonia.

The governance advance

The core question is now broader than whether individual treatment choices were clinically defensible. It asks who owned the unresolved cross-domain problem, what process was supposed to reopen the inherited model, and whether new evidence was allowed to change only the next action or also the historical meaning of the case.

Controlling governance question. Who owned the duty to change the meaning of the case, rather than merely change the patient's medication?

Current GDPR / data-governance significance

The contemporary record contains separate disputes about accuracy, completeness, rectification, restriction and continued use of challenged personal data. The strongest present repository contribution is procedural reconstruction: identifying the incoming challenge, the later institutional state and any missing adjudicative bridge between them.

A concrete example is the HSE / GUH Article 18 sequence: restriction was expressly invoked before the later SIMT process, while the presently assembled record leaves open what procedural treatment the request received before disputed material was subsequently used.

For PCRS, an accidental 9 December 2025 email beginning “Hi Miriam” attached a document explicitly labelled as a draft letter to issue to Liam. The repository preserves that as evidence of an internal drafting / approval pathway and a version-control question — not as proof of motive or predetermination.

What this case does not require

It does not require proof that every historical foot symptom was dystonia, that structural foot disease was unreal, that Podiatry was inherently inappropriate, that Healy ordered immediate levodopa, that later benefit proves historical negligence, or that a missing document proves no reconsideration occurred. Likewise, the GDPR layer does not treat complaints, acknowledgements or regulatory assessment as infringement findings.

Highest-value unresolved evidence

The immediate gaps remain: independent identification and uncontaminated recollection of the four medical students; records generated between the reported 10 July bedside protest and completion of the discharge account; native Complex Case Meeting material and senior treatment advice; complete Podiatry referral/pathway records; any explicit proposition-level reconsideration after later neurological foot evidence; complete PAR/SIMT source and deliberative material; and internal data-governance records showing how challenged information and restriction requests were adjudicated before subsequent use.
