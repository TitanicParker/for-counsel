Counsel entry point

The central question is whether an unresolved clinical problem was ever genuinely reopened.

In 2017 severe young-onset Parkinsonism was recognised, a dystonic-looking lower-limb abnormality was recorded, PRKN/PARK2 was expressly suspected, and levodopa was deliberately deferred. The foot/lower-limb relationship remained “Not clear.” The following eight years test what happened to that uncertainty.

The case in one paragraph

Galway correctly recognised severe young-onset Parkinsonism and entered the PRKN/PARK2 diagnostic architecture early. The same admission recorded a dystonic-looking lower-limb abnormality and genuine structural foot pathology, while leaving the relationship between the feet and neurological syndrome unresolved. A deliberate levodopa-deferral strategy became controlling. As PRKN suspicion strengthened and medication-sensitive, Parkinsonian and later OFF/dystonic phenomena appeared in the feet and lower limbs, the record does not presently show the original treatment and pathway decision being returned to an open state. In 2025 dopaminergic treatment was finally directed at the severe morning-foot problem and generated information that caused Liam to reopen the history. In 2026 a movement-disorders specialist confirmed that his Parkinsonism and dystonia were due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.

Controlling question. Given that PRKN was already suspected, a dystonic lower-limb phenotype was already recorded, and PRKN material supplied with Liam's discharge package described excellent levodopa responsiveness, what was supposed to cause the decision to withhold levodopa to be reconsidered — and did that trigger ever exist?

What this case does not require

It does not require proof that every historical foot symptom was dystonia. It does not require a claim that PRKN was missed. It does not require a claim that levodopa caution was irrational. The documentary record supports a mixed foot phenotype and a genuine rationale for treatment caution.

The issue is whether a legitimate early treatment strategy became sufficiently fixed that it also constrained learning, while the unresolved foot/lower-limb problem was routed elsewhere and later evidence accumulated without a visible formal reopening.

Pending evidence

The most immediate missing primary evidence is the 2017 Podiatry pathway. The site now defines the questions before those records arrive: what was referred, what information travelled, what Podiatry thought it was treating, what treatment was delivered, what changed, and whether anything returned to Neurology.
