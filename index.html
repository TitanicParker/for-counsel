Counsel entry point

The central question is what happened after PARK2 became the probable specialist explanation.

In 2017 severe young-onset symmetrical Parkinsonism and a dystonic-looking lower-limb abnormality were documented, levodopa was deliberately deferred, Podiatry was being prioritised “as Parkinson's,” and the foot relationship remained “Not clear.” In September 2017 Prof Daniel Healy personally reviewed Liam and wrote that he probably had PARK II.

The case in one paragraph

Liam was 39 at his first Neurology presentation, with a possible symptom history extending roughly 13 years. Galway recognised severe young-onset Parkinsonism, entered the PRKN/PARK2 diagnostic architecture, and adopted a deliberate levodopa-deferral strategy because of genuine long-term treatment-risk concerns. During the same admission the feet contained real structural pathology but the neurological relationship remained unresolved. A 6 July GP record says Podiatry would mark the case priority “as Parkinson's.” The 13 July DaTSCAN was abnormal “as was expected.” On 12 September Healy wrote that Liam probably had PARK II, recommended PARK II testing first, and described PARK II patients as managing for long periods on relatively low doses of dopamine replacement therapy while becoming dyskinetic if over-used. By 2018 Galway recorded PARK2 as what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause`, yet continued to withhold levodopa for quite some time. In March 2025 the retrospective position was stated as idiopathic Parkinson's disease. In February 2026 St Vincent's confirmed that Liam's Parkinsonism and dystonia were due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.

Controlling question. After the specialist expressly invited to advise on the hold-back strategy said Liam probably had PARK II and described cautious low-dose dopamine replacement as compatible with that disease, what process caused the existing treatment strategy and unresolved foot partition to return to an open state?

What this case does not require

It does not require proof that every historical foot symptom was dystonia. It does not require a claim that PRKN was missed. It does not require a claim that the original levodopa caution was irrational. The documentary record supports a mixed foot phenotype and a genuine rationale for caution.

The issue is whether that strategy remained proportionate and sufficiently revisitable as the evidential state moved from possible PRKN to probable PARK II and later to confirmed Parkin disease.

Pending evidence

The immediate high-value gaps are now the original 2017 Podiatry referral/pathway, records capable of identifying the four medical students Liam says were together on their first day on St Anthony's Ward on 10 July 2017, Complex Case Meeting provenance, and any document explaining the transition from probable/suspected PARK2 to the March 2025 asserted idiopathic Parkinson's disease position.
