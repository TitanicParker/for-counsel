Counsel-facing case orientation

The corrigibility and reliance case

This is not a hindsight misdiagnosis case. It is a case about a medical record that preserved uncertainty in words, while allowing a provisional diagnostic frame to acquire durable clinical, statutory, administrative, and interpretive authority.

Central strand

Documentation is not corrigibility.

The central proposition is that the patient was extensively recorded, but not allowed to become governing in the record. The problem is not that the clinicians failed to document uncertainty. The problem is that the documented uncertainty was deprived of operative force.

Central formulation: the institution accepted a foot-centred gait failure, recorded atypicality, genetic suspicion, dystonic-looking gait, preserved cognition, medication caution, and unresolved foot symptoms, but nevertheless allowed a Parkinsonian frame to become authoritative before preserving a meaningful route back to correction.

The case should therefore be read as a corrigibility and reliance case. A historically accurate record can become operationally misleading when later relied upon without qualification. The record did not merely remember the patient; it governed him.

Two supporting rails

The argument runs on two rails.

Rail one: sequence and entitlement to finality

The first rail is that finality arrived before inquiry had earned it. The GP referral presented an open, foot-centred gait failure. The hospital record then fixed Parkinson's Disease as the primary diagnosis while preserving unresolved features that made ordinary closure unsafe.

The legal point is not that Parkinsonism was implausible. The legal point is that plausibility was allowed to become authority too soon.

Rail two: continuing reliance and failure to reopen

The second rail is that later contradiction was absorbed without being allowed to reorganise the case. Procyclidine response, Sinemet response, repeated foot pain, dystonic phenomena, patient challenge, advanced-treatment questions, and later genetic confirmation all operated as reopening signals.

The system's characteristic move was not denial. It was containment: accept the fact, preserve the frame.

How the record gives counsel a clean path through the case

1. Entry: the case begins through the feet.

The GP referral records an unusual festinant and stiff gait, bilateral foot issues, callouses and corns, difficulty initiating movement, increased tone, brisk reflexes, dexterity decline, and work-threatening deterioration. It queries a parkinsonian or other underlying diagnosis; it does not assert one.

Open GP referral source record

2. Discharge: the same artefact records uncertainty and performs finality.

The discharge summary states "Primary Diagnosis: Parkinsons Disease" while also recording atypicality, possible autosomal-recessive or genetic Parkinsonism, dystonic-looking gait, minimal tremor, preserved cognition, unresolved leg and foot symptoms, and a strategic deferral of levodopa or dopamine agonists.

Open discharge summary source record

3. 2018: medication response becomes a reopening signal.

The 2018 note records that stopping procyclidine rapidly brought back the outside-left-foot phenomenon and that the patient could be "very much better" if levodopa or another dopamine agonist were given, while that route remained deferred.

Open 2018 neurology source record

4. 2021: the DBS narrowing exposes the elasticity of the frame.

The record shows Parkinson's broad enough to justify caution, dyskinesia risk, medication delay, and long-term management, but narrowed to tremor when DBS was raised. The point is not that DBS had to be indicated; the point is that it should not have been excluded by a tremor-only rule.

Open 2021 neurology source record

5. 2023: the misalignment becomes explicit.

The note records severe foot-centred difficulty, a patient concern that there was a misalignment between the clinician's Parkinson's frame and the patient's belief that the feet remained separate or central, and an acknowledgment that the very first symptoms were in the feet.

Open 2023 neurology source record

6. 2025: contradiction is accepted and contained.

The 2025 neurology note records that foot pain is central to the patient's concern, that Sinemet relieves the feet once it hits the system, and that increasing Sinemet would likely give some further relief but risks dyskinesia. The official rebuttal then accepts suffering, Sinemet benefit, and caution, while preserving the governing idiopathic Parkinson's frame.

Counsel issue statement

The question for legal review

The issue is whether a healthcare institution may record uncertainty, rely on the record as settled, and then deny the recorded uncertainty any practical route back to correction.

In a complex diagnostic case, especially where the patient is young, atypical, and the record itself preserves unresolved features, a healthcare institution must not allow a provisional diagnostic frame to acquire durable clinical or administrative authority without preserving a meaningful route for correction, qualification, or reopening.

That is the strand. The two rails are sequence and reliance: finality before completed inquiry; continuing use of the resulting record despite repeated reopening signals.
