## 1. The case is now best understood as a partition-and-reintegration problem

The presentation was mixed from the outset. There was genuine structural foot pathology: corns, calluses, pressure areas and longstanding podiatry exposure outside the HSE. There was also a severe young-onset movement disorder with marked parkinsonism, dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenology and a live PRKN/PARK2 branch.

The correct question is not whether the feet were wholly neurological or wholly podiatric. The neurological record itself did not resolve that binary. Its own position was that the relationship was **`Not clear`**.

That `Not clear` state had two possible futures:

1. **separation** — the painful feet remain functionally outside the movement disorder and are managed through podiatry/peripheral explanations; or
2. **integration** — later evidence increasingly demonstrates that at least part of the foot/lower-limb phenomenon belongs inside the neurological disorder.

The central longitudinal question is which future the accumulating evidence supported and whether the management architecture updated accordingly.

## 2. Early July 2017 chronology must remain tight

The present chronology is:

- **29 June 2017:** hospital referral enters with the feet/gait/neurological problem attached.
- **3 July:** Neurology examines a severe young-onset parkinsonian presentation with striking lower-limb features.
- **4 July:** the current reconstruction places the Complex Case Meeting / early no-levodopa treatment position here, subject to native meeting records.
- **6 July:** the GP record says he phoned Podiatry; the case would be marked priority **`as Parkinson's`** and should be called in September.
- **during the admission:** the discharge plan records that the hospital SHO had tried to arrange Podiatry.
- **9 July:** the LTI form is visibly dated and uses Parkinson's disease as the operative qualifying state.
- **later completed neurological record:** the relationship between the feet/leg symptoms and the neurological syndrome remains **`Not clear`** while Podiatry is still the practical foot pathway.

The important point is that Podiatry was operationalised very early, before the neurological relationship of the feet had been resolved. The 6 July GP action may reflect information transmitted out from the admission, but the precise communication route is not yet established. The incoming Podiatry records may resolve sender, date, triage and wording.

The discharge history also records longstanding prior podiatry involvement dating back to 2009/2010 outside the HSE. Therefore the 2017 disposition was not simply first referral of a new foot problem to a new service. It was a return toward a pathway already known to have existed for years.

## 3. Documentary uncertainty versus operative separation

The cleanest formulation is:

> **The documentary state remained `Not clear`; the operative state was separation.**

Neurology did not record that the feet had been proved separate, that neurological causation had been excluded, or that Podiatry had resolved the whole syndrome. Yet management divided the presentation into a movement-disorder stream and a foot-pain/podiatry stream.

That distinction is crucial. The case does not require proof that the initial uncertainty was negligent. The potential problem is that an unresolved partition became durable without a documented evidential event resolving it.

---

## 4. The senior treatment architecture may have affected the ability to resolve the partition

The early treatment position was to avoid levodopa/dopamine agonists for as long as possible. The contemporaneous record links treatment caution to Prof Counihan and Neurology colleagues, and Monaghan later described the position to Healy as **`Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?`**

That means the no-levodopa position was not merely an isolated bedside choice. It belonged to a senior/collective treatment architecture.

The important expert hypothesis is not that `Not clear` automatically means `not tested`. It is narrower:

> while the feet/movement relationship remained unresolved, the treatment policy also removed one potentially informative way of observing whether the disputed foot/lower-limb phenomena were dopaminergically responsive.

Whether a levodopa challenge should clinically have been used for that purpose in July 2017 is an expert question, not a documentary conclusion.

## 5. Procyclidine materially changed the evidential state in 2017

This chronology must not again be misstated.

The July 2017 discharge plan itself contemplated a later **test stopping** of Procyclidine/Kemadrin. The withdrawal therefore did not originate years later; it occurred in the same 2017 treatment architecture.

When Procyclidine was withdrawn later in 2017, the later neurological history records rapid return/change at the outside-left-foot/corn site with broader worsening. Reintroduction was followed by major physical improvement. The contemporaneous October 2017 email independently records the withdrawal/reintroduction sequence and dramatic overall improvement.

The strongest safe conclusion is:

> **at least part of the disputed foot/lower-limb phenomenon was neurologically pharmacologically modifiable in 2017.**

This did not prove that every corn, callus or pain episode was dystonia and did not prove levodopa would have produced the same response. But it made the original `Not clear` state less neutral. It was an integration signal.

The key question becomes:

> **Why did the challenge/rechallenge-type neurological medication signal not visibly trigger reconsideration of the original foot/podiatry partition?**

## 6. Later evidence increasingly loads the integration side of the fork

The longitudinal record does not show a clean later proof that the feet were peripheral and separate. Instead it repeatedly adds neurological integration signals:

- later foot/lower-limb medication sensitivity;
- a lower-limb `throw` characterised as Parkinsonian;
- recurrent toe curling and cramping;
- 2023 OFF/dystonia reasoning;
- explicit `misalignment` over the explanatory frame;
- earliest symptoms again recorded as being in the feet;
- 2025 severe morning foot pain improving once Parkinson medication reached the system;
- more Sinemet expected to provide additional relief;
- Sinemet CR deliberately introduced for the severe morning foot problem;
- profound relief subsequently reported.

Meanwhile, the peripheral explanation in 2025 remains conditional: language such as **`if there is a degree of neuropathy here...`** is still hypothesis language, not an established resolution of the original uncertainty.

Thus the longitudinal asymmetry is:

> **the separation hypothesis remains uncertain while integration evidence accumulates.**

## 7. PRKN expertise sharpens, but does not decide, the question

PRKN/PARK2 was live from the original neurological reasoning. Counihan and Healy had prior specific expertise in the Parkin phenotype, including young onset, lower-limb manifestations and dystonia. Healy later personally reviewed Liam and wrote that he **probably had PARK II**.

This does not prove that either specialist classified Liam's painful feet as dystonia. It does make the lower-limb phenotype obviously relevant to the diagnostic branch being considered.

The expert question is therefore not `why did they miss PRKN?` They did not. It is:

> **how was the lower-limb/foot phenotype incorporated into management once PRKN/PARK2 and dystonic phenomenology were already live, and why did the foot stream remain operationally separated while the causal relationship stayed unresolved?**

## 8. The strongest present mechanism

The present case mechanism is:

**mixed foot + movement presentation**
→ **severe young-onset parkinsonism recognised**
→ **PRKN/PARK2 and dystonic lower-limb features live**
→ **early senior/collective no-levodopa architecture**
→ **feet relationship recorded `Not clear`**
→ **Podiatry operationalised anyway, including very early GP priority routing `as Parkinson's`**
→ **the two streams become functionally separated**
→ **2017 Procyclidine withdrawal/reintroduction supplies neurological-modifiability evidence capable of reintegration**
→ **no visible formal reopening of the partition**
→ **later Parkinsonian/OFF/dystonic and medication-sensitive foot evidence accumulates**
→ **2025 dopaminergic timing directly relieves the severe foot problem while neuropathy remains conditional**
→ **2026 PRKN genetics supplies later etiological coherence for Parkinsonism and dystonia**.

The case is therefore increasingly about **failure to update an initially uncertain partition**, not simply about a wrong diagnosis at the outset.
