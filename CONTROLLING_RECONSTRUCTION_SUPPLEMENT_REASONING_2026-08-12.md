# Controlling Reconstruction Supplement — Reasoning Architecture — 12 August 2026

## Why this supplement exists

The controlling reconstruction remains intact, but the latest source comparison adds a more precise mechanism.

The case is no longer best described only as `early levodopa deferral + unresolved feet + no visible reopening`.

The new mechanism is:

> **A highly analytical clinician confronted an unusually open young-onset Parkinsonian presentation. While the aetiology, dystonic phenotype and foot/lower-limb relationship remained fluid, several practical decisions became operationally settled: levodopa would be deferred, Podiatry would handle a foot pathway, Parkinson's would be used administratively. When later information strengthened PARK2, the record shows that information being acknowledged but does not show the practical architecture being returned to the same level of open reasoning that had characterised the original diagnostic work-up.**

## The important asymmetry

The July 2017 document proves that Monaghan was capable of detailed clinical reasoning.

He reasons extensively about:

- chronology;
- Parkinsonian phenomenology;
- dystonia versus foot drop;
- structural feet;
- toxic/metabolic alternatives;
- MRI;
- monogenic Parkinsonism;
- PRKN/PARK2;
- test limitations;
- treatment risks;
- his own subspecialty limitations.

The question is therefore not whether he reasoned.

It is:

> **Why does visible reasoning become comparatively thin at several points where a practical answer has already been supplied?**

Those points are now:

1. **Feet:** `Not clear` → Podiatry, without a visible division-of-responsibility or reopening bridge.
2. **Levodopa:** substantial original risk rationale → no visible prospective rule saying what would reopen the decision.
3. **Healy:** probable PARK2 + low-dose dopamine-replacement framing → no visible comparison with Galway's prolonged hold-back policy.
4. **2018 genetics:** `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause` + `Obviously I'd be particularly interested to know` → confirmation declared not to change management, without an equally detailed patient-specific derivation of that proposition.
5. **2025 aetiology:** extensive treatment reasoning → `idiopathic Parkinson's disease` asserted without a visible bridge from the previously strengthened PARK2 branch.

## The strongest current structural formulation

> **Reasoned caution is not the same thing as reasoned reappraisal.**

The original levodopa caution had reasons. The forensic question is whether the system had a mechanism requiring those reasons to be re-weighed when:

- the patient remained severely impaired;
- the unresolved lower-limb/foot phenotype persisted;
- Healy upgraded PARK2 from possibility to probability;
- Healy described cautious dopamine replacement rather than simple prolonged non-use;
- PARK2 became what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause`;
- later treatment-sensitive and OFF/dystonic phenomena appeared;
- the disease was later called idiopathic without an identified etiological bridge.

## Diagnostic-management decoupling

The April 2018 note now deserves special status because it contains both sides of the polarity in one place.

Etiological side:

- `Obviously I'd be particularly interested to know`;
- PARK2/PRKN is what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause`;
- testing now is reasonable;
- Healy would support it.

Management side:

- it `doesn't particularly change current management`;
- there is `no great loss in 'not knowing'`;
- levodopa could make Liam `obviously ... very much better`;
- but `we do not want to do that - at least for quite some time`.

The resulting hypothesis is:

> **The genetic answer may have been treated as management-neutral because the management plan had already been fixed, rather than the stronger genetic information being allowed to test whether the management plan should itself change.**

That is a hypothesis for expert testing, not a concluded standard-of-care breach.

## Foot/Podiatry consequence

The foot question should now be framed as an ownership problem as well as a causation problem.

Not:

> `Why was he sent to Podiatry?`

but:

> **When a mixed structural/neurological foot-lower-limb presentation was expressly `Not clear`, what component did Podiatry own, what component did Neurology retain, and what event was supposed to return the unresolved relationship to Neurology?**

The 6 July GP record that the case was prioritised `as Parkinson's` makes this more, not less, important.
