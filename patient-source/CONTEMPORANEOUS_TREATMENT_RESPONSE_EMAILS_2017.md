# Contemporaneous treatment-response emails — October 2017

## Purpose

Preserve contemporaneous patient-origin evidence about the effect of the neurological treatment regime before formal propositions are frozen.

This file distinguishes:

- what Liam wrote contemporaneously in October 2017;
- what Liam now recalls about the same episode;
- what can safely be inferred;
- what still requires the clinical record or expert interpretation.

---

## 1. 4 October 2017 email — direct contemporaneous account during Kemadrin/procyclidine withdrawal

**Source:** Gmail, sent by Liam Moloney to Celia Moloney, subject `Here`, 4 October 2017.

The email records Monaghan's staged Kemadrin/procyclidine titration and a planned treatment break to assess its effects. Liam wrote that within days of beginning half a tablet there had been significant improvement and described the period as `living in a miracle, literally`.

He listed concrete functional changes:

- `My walk is back together.`
- `My writing is good.`
- `I can stand up straight.`
- `My legs are loose and limber.`
- he could stand while the bus was moving;
- lift his bicycle without falling over it;
- jump, run, hop and skip;
- and perform ordinary dexterity tasks that had previously been difficult.

Four days into the Kemadrin break, he wrote:

> `I am feeling the tension building up, my feet are restless and my walk is falling apart....but my eyesight is back to normal.`

The same email also contains Liam's then-current historical reconstruction of the longstanding foot problem, including recurrent stinging on the outside of the left foot and years of severe burning foot pain. Importantly, it distinguishes foot pain from leg tension in his own experience while also trying to understand their place within the wider Parkinsonian illness.

### Source-status

This is contemporaneous patient evidence, written while the withdrawal experiment was actually occurring. It directly supports that:

- Kemadrin/procyclidine had produced marked global physical improvement;
- a planned withdrawal was being used to assess the medication's effects;
- during withdrawal, tension increased, the feet became restless and gait deteriorated;
- visual side effects were simultaneously improving off the medication.

It does **not**, by itself, prove the mechanism of the foot symptoms or that levodopa would have produced the same effect.

## 2. 24 October 2017 HR email — independent contemporaneous functional context

**Source:** Gmail, sent by Liam Moloney to Pauline McDonagh and Deborah Walsh, subject `Re: Medical Assessment`, 24 October 2017.

Liam told HR that he had had to stop the medication about three or four weeks earlier because it was affecting his eyesight; the Parkinson's nurse took him off it for about 12 days and reintroduced it at a lower dosage. He reported that the lower dose did not cover all symptoms and that the nurse had just advised increasing the dose again while monitoring vision.

The email records:

> `apart from the vision, I am physically very much improved, you wouldn't believe there was ever much wrong with me.`

and:

> `The joy of being able to move about normally and function unchallenged has been the equivalent of living in a miracle.`

At the same time Liam recorded that he had a Podiatry appointment on Thursday, Physiotherapy on Friday and the Parkinson's nurse on Monday.

### Source-status

This email corroborates, to a workplace recipient and for a practical occupational purpose, that the neurological medication regime had produced a profound physical improvement by October 2017, while visual toxicity was forcing withdrawal/reduction/re-escalation decisions.

It is particularly useful because it was not written for litigation, complaint or retrospective reconstruction.

## 3. Present patient recollection — phenomenological clarification

Liam now clarifies that the improvement during procyclidine titration felt like a profound release of longstanding tension that had become almost a constant background `sound` in his bodily experience. He recalls being able to `walk with power` and experiencing the improvement as almost perfect apart from residual issues such as hand cramps and visual disturbance.

He further recalls that when procyclidine was withdrawn, the **first pain to return** was the focal pain on the outside of the left foot, the same pain he dates back to 2004, and that this focal pain persisted until March 2025.

### Source-status

The present recollection adds phenomenological detail not fully expressed in the surviving October emails. In particular, the claim that the outside-left-foot pain was the *first* symptom to return should remain tagged as present patient recollection unless and until a contemporaneous clinical note, email or witness source independently confirms that precise sequence.

The contemporaneous October emails do strongly support the broader state: dramatic improvement on Kemadrin/procyclidine, planned withdrawal to test effect, deterioration during withdrawal, foot restlessness during withdrawal, and re-escalation because the lower dose failed to control all symptoms.

---

## 4. Clinical significance to be tested, not assumed

The clean documentary point is not that procyclidine proved the feet were dystonia and not that procyclidine and levodopa are interchangeable.

It is narrower:

> **Neurology's own treatment regime materially altered the patient's physical state, and the contemporaneous withdrawal experiment produced deterioration that included the feet.**

Once that information existed, the neurological significance of treatment effects in the feet became a question requiring clinical interpretation.

The later record should therefore be examined for whether Neurology documented what the medication-foot interaction meant, whether it changed the earlier Podiatry/Neurology allocation, and whether it entered the criteria governing prolonged dopaminergic deferral.
