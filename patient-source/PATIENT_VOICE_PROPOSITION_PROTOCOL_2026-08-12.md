# Patient Voice Proposition Protocol — 12 August 2026

## Purpose

Before propositions are finalised, the patient must have a structured route to speak to the same facts and decision points without contaminating the documentary record or converting recollection into documentary proof.

This protocol creates a parallel layer:

**DOCUMENTARY FACT → PATIENT RECOLLECTION / EXPERIENCE → CORROBORATION LEADS → PROPOSITION → EXPERT QUESTION**

The patient voice is not a substitute for records. It is a separate evidential source capable of:

- clarifying chronology;
- identifying omitted conversations;
- distinguishing what was explained from what was merely documented;
- supplying symptom meaning that may be lost in clinical shorthand;
- identifying witnesses and locations;
- identifying practical consequences not captured by formal notes;
- challenging apparent documentary coherence;
- showing what the patient understood the decision to mean at the time.

## Rules

1. Preserve exact patient wording where possible.
2. Date every recollection and distinguish contemporaneous memory from later reconstruction.
3. Do not overwrite earlier patient statements; add clarifications as new entries.
4. Do not feed the patient the desired proposition before taking an account.
5. Record confidence level: exact / strong recollection / approximate / reconstruction / inference.
6. Separate what the patient personally saw or heard from what he later learned from records.
7. Preserve contradictions rather than harmonising them.
8. For potential independent witnesses, first contact should be neutral and open-ended.
9. Patient-source evidence can support chronology and meaning but must not be promoted to documentary fact without corroboration.
10. Every controlling proposition should include a `Patient voice` field: supports / qualifies / contradicts / no evidence / not applicable.

## Proposition worksheet

For each proposed proposition record:

- **Proposition ID**
- **Documentary fact(s)**
- **Primary source(s)**
- **Patient voice** — what Liam personally recalls or experienced
- **Patient-source status** — contemporaneous / recalled / reconstructed / mixed
- **Potential corroboration** — witness, email, family member, work record, pharmacy, GP, placement roster, etc.
- **Conflict or asymmetry** — where patient experience differs from the written record
- **What not to infer**
- **Expert significance**
- **Counsel significance**

40. **Counterfactual patient preference:** Without asking what treatment should medically have been given, what did Liam want clinicians to investigate, explain or reconsider at each stage?

## Why this matters

The proposition phase should not present the patient merely as the object of clinical decisions. His account can reveal the difference between:

- a plan being documented and a plan being understood;
- a risk being explained and a decision being consented to;
- Podiatry being adjunctive and Podiatry being experienced as hand-off;
- diagnostic uncertainty existing on paper and uncertainty being communicated;
- a treatment policy being reviewable in theory and being experienced as fixed in practice.

The strongest propositions will therefore be triangulated rather than document-only:

> **recorded state + patient-experienced state + corroborating source + expert interpretation**.
