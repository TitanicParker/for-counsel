## 1. Evidential labels

- **PRIMARY FACT** — directly recorded event, observation, test, treatment or contemporaneous communication.
- **PATIENT ACCOUNT** — what Liam reported; evidence of the account and, where contemporaneous, his information/knowledge state.
- **CLINICAL OPINION** — clinician interpretation or diagnosis.
- **INFERENCE** — conclusion drawn by comparing sources.
- **ADVERSE / QUALIFYING** — evidence that narrows or may weaken the strongest case.
- **EXPERT QUESTION** — requires independent clinical evidence.
- **UNKNOWN / SOURCE REQUIRED** — not established from the present corpus.

## 2. Reliability boundary

**INFERENCE.** The 2017 record remains strong evidence of severe parkinsonism, observed examination findings, investigations, treatment choices, and the fact of podiatry referral.

It is **not safely self-sufficient proof** that the unresolved relationship between the bilateral foot/lower-limb problem and the neurological syndrome was actually resolved as separate before or through the podiatry pathway.

This is proposition-specific impeachment. It is **not** a claim that the whole medical record is unreliable.

The strongest present issue is therefore not **“Parkinson's was wrong.”** Genuine parkinsonism is strongly supported. The issue is the classification, treatment and reconsideration history of the disabling foot/lower-limb phenomenon.

## 3. The 2017 hinge

**PRIMARY FACT:** the 29 June GP referral places bilateral foot problems, corns/calluses and poor foot condition inside a deteriorating neurological presentation with abnormal gait, movement-initiation difficulty, increased tone, brisk reflexes, toe clawing, impaired dexterity and threatened work function.

**PRIMARY FACT / CLINICAL OPINION:** Neurology records severe bilateral parkinsonism and a striking gait/lower-limb phenotype, including a footdrop-like appearance described as somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture and expressly **not foot drop**.

**PRIMARY FACT / CLINICAL OPINION:** the relationship between leg/foot symptoms, burning, corns/calluses and the neurological syndrome is recorded as potentially separate / possibly neuropathic and ultimately **“Not clear.”**

**PRIMARY FACT:** outpatient Chiropody/Podiatry nevertheless becomes an operative pathway.

**UNKNOWN / SOURCE REQUIRED:** no presently located source demonstrates the reasoning bridge by which that recorded uncertainty became established neurological separation/resolution. “No bridge located” does not mean “no reasoning existed.”

## 4. Early neurological pharmacological modifiability

**PRIMARY FACT:** Kemadrin/Procyclidine was prescribed as neurological symptomatic treatment in 2017, separately from the podiatry referral.

**PRIMARY LATER CLINICAL RECORD OF PATIENT-REPORTED RESPONSE:** the 24 April 2018 Neurology record preserves the Sept/Oct 2017 two-week withdrawal episode. When Procyclidine was stopped because of visual blurring, the first feature said to change rapidly was the return/change at the outside-left-foot/corn site; symptoms worsened somewhat and Liam was more “shifty” and “unrelaxed.”

**PRIMARY CONTEMPORANEOUS PATIENT COMMUNICATION:** the 24 October 2017 email independently records the short medication withdrawal/reintroduction, incomplete symptom control at the lower dose, podiatry appointment, substantial physical improvement and the description of normal movement/function as **“living in a miracle.”**

**INFERENCE:** at least part of the foot phenomenon was neurologically pharmacologically modifiable in the early treatment period.

This does **not** establish that the corn itself was neurological, that every foot symptom was dystonia, or that podiatry was inappropriate.

## 5. Longitudinal foot/lower-limb record

The foot phenomenon remains inside Neurology after the podiatry disposition:

- **2018:** foot cramps continue; toe supports help; withdrawal response is recorded; levodopa/agonist could make Liam substantially better but is deliberately deferred.
- **2020:** exquisite foot sensitivity, excessive movement/urge involving the feet, and a left lower-limb “throw” characterised as parkinsonian.
- **2021:** ordinary Sinemet introduced and escalated while dyskinesia/impulse-control concerns remain active.
- **2022:** morning feet take about 20–25 minutes to “warm up”; soreness around corns/calluses persists.
- **2023:** severe bilateral foot pain; possible OFF-related/dystonic material; dopaminergic adjustment; explicit “misalignment” over whether the feet are separate from Parkinson's; earliest symptoms recorded as being in the feet.
- **2024:** foot pain and toe curling persist; pregabalin/Lyrica provides substantial pain benefit, supporting possible mixed mechanisms.

## 6. Information supplied to the patient: 2017 and 2021

**PRIMARY FACT:** the 2017 “good understanding” wording belongs to the explanation of levodopa/dopamine-agonist deferral. It is not proof that Liam agreed the feet were neurologically separate or that podiatry had resolved them.

**PRIMARY FACT:** the October 2021 Neurology letter says:

> “the DBS is only ever for the tremor element. It will not help with anything else.”

The same letter says Liam had been fully counselled and would receive a copy at home.

Project medical-authority work has established that this was materially overbroad as a **general proposition** by 2021. A narrower patient-specific judgment may have been intended, but the record does not say that.

**EXPERT QUESTION:** nothing here establishes that DBS should have been performed. The expert must address phenotype, treatment state, target-specific expected benefit, candidacy and whether the categorical counselling had clinical significance.

## 7. March 2025 treatment-response sequence

**PRIMARY FACT / RECORDED PATIENT ACCOUNT:** severe morning foot pain is the dominant issue. The record says the first roughly 150 steps are “torture” and that once Parkinson's medication reaches his system the feet feel better from a pain perspective.

**CLINICAL OPINION / PRIMARY FACT OF TREATMENT:** the clinician considers that more Sinemet would probably provide further relief but weighs dyskinesia risk. Possible overnight undertreatment is considered and Sinemet CR is started at night specifically in response to the severe morning problem.

**PRIMARY LATER CLINICIAN EVIDENCE:** Monaghan's complaint response says his 10 March prescription was **“also to try to help the morning experience including of pain”** and describes the subsequent response as **“profound relief currently.”**

This was therefore a therapeutically targeted change in dopaminergic coverage, not merely an incidental observation after treatment prescribed for something unrelated.

**ADVERSE / QUALIFYING:** the same records preserve mixed/peripheral pain possibilities, pregabalin benefit and substantial dyskinesia risk.

## 9. 2026 PRKN / Parkin result

**PRIMARY SPECIALIST SOURCE:** the St Vincent's Neurology letter, dictated 3 February 2026 and e-signed/approved 4 February 2026 by Dr Sean O'Riordan, states:

> “They confirm that he has two pathogenic variants in the Parkin gene as predicted. His Parkinsonism and dystonia are due to compound heterozygous mutations in the Parkin gene.”

**CLINICAL OPINION / SPECIALIST DIAGNOSIS:** Parkinsonism and dystonia share the PRKN/Parkin genetic cause.

**IMPORTANT CHRONOLOGICAL CONTEXT:** the July 2025 Monaghan supporting bundle reproduces the 2017 working record already raising PRKN/PARK2 as a possible genetic explanation. That earlier suspicion was not a diagnosis; the 2026 letter is the later diagnostic source.

**INFERENCE:** genetics supplies later coherent etiological context for earlier dystonic phenomenology and treatment-response evidence. It does not prove every historical foot symptom was dystonia.

## 11. What the evidence supports — and does not

### Presently supported

- genuine severe parkinsonism in 2017;
- contemporaneous dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenology;
- expressly unresolved foot/neurology relationship;
- actual podiatry disposition;
- early Procyclidine-related neurological pharmacological modifiability of at least part of the foot phenomenon;
- persistent/recurrent foot involvement inside Neurology;
- later OFF/dystonia/misalignment evidence;
- deliberate 2025 dopaminergic treatment of the severe foot problem with substantial relief;
- 2026 specialist diagnosis of PRKN-caused Parkinsonism and dystonia.

### Not established merely by those facts

- that every historical foot symptom had one dystonic cause;
- that every corn/callus was neurological;
- that podiatry was inappropriate or negligent;
- that levodopa necessarily should have started in 2017;
- that DBS should have been performed in 2021;
- that later response automatically proves an earlier breach;
- that later genetics can be back-projected into knowledge available in 2017;
- dishonesty, concealment, fraud, conspiracy or bad faith.

## 12. Strongest benign / adverse explanations

A competent review must preserve:

- real severe Parkinsonism and abnormal DaTSCAN;
- possible mixed mechanical/corn/callus, neuropathic, pain-modulation and dystonic components;
- reasonable adjunctive podiatry remains possible;
- clinically defensible caution with dopaminergic escalation in a very young patient remains possible, particularly given later dyskinesia/impulse-control experience;
- Procyclidine benefit was not uniformly clear in later 2018–2019 withdrawal/restart history;
- pregabalin/Lyrica materially helped pain;
- the 2021 DBS wording may have been shorthand for a sound patient-specific judgment not fully expressed;
- the unseen PAR may contain genuine independent revalidation;
- the bedside protest remains a patient account unless independently corroborated.

## 14. Expert questions

The documentary record does not itself decide breach or causation. The movement-disorders expert should address:

1. what components of the longstanding foot/lower-limb syndrome were dystonic/OFF, pain modulation, abnormal mechanical loading, neuropathic/local podiatric pathology, or mixed;
2. what medical significance the 2017 Procyclidine withdrawal signal carried;
3. when, if ever, later response/phenomenology should have caused the unresolved 2017 foot classification to be reconsidered;
4. what the 2021 phenotype meant for DBS counselling, distinct from whether DBS was indicated;
5. what the 2025 targeted Sinemet CR response medically signifies;
6. what the later PRKN result legitimately illuminates retrospectively without hindsight distortion.
