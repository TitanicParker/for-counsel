## Controlling evidential boundary

The accessible record does not safely prove by itself that the unresolved relationship between the feet/lower limbs and the neurological syndrome was clinically resolved as separate before or through the podiatry disposition. The safe formulation is:

> **The accessible record does not demonstrate the reasoning bridge.**

That is not a finding that no reasoning existed, that podiatry was inappropriate, that all foot symptoms had one neurological cause, or that negligence is established.

## Patient protest

The patient reports that, before discharge, he expressly protested that the central foot problem had not been dealt with and used the NCT/bald-tyres analogy. The accessible discharge representation does not record that protest. The protest is patient evidence of an event and must not be reduced to a mere hypothetical because it lacks independent contemporaneous corroboration. At the same time, the present repository does not yet contain an independently corroborating trainee, witness or ward record.

The correct evidential treatment is therefore two-track:

1. preserve and particularise the patient account as evidence, including date, circumstances, words, addressee and later reporting provenance; and
2. separately record the present corroboration gap and the Article 15 search/disclosure history.

Neither the discharge silence nor the corroboration gap is proof that the event did not occur.

The supporting [`Restored Foot-Protest Dependency Audit`](analysis/supporting/RESTORED_FOOT_PROTEST_DEPENDENCY_AUDIT_2026-08-13.md) tests the stipulated premise against the controlled corpus. It does not elevate the premise to independently corroborated institutional fact and does not determine clinical appropriateness, negligence, state of mind, concealment or fraud. Its proper analytical conclusion is that omission materially affects apparent acceptance, sufficiency, absence of dissent and completeness—not the existence of the clinical findings or the possible appropriateness of Podiatry.
