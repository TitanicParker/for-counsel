# Patient Voice Reconstruction Rule

**Function:** back-end evidential rule for reconstruction where the controlling clinical record is materially impeached at a disputed hinge.

## The larger point

Once a record has been materially impeached as a complete, self-sufficient account of a particular transition, the patient's voice cannot continue to be treated merely as secondary material requiring validation from that same record.

At that hinge, the record has lost the privilege of being treated as the complete container of what happened.

The reconstruction must therefore use all available evidential origins capable of recovering the missing state, including the patient's contemporaneous recollection, later consistent accounts, independent witnesses, surrounding communications, chronology, treatment-response evidence, teaching records, referral records and other external traces.

The patient is not made infallible by impeachment of the record. But neither may the record's silence be used as the default measure of the patient's credibility where the very completeness of that record is the proposition under examination.

## Governing rule

> **Where the record is materially impeached at the hinge, the patient's account becomes a primary reconstructive source for the missing clinical and interpersonal state, to be tested against independent evidence rather than subordinated to the impeached record by default.**

This rule is especially important where the disputed matter is not merely a symptom but a disagreement, protest, refusal, question, explanation, uncertainty, or challenge to the treatment/pathway decision. Those events are inherently relational. If they are omitted from the institutional document, they may leave little trace except in the memory of the patient and other people who were present.

## Why this matters in the 2017 hinge

The completed 2017 discharge architecture preserves:

- the operative Parkinson's diagnosis;
- treatment rationale;
- senior/collective language;
- LTI and work consequences;
- Podiatry disposition;
- later DaTSCAN confirmation;
- `good understanding` language.

Liam reports that, at the transition point, he protested and challenged what was being done with the feet. Four medical students were present.

If that account is correct, the omitted protest is not an incidental gap. It is the counter-state to the apparent consensus preserved by the completed record.

The correct evidential response is therefore not:

`the protest is absent from the notes, so its existence is doubtful.`

It is:

`the hinge record is already materially impeached as a complete account, so reconstruct the disputed interaction from every independent source capable of bearing on it.`

That requires active attention to the patient's voice.

## The patient as an evidential sensor

A clinical record is an institutional observation of a patient. It is not the patient himself.

The patient may possess information unavailable to the finished document about:

- what he was trying to communicate;
- what he understood the clinicians to be saying;
- what he disputed;
- what symptoms he was distinguishing from one another;
- whether he regarded a proposed pathway as answering the problem he had presented with;
- what questions he asked;
- whether treatment or non-treatment altered specific symptoms;
- what changed his understanding later.

Where a record is reliable and complete on a point, those accounts can be compared normally.

Where the record is impeached at that point, it is circular to use its silence as the principal reason to downgrade the only source that describes the omitted event.

## The reverse question

The repository should repeatedly ask:

> **If the institutional record is incomplete at this hinge, what does the patient's account tell us that the institutional record may have failed to preserve, and what independent evidence can test that account?**

This is different from advocacy.

It does not require accepting every recollection without testing.

It requires giving the patient's account the correct evidential role once the document that would ordinarily be treated as controlling is itself under reconstruction.

## Application to the protest

The reported bedside protest should therefore be handled as follows:

1. Record Liam's account accurately and without dilution.
2. Do not paraphrase it into a weaker generic statement such as `patient later disagreed` if the actual account is of a contemporaneous protest at the hinge.
3. Preserve the claimed circumstances: bedside setting, timing relative to the pathway decision, subject matter, and presence of four students.
4. Seek independent corroboration from the student witness class and any teaching/ward records.
5. Seek contemporaneous or near-contemporaneous communications that may reflect the disagreement.
6. Compare later behaviour and later documented disputes for consistency, while not treating later consistency as equivalent to independent eyewitness proof.
7. Re-run the discharge-completeness, patient-understanding and governance propositions if independent corroboration is obtained.

## Broader consequence

The larger evidential lesson is not limited to one protest.

Once the record is impeached at the hinge, the reconstruction should recover the patient's voice across the entire sequence wherever the institutional account may have flattened a contested state into apparent agreement.

The repository should distinguish:

- what the institution recorded the patient as understanding;
- what the patient says he actually understood;
- what he says he objected to;
- what he was trying to get clinicians to investigate;
- what later records show about whether that concern persisted;
- what independent evidence supports or contradicts him.

The goal is not to privilege the patient's account over all other evidence.

The goal is to stop privileging an impeached institutional account over the patient's account simply because it is institutional.

## Counsel-facing formulation

> **Once the governing record is shown to be incomplete at the very transition under dispute, the patient's voice is no longer merely evidence against the record. It is evidence needed to reconstruct what the record failed to preserve.**

That is the evidential posture the second-generation counsel site should carry forward.
