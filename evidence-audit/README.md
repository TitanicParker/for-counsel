## Classification rules

**PRIMARY** means the item is itself a contemporaneous or later source event: clinical record, imaging report, patient communication, clinician communication, administrative communication, governance decision, or regulatory communication. A later primary communication may prove only what was said or done at that later date; it is not necessarily independent proof of the historical clinical facts it discusses.

**DERIVATIVE** means the document proves a later institutional or summary act but materially depends on upstream sources for the historical proposition. Examples include a SIMT outcome letter or later QPS reliance statement.

**ANALYTICAL** means project/model/narrative material. It may locate or quote a source but is not counted as an evidential origin.

**Independent evidential origin** means a source capable of adding evidence not merely copied, summarised or repeated from the same upstream source. Multiple documents do not necessarily mean multiple independent origins.

## Repository-search limitation

The GitHub repository is not code-search indexed. The source-record corpus and commit history were therefore inspected directly through repository file/commit reads. A source is marked **not located** rather than **non-existent** unless the evidence supports the stronger statement.

## Source-location rule

Where a primary document exists in the project archive/Gmail but has not been mirrored into `for-counsel`, the inventory says so explicitly. No synthetic repository path is invented.

## Controlling safeguard

The core documentary issue is reproducible from the source material only if the reader can distinguish:

- what happened as an event;
- what a source actually records;
- what is inferred from the sources;
- what is disputed;
- what is unknown;
- what requires expert evidence; and
- what requires disclosure.
