# Authority, Expertise, and Recursive Failure

**Function:** reconstruct who held which kind of authority around the 2017 young-onset Parkinsonism / PRKN / levodopa decision, and identify where responsibility for reopening the decision may have failed.

## Core proposition

The relevant structure is not a simple chain of command.

It is a distribution of different kinds of authority:

- **Thomas Monaghan:** treating consultant / bedside ownership / evolving patient information;
- **Timothy Counihan:** senior local neurological authority and movement-disorder expertise within Galway;
- **Daniel Healy:** external specialist authority with substantial Parkinson genetics / neurogenetics expertise.

The working concern is not that one of these men was necessarily "the boss" in every sense.

It is that the patient-specific information, local treatment authority, and external specialist expertise were distributed across different people.

That creates a specific governance risk:

> **an early decision can acquire authority at one level, while later information capable of changing it remains at another level unless someone deliberately sends the case back upstream.**

This file calls that problem **failure of recursive authority**.

---

## 1. Thomas Monaghan — bedside ownership and information control

Monaghan was the treating neurologist who:

- took the history;
- examined Liam;
- recorded the dystonic-looking lower-limb posture;
- documented severe young-onset Parkinsonism;
- developed the recessive / PRKN differential;
- recorded the unresolved foot relationship as `Not clear`;
- authored the evolving discharge architecture;
- sought external advice from Dan Healy;
- later recorded that he was not a movement-disorders neurologist.

This made Monaghan the principal holder of the evolving patient-specific information state.

His authority was therefore **informational and clinical at the bedside**.

The key question for him is not simply what he believed.

It is:

> **What material did he learn after the initial senior levodopa advice, and what did he do with information that might have changed the assumptions on which that advice rested?**

## 2. Timothy Counihan — local senior authority

Later Monaghan material places Counihan in the 2017 Complex Case Meeting context and attributes to him advice concerning commencement / escalation of levodopa in a younger patient.

The exact formal administrative title and departmental chain in July 2017 should not be overstated unless a primary institutional source fixes it.

For the case mechanism, however, the functional point is enough:

- Counihan's view carried sufficient senior local weight to shape the treatment ceiling;
- Monaghan later describes the levodopa position as a collective one involving `Tim`;
- the treatment plan records senior/collegial advice and an intention to avoid levodopa as long as possible.

This made Counihan a holder of **local authoritative treatment judgment**.

The critical question is informational:

> **What exact phenotype had been presented to Counihan when his advice was given?**

In particular:

- did he know about the bilateral foot pain?
- did he know about burning?
- did he know about corns/calluses only, or the broader lower-limb problem?
- did he know the gait appeared dystonic?
- did he know the foot relationship remained unresolved?
- did he know the later PRKN reasoning?
- was he updated after Monaghan's understanding developed?

The difference between `Counihan advised against levodopa after considering the full phenotype` and `Counihan advised against levodopa on an earlier, thinner information state` is fundamental.

## 3. Daniel Healy — external specialist authority

Monaghan's contemporaneous correspondence to Healy asks for advice on:

- appropriate genetic testing;
- possible specialist review in Dublin;
- and expressly whether Galway should continue to hold levodopa back.

The key sentence is:

> **`Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?`**

That wording establishes an important functional relationship.

Healy was not being treated merely as a laboratory contact.

He was being invited to advise on a live management question that Galway had already framed provisionally.

The phrase `unless you'd advise otherwise` shows that the local position remained theoretically defeasible by external specialist advice.

By April 2018, Monaghan's note records PRKN/PARK2 as what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause` and states that Healy would be content with the PRKN / panel testing route.

This makes Healy an important holder of **external epistemic authority** on the young-onset / genetic Parkinsonism question.

The controlling source gap is his native reply.

Questions:

- Did he answer the levodopa question directly?
- Did he recommend holding back?
- Did he recommend a trial?
- Did he focus only on genetics?
- What clinical material did he receive?
- Did he receive the video?
- Did the video show the dystonic-looking gait/lower limb?
- Did he receive the painful-foot history?
- Did he know the foot relationship was `Not clear`?
- Did he recommend seeing Liam in Dublin?
- What was the timing of his advice relative to discharge, LTI, DaTSCAN and finalisation of the record?

## 4. The authority problem is distributed, not vertical

It is misleading to ask simply:

`Who was the boss?`

The more useful map is:

### Monaghan
Held the **patient**.

### Counihan
Held substantial **local senior authority** over the treatment frame.

### Healy
Held substantial **external specialist authority** over the unusual young-onset / genetic Parkinsonism question.

These forms of authority did not sit in one person.

The potential system failure is therefore one of **integration**.

The person who knew the evolving patient best was not necessarily the person whose earlier judgment had the greatest local authority.

The person whose local judgment carried authority may not have possessed the later detailed foot phenotype.

The person with specialist external expertise depended on whatever information Galway transmitted.

---

## 5. Failure of recursive authority

A safe clinical authority structure must be recursive.

That means:

**information flows upward → advice is given → new information emerges → the decision is returned upward if the assumptions materially change.**

The present reconstruction raises the possibility that the process instead operated as:

**information flows upward → senior advice hardens → new information emerges → treating clinician works within existing advice → external opinion is sought inside the existing frame → decision remains governing.**

That is the lock.

The problem is not hierarchy itself.

The problem is an authoritative answer outliving the information state on which it was based.

## 6. The most important actor-specific questions

### Monaghan

> **After the initial senior decision, what new information did you learn about the painful feet, dystonic-looking lower limb and PRKN possibility, and why did that information not trigger an explicit reconsideration of the levodopa position?**

### Counihan

> **What exactly did you know when you advised on levodopa, and were you later updated when the patient's phenotype became more complex?**

### Healy

> **What did Galway send you, what did you conclude about PRKN, and what did you answer to the explicit question asking whether levodopa should continue to be withheld?**

These three questions reconstruct the lock more directly than any abstract argument about hierarchy.

---

## 7. Why PRKN sharpens rather than solves the issue

The case is not that PRKN was absent from the clinicians' thinking.

It was present.

The stronger question is:

> **Why did increasing confidence in a PRKN/Parkin explanation not itself reopen the original treatment decision or the unresolved lower-limb/foot relationship?**

By 2018 the record simultaneously contains:

- PRKN/PARK2 as the suspected cause;
- Healy's apparent agreement with that causal suspicion;
- the recorded proposition that Liam could be `very much better` with levodopa or another dopamine agonist;
- continued deliberate withholding of those drugs;
- early medication-sensitive phenomena involving the foot/lower-limb system.

This is not a simple knowledge deficit.

It is a question about whether **knowledge accumulated inside a treatment architecture that had already become difficult to alter**.

---

## 8. The missing Healy reply becomes a first-order source

Because Monaghan expressly asked Healy about levodopa, the native Healy reply is not a peripheral correspondence gap.

It is potentially the only surviving source capable of showing whether the external specialist escape route was actually exercised.

If the reply says `continue to hold back`, that is new independent support for the treatment strategy and changes the provenance chain.

If it says `try levodopa`, `consider a trial`, or recommends specialist reassessment, that would materially alter the case.

If it does not address levodopa at all, then the question becomes whether Galway treated silence on the management question as acquiescence.

If no reply exists, that also matters because the record later invokes `we/Prof Healy suspect` PRKN without the full external reasoning presently available.

**Acquisition priority:** native Healy correspondence should be treated as one of the highest-value missing primary sources in the entire repository.

---

## 9. The Complex Case Meeting becomes a decision provenance source

The Complex Case Meeting record should be sought not merely to prove attendance.

It should answer:

- who presented Liam;
- what history was presented;
- whether foot pain was mentioned;
- whether the dystonic-looking gait was described or shown;
- whether PRKN was discussed at that stage;
- who expressed the levodopa concern;
- whether any alternative view was recorded;
- whether the decision was provisional;
- whether any trigger for reconsideration was set;
- whether the case returned to the meeting after further findings.

The crucial provenance question is:

> **Was the no-levodopa position a one-time recommendation attached to an early snapshot, or a repeatedly reconsidered decision attached to the developing phenotype?**

---

## 10. Counsel-facing formulation

> **The 2017 treatment architecture distributed responsibility across three different forms of authority: Monaghan held the evolving bedside facts; Counihan's view carried senior local treatment weight; Healy was consulted as an external specialist on the unusual young-onset / genetic Parkinsonism problem and was expressly invited to advise whether levodopa should remain withheld. The central governance question is whether later information capable of changing the original decision ever travelled back through that authority structure. The present record shows upward consultation. It does not yet demonstrate recursive reconsideration after the phenotype developed.**

---

## Bottom line

The relevant failure mechanism is not simply `deference to hierarchy`.

It is more precise:

> **Authority attached to an early clinical answer. Patient-specific knowledge continued to develop elsewhere in the system. The unresolved question is whether the changed information state was ever returned to the people whose authority had made the original answer controlling.**

That is the authority-side expression of the first lock.
