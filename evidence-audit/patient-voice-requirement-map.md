# Patient Voice Requirement Map — Restrained Use

**Purpose:** identify the limited points at which Liam's first-person evidence is necessary to complete the reconstruction, without allowing patient voice to overwhelm a case that is already substantially clear from the documentary record.

## Governing rule

Patient voice is not a parallel narrative layer to be inserted everywhere.

It is required only where Liam is the best or only available source for a material point that the institutional record cannot reliably establish by itself.

The default remains:

> **Use the documentary record where the documentary record can answer the question. Use Liam's voice where the missing fact is inherently first-person: what happened to him, what was said directly to him, what he said in response, what sequence he personally experienced, or what treatment he actually received.**

The aim is evidential completion, not narrative saturation.

---

# Priority patient-voice areas

## PV-01 — The order of events on Tuesday 4 July 2017

**Why Liam's voice is required:**
The current institutional record does not fully preserve the sequence as Liam experienced it.

**Patient evidence should address only:**
- when he was taken from the ward/bed;
- where he understood he was being taken;
- whether he was physically present at the Complex Case Meeting;
- what examination or questioning occurred there;
- when he returned or was subsequently spoken to;
- when he understood the levodopa decision had been made.

**Do not ask Liam to reconstruct:**
- what was discussed in his absence;
- what each clinician privately believed;
- formal meeting governance he could not personally observe.

**Corroboration routes:**
Complex Case Meeting records, ward records, teaching records, clinician accounts, student witnesses.

---

## PV-02 — What the neurologist directly said to Liam

**Why Liam's voice is required:**
A clinician's note can record the clinician's intended explanation, but it cannot by itself establish the words communicated to Liam or how the exchange actually occurred.

**Patient evidence should preserve:**
- what Monaghan directly told him about the diagnosis;
- what he was told about why levodopa would not be used;
- any reference he recalls to senior authority / `the boss`;
- what he was told about proving or confirming the diagnosis;
- what he was told about the feet and their relationship to the neurological problem;
- what he was told would happen next.

**Discipline:**
Record this as Liam's recollection of the communication. Do not silently convert remembered substance into verbatim quotation unless he is certain of the wording and it is clearly labelled as recollection.

---

## PV-03 — The bedside protest

**Why Liam's voice is required:**
The completed record does not preserve the reported protest, while preserving apparent understanding and collective authority. Liam is the primary direct source for his own side of that encounter.

**Patient evidence should address:**
- where the protest happened;
- who was present;
- what prompted it;
- what Liam was objecting to;
- what he said about the feet;
- whether he was objecting to Podiatry itself, to treating Podiatry as the explanation, or to separation of the feet from the neurological inquiry;
- what the clinician said in response;
- what happened immediately afterwards.

**Independent route:**
four medical students reported present.

**Do not overextend:**
The protest should remain one important hinge event, not become a substitute for the documentary case as a whole.

---

## PV-04 — What Liam meant by the foot/tension distinction

**Why Liam's voice is required:**
The 2017 record says Liam had distinguished foot pain from leg tension, but the record may broaden that distinction into a question of separate causes.

**Patient evidence should answer narrowly:**
- what he meant when he said the foot pain and leg tension were `not the same problem`;
- whether he meant they felt different;
- whether they changed under different conditions;
- whether he believed they had entirely separate causes;
- what part of the lower-limb/foot problem he still believed was unexplained.

**Purpose:**
Clarify meaning, not retrospectively give Liam a 2025/2026 neurological theory in 2017.

---

## PV-05 — The Podiatry treatment Liam actually received

**Why Liam's voice is required:**
The original 2017 Podiatry referral and complete pathway are still missing. Liam is a direct witness to the treatment he actually underwent, even though institutional Podiatry records remain the preferred source for dates, diagnoses, procedures and clinician rationale.

**Patient evidence should address:**
- where Podiatry treatment occurred;
- approximately when it began;
- what physical treatment was performed;
- what areas of the feet were treated;
- what explanation he was given for the treatment;
- what he understood Podiatry was being asked to treat;
- whether treatment helped, did nothing, or worsened particular symptoms;
- whether he reported worsening or lack of benefit;
- whether Podiatry communicated anything back to Neurology or the GP, so far as he personally knows;
- how and when the Podiatry pathway ended.

**Boundary:**
Liam's account can establish what he personally experienced and was told. It should not replace the missing Podiatry record for the clinician's own findings, reasoning, coding, referral content or communications.

# Secondary patient voice — use only if a documentary ambiguity actually requires it

Patient evidence may be useful, but should not automatically be solicited, on:

- what specific symptom changed during the Procyclidine withdrawal/restart;
- how pain, tension, clawing and movement difficulty differed phenomenologically;
- what he understood by later `misalignment` discussions;
- what precisely changed in his understanding after the March 2025 Sinemet CR response.

These should be obtained only where they resolve a live proposition or source ambiguity.

The existing clinical record is already substantial on these periods and should remain primary where it adequately answers the question.

---

# Areas where patient voice is not required unless a specific dispute arises

Do not solicit Liam's recollection merely to duplicate facts better established by records, including:

- DaTSCAN result;
- MRI/ultrasound results;
- exact dates/signatures contained in metadata;
- contents of Monaghan-to-Healy correspondence;
- formal PRKN genetic result;
- formal QPS, Medical Council, PAR/SIMT or GDPR communications already preserved;
- clinician professional roles established by external/primary sources;
- formal medication prescription dates where the prescription/clinical record exists.

Repeated patient retelling of documentary facts would add volume without adding evidential value and could blur source genealogy.

---

# Proportionality rule

Before requesting patient evidence, ask:

1. Is there a material ambiguity?
2. Can an existing documentary source answer it better?
3. Is Liam a direct witness to the missing point?
4. Will his evidence change or clarify a proposition?
5. Is there an independent corroboration or contradiction route?

If the answer to 1, 3 and 4 is not yes, patient evidence should normally not be added.

---

# Present priority order

The immediate patient-evidence work should therefore be limited to four tightly connected subjects:

1. **the order of events around the Tuesday morning decision;**
2. **what the neurologist directly said to Liam;**
3. **the bedside protest and the four students;**
4. **the Podiatry treatment Liam actually received and what happened to the feet through that pathway.**

The foot-pain / leg-tension meaning should be captured within that work because it may explain what the protest was actually about.

Nothing else should be expanded merely because patient voice is now recognised as evidence.

---

# Governing formulation

> **The case should remain document-led. Liam's voice should enter where the missing evidence is inherently his: the sequence he experienced, the words spoken directly to him, his contemporaneous protest, the meaning of his own distinctions, and the treatment he actually underwent. Beyond those points, the record should be allowed to speak for itself unless a specific ambiguity requires first-person evidence.**
