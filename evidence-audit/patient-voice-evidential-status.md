# Patient Voice as Evidence After Hinge Impeachment

**Status:** governing evidential principle for the counsel-site rewrite  
**Branch:** `agent/counsel-site-rotation`

## Core proposition

Once the 2017 hinge record is no longer treated as a complete, self-sufficient account of the transition from the unresolved bilateral foot/lower-limb problem into the operative treatment and Podiatry pathways, the patient's contemporaneous and retrospective account cannot be treated as a decorative qualification to the documentary record.

It is evidence.

That does not mean every recollection is automatically established as fact. It means the patient's voice must enter the evidential architecture at the level appropriate to its provenance: **first-person witness evidence**.

The correct discipline is therefore not:

> record says X; patient says Y; absent documentary corroboration, X governs.

It is:

> record says X; patient gives direct evidence Y; the record's completeness at the disputed hinge is itself under examination; therefore X and Y must both be preserved, sourced, tested and weighted.

## Why the impeachment changes the evidential posture

Documentary silence only has evidential force to the extent the document can reasonably be expected to preserve the event in question.

Here, the disputed issue is the completeness of the very record that governs the transition under examination. The record preserves diagnosis, treatment rationale, collective authority, patient-facing explanation and `good understanding`, but it does not presently demonstrate a complete reasoning bridge for the unresolved foot/lower-limb problem, and it does not preserve Liam's reported protest.

The record therefore cannot prove its own completeness merely by being silent about a proposition that would challenge the apparent settlement it records.

This does not reverse the burden and make the patient's account automatically true. It changes the forensic task from **record versus allegation** to **competing evidential origins requiring reconstruction**.

## Direct patient evidence must be atomised, not paraphrased away

Where Liam gives a first-person account of an event he personally experienced, preserve its components separately.

For example, his account of Tuesday, 4 July 2017 contains distinct propositions:

1. he was taken from his bed at approximately 08:00 to a Complex Case Meeting;
2. he was physically present at that meeting;
3. he was examined there, including for tremor;
4. by approximately 10:00 he was informed that a decision had been made not to use levodopa;
5. he recalls being told, in substance, that idiopathic Parkinson's disease could generally only be proved by brain examination after death;
6. he recalls being told that there was no other way to prove it at that point because `the boss` had said levodopa could not be used;
7. the wording is his recollection of what was said and must not be silently converted into a verbatim clinician quotation unless independently sourced.

Each proposition has its own evidential value and its own possible corroboration routes.

## Evidential labels

Patient-origin evidence should be labelled precisely rather than downgraded generically.

Use categories such as:

- **FIRST-PERSON EVENT EVIDENCE** — the patient describes an event he personally experienced;
- **FIRST-PERSON COMMUNICATION EVIDENCE** — the patient reports what he was told or what he said;
- **FIRST-PERSON TEMPORAL EVIDENCE** — the patient recalls timing or sequence;
- **PATIENT INTERPRETATION** — the patient states what he understood an event to mean;
- **PATIENT RECONSTRUCTION** — later synthesis of multiple events rather than a single remembered occurrence.

Do not use `PATIENT ACCOUNT` as a rhetorical synonym for `weak evidence`.

The provenance label identifies the source. Weight is a separate question.

## Corroboration is about weight, not admissibility into the architecture

Independent corroboration can strengthen patient evidence, but the absence of corroboration does not erase it from the case model.

Potential corroboration may come from:

- contemporaneous clinical notes;
- medication chronology;
- letters and emails;
- administrative records;
- later clinician admissions or descriptions;
- witnesses;
- teaching/placement records;
- timing relationships that independently fit the account;
- subsequent events that are consistent with, but do not by themselves prove, the earlier account.

Contrary evidence must be preserved with equal discipline.

The aim is not to promote the patient's account above the documents. It is to stop documentary status from being mistaken for automatic evidential superiority where the document's completeness is itself disputed.

## The Complex Case Meeting sequence

Liam's direct evidence now materially changes the status of the 4 July sequence.

The repository should no longer describe the existence of the Tuesday morning Complex Case Meeting and his attendance there as merely a reconstruction inferred from later Monaghan material.

The proper formulation is:

> **Liam gives direct first-person evidence that at approximately 08:00 on Tuesday, 4 July 2017 he was taken from his bed to a Complex Case Meeting, was examined there including for tremor, and by approximately 10:00 was informed that a decision had been made not to use levodopa. He recalls being given an explanation involving the difficulty of proving idiopathic Parkinson's disease during life and being told, in substance, that `the boss` had said levodopa could not be used.**

The documentary task is now to test that account against independent sources, not to downgrade the event to inference simply because the current clinical record does not preserve it fully.

## The protest sequence

The same principle applies to the reported bedside protest.

Liam reports that he disputed the emerging separation of the feet from the neurological problem and that four medical students were present.

That account should remain clearly labelled as his evidence unless and until independently corroborated. But because the disputed hinge record is already impeached as a complete account, its silence is not strong affirmative evidence that the protest did not occur.

The correct response is active witness reconstruction, governed by `evidence-audit/protest-witness-investigation.md`.

## Relationship to the fact register

The existing F0001–F0283 freeze applies to analysis generated from the already-ingested source corpus. It does not prevent genuinely new source evidence from reopening the register.

A newly supplied first-person witness statement containing materially new event propositions is a new source event.

The appropriate process is:

1. preserve the statement as a dated source record;
2. atomise distinct propositions;
3. assign patient-origin provenance and substantive-status labels;
4. identify corroboration and contradiction routes;
5. append new fact IDs rather than silently altering F0001–F0283;
6. rerun affected propositions and narrative pages.

## Governing writing rule

The second-generation counsel site should not present the patient as an object observed entirely through institutional documents.

Where Liam is the direct witness to a disputed encounter, communication or sequence, the prose should say so plainly.

The reader should be able to distinguish:

- what the record says happened;
- what Liam says happened;
- what independent evidence corroborates either account;
- what remains unresolved.

That is a stronger evidential architecture than allowing one source class to erase another.

## The larger point

The impeachment of the hinge record has a consequence beyond one protest or one meeting.

It changes the epistemic posture of the whole investigation.

The patient is not merely the subject of the medical record. He is also a source.

Where the institutional record is incomplete at the exact point under dispute, his first-person evidence must genuinely enter the reconstruction and be tested on its merits.

The repository should therefore treat the patient's voice neither as sovereign nor as subordinate.

It should treat it as evidence.
