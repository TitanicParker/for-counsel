GDPR / documentary case

Incorrigibility in the record, unfolding

A counsel-facing map of what happened when the data subject moved from challenging the governing clinical frame to using the GDPR's formal mechanisms for access, rectification, completion, restriction and regulatory review.

Central proposition

The GDPR episode is the corrigibility theory tested in real time.

The earlier record raises a clinical and governance question: could a diagnostic frame remain open to correction when unresolved facts kept returning? The 2025-2026 GDPR sequence supplies a more objective test. The data subject invoked the actual statutory mechanisms designed to make a record contestable: Article 15 to see the data, Article 16 to rectify or complete it, and Article 18 to restrict reliance while accuracy remained contested.

Core formulation. The issue is not whether historical medical opinion must be erased. The issue is whether a historically faithful record remained practically corrigible once later information materially qualified it, and whether the statutory mechanisms for access, completion, annotation and restriction were effective when invoked.

The working theory is interpretive, not doctrinal. “Incorrigibility of the record” is not presented as a freestanding legal cause of action. The enforceable questions arise under the GDPR itself: accuracy and accountability, transparent and timely rights administration, access, rectification/completion, restriction, notification and effective supervisory remedy.

The environment before GDPR

The frame was already operative before the first Article 16 request.

25 March 2025 - Monaghan

Dr Thomas Monaghan's QPS response is deeply apologetic about Liam Moloney's experience and the pain and discomfort he had endured. It accepts that Sinemet helped the experience of pain, while preserving idiopathic Parkinson's disease as the governing explanatory frame and defending cautious levodopa escalation.

15 April 2025 - Nolan

Aoife Nolan's closure letter records the complaint as one concerning bias and diagnostic anchoring, notes that Sinemet CR had been effective for foot pain, and records Moloney's allegation that the earlier failure to consider it led to prolonged and preventable pain. The complaint is classified as “solely relating to clinical judgment” and routed to the neurologist for response.

30 May 2025 - SIMT pathway

After continuing dissatisfaction, Nolan moves the matter into the Incident Management Framework. A Preliminary Assessment Report is to be completed and presented to the Serious Incident Management Team. By the time GDPR restriction is later invoked, SIMT is therefore a known and imminent processing venue.

The GDPR rights were not invoked against an empty file. They were invoked against an existing institutional account that had already absorbed complaint, apology, treatment response and continuing disagreement without surrendering the governing frame.

current procedural posturetwo DPC matters

The statutory interruption points

Three rights, one practical question: could the record be challenged before it governed again?

Article 15 - see

The access right should make the informational environment visible. The 7 July request asked specifically about a remembered teaching encounter and the data that might situate it institutionally. GUH's formal reply leaves questions about refusal versus non-holding, search scope and the 2015/2017 date.

Article 16 - correct or complete

The 12 June request did not seek to rewrite history wholesale. It identified omission, misleading implication and incompleteness, and sought rectification plus annotation. The later decision's “erasure” framing therefore matters because completion and supplementary qualification are distinct from deletion.

Article 18 - pause reliance

The restriction right was invoked before SIMT and named SIMT prospectively. The critical audit question is what effect GUH actually gave that request between 4 August and the point at which it says accuracy was determined.

The structural point. If Article 15 could not reliably expose the relevant data, Article 16 did not produce a timely and clearly framed correction/completion decision, and Article 18 did not visibly interrupt downstream processing, then the GDPR episode supplies evidence about practical corrigibility without requiring a regulator to decide the underlying medical diagnosis.

Seven live documentary questions

What Fleming can ask without accepting the larger theory.

1. Which June Article 16 request did the 14 July acknowledgment concern?

Two separate Article 16 requests existed and both had been reminded. Madden's singular acknowledgment does not identify either by date or subject.

Regulatory proof required: request register, internal reference, allocation/routing trail, and status of the other June request.

2. What Article 12(3) treatment did each June request receive?

The substantive decisions are dated 6 September. No extension notice has been located in the primary package. Complexity could justify an extension; it does not itself prove that the extension mechanism was used.

Regulatory proof required: any timely extension notice, reason, date, duration and evidence of communication.

3. Did the 6 September decision determine rectification/completion, or answer an erasure problem?

The 12 June request expressly sought rectification and annotation. The decision opens by calling it “erasure” and reasons substantially from preservation of historic opinion. The question is not whether the historic diagnosis may remain; it is whether completion/annotation was separately assessed.

Regulatory proof required: contemporaneous Article 16 analysis addressing inaccuracy, incompleteness and supplementary annotation.

4. What effect was actually given to Article 18 before SIMT?

The 4 August request is express and names SIMT. SIMT then meets on 27 August before the 6 September Article 16 determination.

Regulatory proof required: restriction log, flags/controls, data sources, processing audit, decision date, lawful basis for any continued processing, and lifting notice if restriction was ended.

5. Was the Article 15 outcome a refusal, a non-holding result, or both?

The 5 August letter uses refusal language and non-holding language in the same determination. The existence of undisclosed records is not assumed.

Regulatory proof required: systems, custodians and repositories searched; category-by-category search outcome; any actual withholding ground.

6. Was 2015 a typo, or the period actually searched?

The request concerns July 2017. The response says 10-11 July 2015. A simple search record and attachment list can resolve the issue.

Regulatory proof required: search instruction, patient/admission identifiers used, and dates of the records supplied.

7. Was the January 2026 Article 16/18 exercise ever determined by the controller?

The renewed request is sent directly to the DPO and expressly labels the rights invoked. QPS later responds on governance; Moloney then expressly says he is still awaiting the GDPR determination. No separate DPO determination is located in the present package.

Regulatory proof required: acknowledgment, extension, refusal, Article 12(5) position, adopted QPS determination, or other DPO response.

Two controllers / one event

The University strand does not merge the controllers; it exposes the boundary.

GUH / HSE - C-25-7-2074

The hospital was asked in July 2025 for personal data relating to the bedside teaching interaction. It gave a category-by-category response that combines refusal/non-holding language and denies holding several teaching-related categories.

University of Galway - DPC0826812075

The University was separately asked on 24 November 2025 about University-governed teaching, supervision and academic processing arising from the same event. On the present record, no substantive controller determination followed before the separate complaint was made.

Joined-up but legally separate. The regulator can preserve controller separation while still asking a factual accountability question: how can the data subject obtain a reliable account of the personal data arising from one identifiable teaching event when the hospital gives a negative/non-holding position and the University does not substantively determine its own request?

Evidential discipline

What this page does not conceal.

  • The 14 July Madden acknowledgment exists; later correspondence inaccurately says there had been no acknowledgment.
  • The first 4 August Article 18 email says Article 16 remains outstanding; the follow-up says the June requests are formally closed.
  • The 2015 reference in the Article 15 decision may be a clerical error rather than proof that the wrong year was searched.
  • “Erasure” may be a drafting/template error; its significance depends on whether the underlying analysis actually considered annotation/completion.
  • “Refuse” may be template language; its significance depends on what searches occurred and whether any responsive data were actually withheld.
  • The Article 18 emails prove invocation, not by themselves a breach. Operational compliance or non-compliance requires GUH's internal evidence.
  • Monaghan's apology is not an admission of negligence, and Nolan's “prolonged and preventable pain” formulation records Moloney's allegation rather than an independent QPS finding.
  • The presence of medical students, as remembered by Moloney, does not prove that a particular roster, logbook or teaching note exists.
  • The University and GUH are treated as separate controllers unless evidence establishes a different controller relationship for a particular processing operation.
The strongest test remains: even if a benign explanation is accepted for an individual irregularity, what documentary or procedural question remains?

Regulatory significance

From one complaint to a possible rights-management question.

The present DPC complaint does not need the Commission to adopt “incorrigibility” as a theory. It can proceed document by document. But if controller-side evidence confirms repeated failures across Article 12 timing, Article 15 search/communication, Article 16 completion/annotation, Article 18 operational restriction and the January re-engagement, the cumulative question changes.

Potential regulatory question. Is this a series of isolated mistakes affecting one complainant, or does the sequence indicate a defect in how Galway University Hospitals recognises, routes, distinguishes, times, determines and operationalises data-subject rights across DPO/SARS, Medical Records and QPS functions?

The DPC publicly states that an inquiry is generally considered where an alleged breach appears extremely serious and/or indicative of a systemic failing. This page does not assert that threshold has been met. It identifies the evidence that could rationally cause a regulator to test it.

Documentary synthesis

What the GDPR chapter adds to the counsel case.

The counsel theory began with a record that documented uncertainty yet allowed one frame to acquire durable authority. The GDPR chapter adds a later and more testable proposition: when statutory correction mechanisms were invoked, did they have enough practical force to alter, qualify, expose or temporarily restrain that authority?

Existing frame → complaint → clinical-judgment classification → SIMT pathway → Article 16 correction/annotation → Article 15 access → Article 18 restriction naming SIMT → SIMT “no deficit” output → Article 16 refusal framed partly through erasure/retention → continued governance reliance → renewed Article 16/18 → governance answer without located DPO determination → regulator assessment.

The record does not need to prove institutional motive to establish a corrigibility problem. It is enough to ask whether the systems that should permit correction actually worked. If access cannot expose the data, completion cannot qualify the record, restriction cannot interrupt reliance, and later requests cannot obtain a determination, then the legal machinery itself becomes evidence about the record's practical resistance to correction.

Counsel caution

Use the structure, not the overclaim.

This page is strongest if it remains a map of evidence and legal questions. It should not be pleaded as proof that GUH deliberately concealed records, that SIMT was predetermined, that the University and hospital colluded, or that every disputed clinical proposition was factually false. The documentary force is more exact: the record acquired reliance; formal mechanisms for correcting, exposing and restricting that record were invoked; and the effectiveness of those mechanisms is now itself capable of objective regulatory examination.

Usable formulation. The GDPR chapter asks whether the institution's record was meaningfully corrigible in practice. The answer does not turn on hindsight. It turns on what happened when the data subject invoked the legal mechanisms specifically designed to make personal data accessible, correctable, completable and, while accuracy was contested, restrictable.
