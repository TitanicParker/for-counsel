Case theory

The corrigibility and reliance case

The case is not that medicine failed because it began in uncertainty. It is that medicine recorded uncertainty and then failed to let uncertainty retain operative authority.

Core proposition. A provisional diagnostic frame became clinically and administratively powerful before the originating problem had been resolved, and later contradiction was absorbed without reopening the governing record.

One-paragraph case theory

This is a corrigibility and reliance case arising from a young-onset movement-disorder presentation. The allegation is not that later PRKN confirmation should be imposed retrospectively on 2017 clinicians. The allegation is that the hospital accepted a foot-centred gait failure, recorded atypicality, uncertainty, possible genetic disease, medication caution, dystonic features, preserved cognition, and unresolved foot symptoms, but nevertheless allowed a Parkinsonian frame to acquire clinical, statutory, administrative, and interpretive authority before the initiating problem had been resolved. Once that frame hardened, later patient testimony, medication response, specialist reassessment, data requests, advanced-treatment questions, and complaint processes were absorbed without being allowed to reopen the governing record. The legal issue is whether the institution breached duties of care, disclosure, record accuracy, procedural fairness, and professional governance by failing to preserve a meaningful route for correction.

What the case is not

  • It is not a claim that 2026 genetic confirmation should be projected backwards as knowledge available in 2017.
  • It is not a claim that Parkinsonism was invented or wholly absent.
  • It is not a claim that every act of care was negligent or that clinicians acted maliciously.
  • It is not a claim that levodopa, DBS, genetics, or any one intervention would necessarily have solved the case.
  • It is not a demand that clinical records be rewritten as though earlier uncertainty did not exist.

What the case is

  • A claim that the record itself contained enough uncertainty to require continuing diagnostic openness.
  • A claim that plausibility was converted into authority before the foot-centred initiating problem had been resolved.
  • A claim that patient testimony was recorded but not allowed to govern the hierarchy of explanation.
  • A claim that treatment caution was not matched by caution about finality.
  • A claim that later contradiction generated reopening signals which were absorbed rather than allowed to alter the governing frame.
  • A claim that a historically faithful record became operationally misleading when later relied upon without qualification.

The documentary sequence

The case begins with an open referral, not a closed diagnosis. The patient entered the system through abnormal gait, bilateral foot problems, increased tone, stiffness, impaired movement initiation, dexterity decline, and work-threatening deterioration. The GP referral did not declare a final diagnosis; it asked whether the presentation was Parkinsonian or another underlying diagnosis.

The hospital then identified a Parkinsonian frame. That was not baseless. The record contained real parkinsonian signs and a DAT scan supported a presynaptic dopaminergic disorder. But the same record also preserved the very features that made ordinary closure unsafe: young onset, atypicality, possible genetic/autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism, dystonic-looking gait, preserved cognition, treatment caution, and unresolved foot symptoms.

The problem is not that uncertainty was absent. The problem is that uncertainty was documented, then deprived of governing force. It became text within a record whose practical effect was increasingly final.

The governing contradiction

Plausibility is not finality. Documentation is not corrigibility. Caution is not neutral if it delays clarification while allowing the diagnostic frame to govern.

The record shows care, detail, explanation, follow-up, and patient-facing correspondence. That matters. It prevents the case from being caricatured as abandonment. But the legal and ethical issue is more exacting: whether care continued inside an explanatory structure that had become insufficiently revisable.

The patient was not simply ignored. He was quoted, paraphrased, counselled, copied into letters, and treated as intelligent. But being recorded is not the same as having authority. The feet were repeatedly narratable, but not governing. Dystonia was mentioned, but not allowed to organise the frame. Genetic Parkinsonism was entertained, but not allowed to prevent idiopathic Parkinson’s from becoming the practical structure. Later medication response was acknowledged, but absorbed.

Reopening signals

The later record supplied repeated reopening signals. These included the return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon after procyclidine withdrawal; the expectation that levodopa or dopamine agonist therapy could substantially improve function; the eventual introduction of Sinemet; the severe recurrence of bilateral foot pain; the patient’s express concern about misalignment between the Parkinsonian frame and the foot-centred reality; the later recognition that Parkinson’s medication relieved the feet; and genetic confirmation of PRKN/PARK2-related monogenic Parkinsonism with dystonia.

The point is not that any single signal proves liability. The point is cumulative. A safe system must have a threshold at which accumulated mismatch becomes a reason to reopen, qualify, refer, or correct.

The DBS inversion

The DBS issue is not that DBS was necessarily indicated. The point is that the patient raised an advanced-treatment pathway in a record already rich with young onset, dystonic features, medication sensitivity, and preserved cognition. The response reduced DBS to a tremor-only intervention. That is structurally revealing. Parkinson’s disease was broad enough to justify medication caution, dyskinesia risk, impulse-control concern, pain magnification, certification, and long-term management, but narrow enough to exclude DBS when a possible advanced-therapy pathway arose.

The diagnosis expanded when it justified control and contracted when it might have opened referral. That inversion supports the broader corrigibility case: corrective pathways were repeatedly framed in ways that preserved the existing structure.

Legal principle proposed

Proposed principle. In a complex diagnostic case, especially where the patient is young, the presentation is atypical, and the record itself preserves unresolved features, a healthcare institution must not allow a provisional diagnostic frame to acquire durable clinical or administrative authority without preserving a meaningful route for later correction, qualification, or reopening.

Why this matters

The case matters because modern healthcare can appear attentive while remaining structurally resistant to correction. A patient may be fully documented and still epistemically overruled. A record may be detailed and still misleading in hierarchy. A diagnosis may be plausible and still over-govern. A complaint response may accept suffering, apology, and treatment benefit while preserving the very frame whose authority is disputed.

The deepest question is whether medicine has an obligation not merely to diagnose, but to remain corrigible when the patient’s unresolved reality keeps returning to the record.
