Documentary case

Documented on the face of the record

The case is not built against the record. It is built from the record.

Documentary proposition. The central problem does not require a strained or sympathetic reading. In chronological order, the documents show an open foot-centred referral, premature administrative closure, recorded atypicality, genetic suspicion, scope limitation, deferred clarification, medication-sensitive feet, DBS narrowing, explicit misalignment, crisis-level foot pain, and final containment of contradiction.

The point is facial, not hidden

The strongest version of this case does not depend on motive, speculation, or projecting later genetic knowledge backwards. It depends on what the documents do in sequence. Counsel can read the record chronologically and see the structure: uncertainty is recorded, finality is performed, contradiction returns, and the frame remains intact.

These are not fifteen accusations. They are fifteen documentary locks. Each locks a different door against the institution’s easiest defence.

1. The case begins open, foot-centred, and work-threatening

The GP referral does not say, “this is Parkinson’s disease.” It describes an unusual festinant and stiff gait, bilateral foot issues, callouses and corns, trouble initiating movement, increased tone, brisk reflexes, dexterity decline, and deteriorating work function. It asks whether this is “parkinsonian or other underlying diagnosis.” That means the hospital accepted responsibility for an open diagnostic problem, not a closed Parkinson’s label.

Usable sentence: The institution did not inherit a Parkinson’s diagnosis; it accepted an unresolved foot-centred gait failure requiring explanation.

Open GP referral source record

2. The discharge summary performs finality while recording the facts that made finality unsafe

The discharge document states “Primary Diagnosis: Parkinsons Disease,” but the same artefact records a very young patient, atypical presentation, minimal tremor, dystonic-looking gait, possible autosomal-recessive/genetic Parkinsonism, unresolved leg/foot symptoms, and strategic caution around levodopa/dopamine agonists.

Usable sentence: The discharge summary is not merely inconsistent; it is the moment uncertainty is preserved as text while finality is allowed to govern.

Open discharge summary source record

3. The LTI point acts out premature closure in administrative form

The discharge record says the LTI forms had been signed and posted, while the DAT scan confirmation is later appended at transcription finalisation. Separately, PCRS later treated 09/07/2017 as the LTI scheme date even though the clinical addendum/DAT scan confirmation was 13/07/2017.

Usable sentence: The premature closure is not an inference; the record acts it out — statutory certification precedes the appended diagnostic confirmation.

Open discharge summary source record · Open 2017 neurology source record

4. The DAT scan request itself documents the case as atypical and genetically suspicious

The DAT scan is often used as if it simply closes the case. But the clinical indication for the DAT scan records a very young patient, bilateral Parkinsonism, difficulty lateralising, gross bradykinesia, an “in many respects atypical” presentation, symptoms for up to 13 years, a walk that is almost dystonically unusual, and suspected autosomal-recessive “idiopathic” Parkinson’s.

Usable sentence: The scan did not erase uncertainty; it confirmed dopaminergic abnormality inside a referral that was already atypical, dystonic, young-onset, and genetically suspicious.

Open 2017 neurology source record

5. The clinician expressly records that he is not a movement-disorders neurologist

The record entertains PRKN/PARK2, PARK6, PARK7, PARK9, neuropathy questions, prognostication, levodopa sensitivities, and genetic complexity — and then records a scope limitation: “I am not an expert on these conditions (I am not a movement disorders neurologist).”

Usable sentence: The record contains its own scope limitation, yet the case remained under the authority of the very frame the clinician said lay outside his specialist expertise.

Open discharge summary source record

6. PRKN/PARK2 was not a later fantasy; it was named in the original diagnostic architecture

The record says that, given very young onset, akinetic rigidity, lack of dominant family history, marked symmetry, and excellent cognition, there was a question whether Liam had a PRKN/PARK2 disorder. The later genetic clarification does not arrive from nowhere. It returns to a possibility the record itself had already named.

Usable sentence: The later genetic result did not invent a new issue; it vindicated a possibility the 2017 record had already placed on the table.

Open discharge summary source record

7. Genetic testing was deferred despite being linked to prognosis and levodopa sensitivity

The record says a genetic diagnosis might help with the actual neuropathological form, prognostication, and understanding levodopa sensitivities, but then reasons that testing may not change current management and could be delayed until whole-genome or whole-exome knowledge improves.

Usable sentence: Genetic testing was treated as not management-changing at the same time as the record admitted it might bear on prognosis and levodopa sensitivity.

Open discharge summary source record

8. Levodopa was deferred while the diagnosis was allowed to govern

The discharge material says ordinary therapy would include levodopa or dopamine agonists, but that in this type of situation they would be used with extreme caution and left off as long as possible. Later in the same narrative, the patient is told they may regret not starting sooner.

Usable sentence: Caution about treatment was not matched by caution about finality.

Open discharge summary source record

9. The 2018 record shows the outside-left-foot phenomenon returning when procyclidine was stopped

In 2018, the record says that when procyclidine was stopped, the first thing that changed rapidly was the return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon; symptoms worsened, and Liam became more “shifty” and “unrelaxed.”

Usable sentence: The foot problem behaved like a medication-sensitive neurological signal, not a safely externalised podiatric subplot.

Open 2018 neurology source record

10. The same 2018 note says he could be “very much better” with levodopa or another dopamine agonist

The record then says, plainly, that Liam “could be very much better” if levodopa or another dopamine agonist were given, but that for clearly set-out reasons this was not wanted “for quite some time.”

Usable sentence: The record knew there was a route that might substantially improve him, but chose deferral while the diagnostic frame continued to harden.

Open 2018 neurology source record

11. In 2021, DBS was wrongly narrowed to tremor after years of non-tremor, dystonic, young-onset complexity

By 2021, the record already contained young onset, atypicality, dystonic gait, PRKN/PARK2 suspicion, medication sensitivity, dyskinesia risk, and minimal tremor. Yet when DBS was raised, the answer was that DBS is “only ever for the tremor element” and “will not help with anything else.”

Usable sentence: The diagnosis was broad when it justified caution, but narrowed to tremor when it might have opened an advanced-therapy question.

Open 2021 neurology source record

12. Sinemet entered through substitution and management necessity, not a structured diagnostic re-test

The 2021 note records that Liam had felt undertreated and was started on a tiny Sinemet dose; then Kemadrin was removed because of eye-pressure/ocular issues and replaced with Sinemet, which was escalated rapidly enough to raise concern about chorea.

Usable sentence: The levodopa pathway entered through medication substitution and management necessity, not through a disciplined reopening of the original foot/gait question.

Open 2021 neurology source record

13. In 2023, the GP referral records severe bilateral burning foot and lower-leg pain causing a “death wish”

The 2023 urgent referral says Liam presented with severe bilateral burning pain in both feet and lower legs, extreme at times and causing “death wish,” while on escalating pregabalin/Lyrica doses. The GP adds: “I am worried about him” and asks for review “before we get into trouble here.”

Usable sentence: The unresolved foot problem did not remain theoretical; it became a documented crisis of pain, function, and safety.

Open 2023 neurology source record

14. The 2023 neurology note explicitly records the “misalignment”

The clinician records that Liam believed there was a “misalignment” between the clinician’s firm belief that everything had to be explainable by Parkinson’s and Liam’s “reasonable and justifiable” concern that much of it related to the feet as a separate problem. The same note acknowledges that the very first symptoms were in the feet.

Usable sentence: By 2023, the record itself recognised the dispute: the patient’s body kept returning to the feet while the clinician’s frame kept returning to Parkinson’s.

Open 2023 neurology source record

15. In 2025, the record accepts the contradiction and still contains it

The 2025 note says the one thing on Liam’s mind is foot pain; he describes “murder in the feet,” the first 150 footsteps as torture, and relief once Parkinson’s medications hit his system. The same note says Sinemet “should not do anything for pain,” that pain is not really a direct part of Parkinson’s, that dystonia can be painful, and that increasing Sinemet would probably give further relief but risks dyskinesia.

Usable sentence: By 2025, the record had accepted the essential contradiction: Parkinson’s medication helped the feet, pain was said not to be directly Parkinsonian, and more Parkinson’s medication might help further — but the governing frame still remained intact.

Open 2025 neurology source record

How these locks answer the easiest defences

If the defence says “this is hindsight”

PRKN/PARK2, atypicality, dystonia, genetic suspicion, levodopa caution, and scope limitation were already in the 2017 record.

If the defence says “the feet were incidental”

The GP referral begins through the feet, the 2018 medication response returns through the feet, the 2023 crisis returns through the feet, and the 2025 contradiction returns through the feet.

If the defence says “the diagnosis was reasonable”

Reasonableness of a working diagnosis is not authority to create uncorrectable finality.

If the defence says “the patient was listened to”

Yes — and that is precisely the point. He was recorded but not governing.

The killer formulation

What is unbelievable is not that the record is sparse. What is unbelievable is that the record is rich enough to convict its own ordering. It documents the open referral, the premature certification, the atypicality, the genetic suspicion, the non-specialist limitation, the deferred discriminator, the foot-medication response, the DBS narrowing, the explicit misalignment, the crisis-level foot pain, and the final containment of contradiction. The case is not built against the record. The case is built from the record.
