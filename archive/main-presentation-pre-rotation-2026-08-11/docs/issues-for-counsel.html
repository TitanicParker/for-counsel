Issues for counsel

A lawyer-facing map of the issues, routes, risks, and evidence required to test the case without converting it into a hindsight claim.

Purpose. This page is a triage map for counsel. It identifies issues to test, not findings to assert. The strongest version of the case avoids hindsight and asks whether the institution preserved a meaningful route for correction once reliance on the record had begun.

Executive theory for counsel

This is a proposed clinical negligence, data protection, maladministration, and professional-governance matter arising from premature reliance on a diagnostic frame in a complex young-onset movement-disorder presentation. The pleaded centre should not be that PRKN/PARK2 was missed in 2017. The pleaded centre should be that the institution recorded enough uncertainty to require diagnostic corrigibility, but allowed the Parkinsonian frame to govern care, certification, patient understanding, later referrals, complaint handling, and data processing without a meaningful reopening mechanism.

Primary legal questions

  1. Did the 2017 record contain sufficient atypicality and unresolved presenting features to require the Parkinsonian frame to remain expressly provisional?
  2. Was the patient given enough information to understand not only treatment risks, but the diagnostic consequence of deferring levodopa or dopamine agonist therapy?
  3. Did the discharge correspondence create or contribute to administrative finality, including LTI certification and downstream reliance, before the initiating foot-centred problem had been resolved?
  4. Did later medication response, persistent foot symptoms, dystonic phenomena, and specialist/genetic developments generate a duty to reopen, qualify, or correct the governing record?
  5. Was the patient’s testimony recorded but deprived of effective authority in the hierarchy of explanation?
  6. Did the institution’s complaint handling misclassify a corrigibility/reliance complaint as a mere clinical-judgment dispute?
  7. Did the record become inaccurate, incomplete, or misleading for ongoing GDPR/data-protection purposes once later information materially qualified the earlier frame?

Facts counsel should test

  • What was the exact standard of care in 2017 for young-onset atypical Parkinsonism with dystonic gait, preserved cognition, possible autosomal-recessive disease, and unresolved foot symptoms?
  • Would a reasonably competent neurologist have referred to a movement-disorders specialist or genetics service earlier, particularly after acknowledging non-specialist status?
  • Was levodopa deferral reasonable only if paired with an explicit provisionality / reopening plan?
  • What clinical significance should have attached to procyclidine withdrawal and the return of outside-left-foot phenomena?
  • Was the 2021 DBS explanation materially inaccurate, and if so did it evidence inadequate advanced-therapy counselling or failure to refer?
  • When did actionable knowledge arise for limitation purposes: 2017 discharge, later medication response, 2023 crisis, 2025 Sinemet CR relief, 2025/2026 specialist reassessment, or genetic confirmation?
  • What damage is attributable to the alleged foreclosure, as distinct from underlying disease progression?
  • What records, teaching records, consent records, supervisory records, emails, and complaint records remain undisclosed or disputed?

Pleading cautions

  • Do not plead the case as simple misdiagnosis if the stronger claim is premature authority and failure of corrigibility.
  • Do not assert that 2026 genetic knowledge should have been available in 2017.
  • Do not say DBS was necessarily indicated; say the patient received an inaccurate exclusionary account that narrowed advanced-therapy inquiry.
  • Do not say the patient was ignored; say he was documented without governing authority.
  • Do not allege malice unless evidence independently supports it.
  • Do not seek erasure of old records where supplementation and qualification are more legally coherent.

Best legal language

The institution did not merely make a diagnosis; it allowed a provisional diagnostic frame to acquire durable reliance while the record itself preserved reasons why that reliance should have remained corrigible.

Disclosure / evidence requests

  • Complete unredacted clinical record, including internal notes, drafts, addenda, clinic letters, nursing contacts, and medication-change communications.
  • Movement-disorder referral history and any internal discussions about genetics, dystonia, levodopa, dopamine agonists, or DBS.
  • All complaint-handling material, including classification decisions, QPS correspondence, SIMT/PAR-related material, and reasons for any refusal to escalate.
  • GDPR access, rectification, and restriction correspondence from hospital and university controllers.
  • Teaching, ward-round, student, roster, supervision, and educational records relevant to any remembered teaching encounter or bedside protest.
  • Pharmacy/prescribing history showing timing and changes in procyclidine, Neupro, Sinemet, Stalevo, Sinemet CR, pregabalin/Lyrica, gabapentin, and benzodiazepines.

Counsel’s strategic test

The threshold question is whether the case can be supported by independent expert evidence without depending on hindsight. The ideal expert opinion would say not that PRKN should have been diagnosed in 2017, but that the documented uncertainty, atypicality, foot-centred onset, dystonic features, treatment deferral, and later response required preservation of diagnostic openness, referral, qualification, or reopening.

Relief sought

  • Compensation if negligence, causation, and limitation can be made out.
  • A qualified corrective addendum to the clinical record.
  • Restriction or contextualisation of disputed health data where continued reliance is unsafe or misleading.
  • A reasoned response to the governance complaint, not merely a clinical defence.
  • Acknowledgment that the complaint concerns corrigibility, reliance, and record governance.
  • Institutional learning on young-onset atypical movement-disorder records and patient-facing uncertainty.

Short formulation

The case asks whether a healthcare institution can document uncertainty, rely on a provisional frame as though it were settled, and then treat later contradiction as merely another fact inside that frame. The proposed answer is no: once a record is relied upon, the institution must keep it corrigible.
