# Current Case Position — Management Invariance, Omission, Asymmetric Integration and Record Impeachment

**Date:** 13 August 2026  
**Purpose:** controlling present-outlook document for the current case theory. This document supersedes weaker formulations based primarily on generic `failure to reassess`. It is an analytical control note, not a pleading, expert report, clinical diagnosis, finding of negligence, dishonesty or causation. Every factual proposition remains subject to its underlying source and source-status classification.

---

# 1. Present force of the case

The case is now best understood as a dispute about **which variable was allowed to govern the longitudinal record**.

The record contains genuine diagnostic uncertainty, genuine structural foot disease, genuine treatment risks, and genuine patient-responsive changes in medication. Those facts must remain intact.

But the strongest current documentary architecture is:

> **The July 2017 management plan was substantially settled while diagnosis, genetics and the foot/movement relationship remained unresolved. Later evidence repeatedly changed treatment locally, but the presently available record does not show that materially contrary evidence was allowed to reopen the global architecture built on the original partition.**

The central question is therefore:

> **Was the patient's evolving condition governing the management plan, or did the management plan increasingly govern what the patient's evolving condition was allowed to mean?**

The latest independent management-plan audit ranks **path-dependent management** as the strongest overall documentary fit, while finding a later transition toward the narrower provable element of **conscious interpretive preservation** by 2025.

The current case should therefore be organised around four mutually reinforcing mechanisms:

1. **Management invariance** — the treatment/disposition architecture was formulated as substantially stable before diagnostic and genetic resolution.
2. **Material omission** — the patient reports a direct pre-discharge protest against the unresolved-foot/Podiatry disposition; the durable discharge summary contains no such protest. If independently corroborated, this becomes a demonstrated material omission from the foundational document.
3. **Asymmetric integration** — later neurological evidence was allowed to alter treatment prospectively without being given equivalent retrospective force against the historical causal partition and decisions built on it.
4. **Record impeachment** — the chart remains reliable for many direct observations, tests, prescriptions and institutional acts, but is materially unsafe as a self-authenticating longitudinal narrative on disputed patient-position, causal-classification, reassessment and closure propositions.

The short formulation is:

> **The body was allowed to change the prescription. It was not visibly allowed to change the history.**

# 2. The July 2017 discharge summary contains the seed of its own impeachment

The discharge summary is the foundational patient/GP-facing document. It is not merely a passive record of the admission. It communicates and stabilises the working diagnosis, treatment plan, referrals, genetic reasoning, patient-understanding account and follow-up structure that later clinicians may inherit.

The document itself preserves major uncertainty:

- severe young-onset Parkinsonism is established;
- structural foot pathology is real;
- the gait has a footdrop-like appearance `somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP`;
- PRKN/PARK2 is specifically contemplated;
- peripheral neuropathy is only a possibility;
- the relationship between leg symptoms, corns/calluses, burning and the neurological syndrome is expressly:

> **`Not clear`.**

Yet the same document operationalises a substantially settled plan:

- Rasagiline/Azilect;
- exceptionally slow Procyclidine/Kemadrin titration;
- Clonazepam and supportive therapies;
- Podiatry/Chiropody for the feet;
- deliberate prolonged caution/avoidance with levodopa and dopamine agonists;
- genetic assessment treated as usually not actively changing management;
- follow-up inside the selected architecture.

The key distinction is:

> **The documentary state was uncertainty; the operative state was partition.**

This is the first major architectural asymmetry.

# 3. The page-16 genetics/therapy language establishes prospective management invariance

The Genetics section of the discharge summary begins:

> **`I have explained to him the difficulties with the genetic assessment.`**

It then states:

> **`One of the chief difficulties with this is the question of whether a firm genetic diagnosis will actually actively change management?`**

and:

> **`This question is (in the majority of cases) usually answered in the negative.`**

The same section does not treat genetics as irrelevant in this patient. It specifically identifies PRKN/PARK2 as plausible based on very young onset, symmetry, akinetic-rigid presentation, preserved cognition and absence of a dominant family history. It also acknowledges that genetic knowledge may assist with neuropathological understanding, prognosis and potentially **`Levodopa sensitivities?`**

The immediately following Therapy reasoning then says, in substance:

> **`Whatever form and whatever genetic cause is the cause`**

ordinary levodopa/dopamine agonist treatment will be approached with extreme caution and:

> **`we will try to leave off on this for as long as possible.`**

This language is crucial.

It shows that before Healy's second opinion, before the Procyclidine withdrawal signal, and before later PRKN evidence, the first patient/GP account already treated the management plan as sufficiently stable to survive a firm genetic diagnosis in most cases and to apply **whatever** the genetic cause.

The strongest present formulation is:

> **Diagnostic and genetic information were permitted to refine description, but the discharge architecture did not make management contingent upon their resolution.**

This does not prove the plan was medically wrong. It proves an early **presumption of management invariance**.

The key question becomes:

> **If a firm genetic diagnosis was considered unlikely to change management before the external specialist had even examined the patient, what evidence was authorised to change management?**

# 4. No global reopening rule has been identified

The complete discharge record contains several careful **local** change rules:

- Procyclidine dose increases only if tolerated;
- optional test stopping if there is no obvious benefit;
- medications may be stopped before a second opinion to permit examination;
- DaTSCAN and pending investigations are processed;
- individual drugs may be changed for benefit or toxicity;
- levodopa may be needed `sooner or later`.

But the management-plan audit found:

> **NO DOCUMENTED GLOBAL REOPENING RULE IDENTIFIED.**

No prospective rule has been identified specifying that the global architecture should be reopened if:

- PRKN/PARK2 became substantially more likely or confirmed;
- an external expert described a materially different treatment model;
- Podiatry failed, ended or worsened symptoms;
- the feet became neurologically medication-responsive;
- Procyclidine withdrawal altered the disputed foot domain;
- dystonia became clearer;
- disability or pain became intolerable;
- neurological medication was shown to affect the feet;
- peripheral neuropathy remained unsupported;
- the patient directly challenged the adequacy of the allocation.

The difference is fundamental:

> **The plan had rules for changing things inside it. The record does not identify a rule for reopening the plan itself.**

This is the core of management invariance.

# 5. The no-levodopa decision was part of the architecture, not merely a drug choice

The contemporaneous Monaghan-to-Healy correspondence says:

> **`Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?`**

This establishes four things:

1. a pre-existing Galway preference for prolonged levodopa deferral;
2. a collective/consultative element rather than an individual spontaneous choice;
3. an open-ended formulation — `insofar as maybe possible`;
4. a theoretical escape clause — `unless you'd advise otherwise`.

The contemporaneous page itself does not identify `Tim`; the separate later Monaghan material identifies Prof Timothy Counihan as the senior neurologist whose advice formed part of the treatment-caution architecture.

The patient separately recalls the position being communicated colloquially as a senior decision, including `my boss told me not to give you the good stuff`. That remains patient-source evidence unless independently corroborated.

The correct proposition is not that levodopa necessarily had to be started in July 2017. Independent expert evidence is required on that question.

The architectural proposition is:

> **With conventional dopamine replacement held outside the immediate treatment environment, the movement disorder could be managed conservatively while the unresolved feet were routed to Podiatry. The `Not clear` relationship did not have to be resolved before discharge.**

Thus treatment policy and causal partition were structurally linked.

# 6. The patient protest and the omission must be treated as separate evidential events

This distinction is now permanent.

## 6.1 The protest

The patient reports that before discharge he asked four medical students to summon the treating neurologist and challenged the adequacy of discharge because the foot problem remained unresolved. He recalls using the analogy of receiving a full NCT and being sent away on **bald tyres**. He recalls the neurologist responding in substance that obtaining Podiatry was as easy for the patient as for him.

The reported protest is patient-source evidence. The four students are potential real-world witnesses. Their identification and independent recollection are therefore high-value evidential targets.

The protest, if established, proves something about **the neurologist's knowledge before discharge**:

> **The patient did not regard the unresolved foot problem as adequately dealt with and directly challenged the sufficiency of the Podiatry disposition.**

The patient did not need to know the words PRKN, painful dystonia, phenotyping, dopaminergic challenge, anchoring or causal integration. His clinically material signal was simpler:

> **The problem that brought me here remains unresolved and you are discharging me anyway.**

## 6.2 The omission

The omission is a separate proposition.

The discharge summary contains:

- `Not clear`;
- Podiatry;
- levodopa/dopamine-agonist deferral;
- genetics and therapy reasoning;
- extensive language concerning patient understanding.

It does not contain the reported direct challenge to the adequacy of the foot/Podiatry disposition.

The document's silence is directly observable. What remains to be independently proved is the occurrence and substance of the event omitted.

If one or more independent students credibly corroborate the protest, the evidential position changes qualitatively:

> **The protest becomes independently established; comparison with the discharge summary then establishes a demonstrated material omission from the foundational document.**

That omission would not prove that every sentence in the discharge summary is false. Its importance is proposition-specific and structural.

It would materially undermine:

- any inference that silence proves absence of disagreement;
- any reading of `good understanding` as global agreement;
- any assumption that Podiatry was consensually accepted as sufficient resolution;
- any later account treating the discharge architecture as uncontested;
- the discharge summary's claim to be a complete neutral narrative of the decision process.

## 6.3 Why the omission is potentially the upstream mechanism

If the protest is corroborated, the sequence becomes:

> **unresolved mixed presentation → senior-linked levodopa deferral → feet operationally partitioned to Podiatry → patient expressly challenges unresolved feet → no documented global reopening → protest omitted from the durable discharge narrative → later clinicians inherit the architecture without inheriting the contemporaneous challenge to it.**

That is more than incomplete note-taking.

It provides a plausible upstream mechanism by which later path dependence could occur:

> **later readers inherit `Not clear + Podiatry + good understanding`, rather than `Not clear + Podiatry + patient expressly disputes adequacy of the arrangement`.**

The evidential meaning of every later foot event would be different under the latter record.

This is why the omission belongs at the centre of record impeachment.

# 7. `Good understanding` cannot carry the weight later placed on it

The discharge summary records that the patient had a good understanding of why clinicians wished to delay levodopa/dopamine-agonist treatment at least for then.

That directly supports comprehension of the treatment-risk explanation.

It does **not** by itself establish:

- agreement with the causal partition;
- satisfaction that the feet had been dealt with;
- acceptance of Podiatry as sufficient;
- absence of protest;
- understanding of every diagnostic implication of prolonged treatment deferral;
- understanding that genetic clarification was considered largely management-neutral;
- agreement with the full discharge architecture.

If the protest is corroborated, `good understanding` and disagreement would demonstrably coexist.

The correct evidential rule is:

> **Understanding is not agreement. A patient can understand a clinician's rationale and expressly reject the adequacy of the resulting plan.**

This is central to patient-position impeachment.

# 8. Healy was the first explicit policy-level stress test

Galway asked Healy while disclosing the existing preference:

> **`Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?`**

Healy then examined the patient and wrote:

> **`I think probably has Park II and I would suggest testing that initially.`**

He recorded dramatic motor benefit from Procyclidine and Rasagiline and stated that PARK II patients can manage for long periods with:

> **`relatively low doses of Dopamine replacement therapy`**

while becoming dyskinetic if **over-used**.

Healy did not order immediate levodopa. That must remain explicit.

But his treatment wording is materially distinguishable from Galway's existing formulation:

- Galway: hold levodopa back insofar as possible;
- Healy: relatively low dopamine replacement over long periods, avoiding over-use.

This gives the documentary test:

> **If Healy's response was not enough to trigger explicit reconsideration under the `unless you'd advise otherwise` clause, what would have been?**

The presently available record does not identify the reconciliation.

This is not proof of selective bad faith. It is evidence of selective **effect**.

The controlling source-genealogy question is:

> **Did the pre-existing Galway management plan govern the practical interpretation of Healy's opinion more strongly than Healy's opinion governed the plan?**

# 10. April 2018 is the first major documentary demonstration of conscious policy maintenance

The 24 April 2018 note must be treated as one knowledge state.

By then the treating neurologist simultaneously possessed or recorded:

- the Podiatry disposition;
- Healy's probable-PARK-II opinion;
- suspected PARK2/PRKN;

> **April 2018 shows conscious maintenance of a pre-existing treatment policy in the presence of information capable of challenging its assumptions.**

This proves policy maintenance more strongly than improper motive.

If the pre-discharge protest is independently corroborated, the April 2018 knowledge state becomes still stronger because the same clinician would then have already known that the patient had directly challenged the unresolved-foot disposition before leaving hospital.

# 11. The Procyclidine event made `Not clear` less clear in only one direction

The Procyclidine chronology must remain exact.

The discharge summary contemplated a later optional test stop if there was no obvious benefit. The actual two-week withdrawal later occurred because of visual blurring. These are distinct facts.

The reason for the actual withdrawal does not erase the information generated by it.

The evidential layers include:

- contemporaneous patient email: tension rebuilding, feet restless, gait falling apart;
- contemporaneous later patient email: stop/restart/re-escalation with dramatic global improvement described as `living in a miracle`;
- April 2018 treating-neurologist record: the first rapidly changing feature was the outside-left-foot/corn-site phenomenon; the patient was more `shifty` and `unrelaxed` off Procyclidine.

The safe conclusion is:

> **The disputed foot/lower-limb domain was neurologically pharmacologically modifiable by late 2017.**

That did not necessarily identify the generator of every symptom. It did, however, reduce the neutrality of the original partition.

`Not clear` therefore had two legitimate possible futures:

- **separation becomes better established**, through independent local/peripheral evidence;
- **integration becomes better established**, through medication-state, dystonic, OFF, gait and PRKN evidence.

The later record increasingly supplied integration evidence while preserving real structural pathology.

The problem is not that mixed causation remained possible.

The problem is that the historical management partition did not visibly move with the increasing neurological evidence.

# 12. Integration required reopening the whole decision architecture

Integration never required declaring every foot symptom Parkinsonian.

It required reopening the **dependency structure** created in July 2017.

A proper integration exercise would have had to revisit:

1. **The original `Not clear` finding** — what remained unresolved and which later facts changed the probabilities.
2. **Podiatry allocation** — whether Podiatry was adjunctive for real structural lesions or had become the effective answer to the entire foot syndrome.
3. **Symptom map** — focal corn/pressure pain; burning; toe curling/clawing; dystonic posture/throw; restlessness; gait breakdown; OFF/morning pain; central pain modulation.
4. **Procyclidine evidence** — what precisely returned, why, and what it meant for the original partition.
5. **Peripheral neuropathy** — hypothesis versus objectively supported diagnosis; what evidence supported it and what could falsify it.
6. **Local-treatment responses** — supports, paring and bunion covers can establish a local component without proving the medication-sensitive domain was also local.
7. **Pregabalin evidence** — benefit does not prove neuropathy; persistent severe pain at high dose requires mechanism reassessment.
8. **Accumulating neurological phenotype** — dystonic-looking gait, suspected PRKN, parkinsonian foot throw, toe curling, OFF dystonia, shuffling and medication timing.
9. **Levodopa balance** — whether disability, pain, probable PRKN and treatment responsiveness altered the risk-benefit balance.
10. **Purpose of dopaminergic treatment response** — what outcome was considered informative and what the patient was told.
11. **Sinemet responsiveness** — OFF dystonia, central modulation, altered loading, mixed mechanism or other explanation.
12. **Patient position** — `good understanding` separated from agreement, protest, requests and lived treatment priorities.
13. **Psychological and functional severity** — work, mobility, fear, reduced activity, `death wish`, `I won't live with it`, quality-of-life collapse.
14. **Ownership and feedback** — what Podiatry found, why it ended, what came back to Neurology and who owned integration.
15. **Genetics** — whether PRKN remained management-neutral and whether later diagnostic movement was adequately explained.
16. **The record itself** — one explicit longitudinal reconciliation rather than isolated local observations.

The governing proposition is:

> **Integration required more than acknowledging that Parkinson medication helped. It required allowing that fact to reopen the Podiatry allocation, peripheral-neuropathy hypothesis, levodopa-risk balance, symptom classification, patient understanding and historical interpretation of the case.**

Awareness is much more strongly supported than improper motive.

The evidential test is:

- what patient-specific evidence positively established the retained peripheral generator?
- what evidence could falsify it?
- where was the component-specific mixed model documented contemporaneously?
- where was the 2017 Procyclidine history incorporated?
- why did Podiatry worsening, pregabalin failure/incomplete control, OFF evidence and dopaminergic relief not change the historical classification?

If the retained model had no independent foundation and no falsification condition, frame-preserving interpretation becomes materially stronger.

# 16. Mixed mechanism is credible only if it is testable

A rigorous mixed model would distinguish at least:

- focal corn/pressure pathology;
- mechanical consequences of abnormal neurological posture/loading;
- painful OFF dystonia;
- peripheral neuropathy;
- central/non-motor pain modulation.

Each component should have evidence, predictions and revision criteria.

An unfalsifiable mixed model instead operates as follows:

- local pathology supports peripheral causation;
- local treatment benefit supports it;
- local treatment failure does not reduce it;
- pregabalin benefit supports it;
- pregabalin incomplete effect does not reduce it;
- OFF association is called modulation;
- Sinemet benefit is called modulation;
- profound CR relief is called modulation;
- PRKN/dystonia evidence does not reopen it.

The key expert question is:

> **What result, in this patient's actual longitudinal course, would have caused clinicians to reduce or abandon the peripheral-generator hypothesis?**

If no answer exists, the hypothesis was not operating as a genuinely corrigible clinical proposition.

Permanent rule:

> **The patient and chart are competing primary evidential sources on different propositions, not a primary chart and subordinate allegation.**

Where the chart says the patient `understood`, `accepted`, `didn't want`, or `agreed`, that is first **clinician-mediated patient-position evidence**.

# 18. Knowledge asymmetry and authority asymmetry explain why patient signals could fail to govern

The case contains at least six asymmetries:

1. **technical knowledge asymmetry** — clinicians understand neurology, genetics, risk and differential diagnosis;
2. **decision-state asymmetry** — clinicians know what remains unresolved and what institutional choices have been deferred;
3. **causal-meaning asymmetry** — the patient experiences treatment effects while clinicians control their formal interpretation;
4. **record asymmetry** — the institution authors the durable account inherited downstream;
5. **authority asymmetry** — only the institution can turn information into diagnosis, referral, testing, prescription, discharge or governance consequence;
6. **retrospective-access asymmetry** — the patient later reconstructs decisions from a record authored by those with greater contemporaneous institutional knowledge.

The governing sentence remains:

> **The patient supplies the signal. The institution controls whether the signal becomes knowledge.**

The reported bedside protest is the clearest early example.

The patient could say:

> **My feet remain unresolved and Podiatry is not an adequate answer.**

Neurology possessed the technical knowledge and authority to translate that into:

- causal uncertainty;
- ownership;
- need for reassessment;
- treatment implications;
- follow-up triggers.

If the protest occurred and was omitted, the institution also controlled whether later readers even knew the challenge had been made.

# 19. Record impeachment is now proposition-specific but deep

The record should not be dismissed wholesale.

The present reliability classification is:

> **Reliable for many direct factual components; materially impeached as a self-sufficient longitudinal narrative; unsafe for unqualified reliance on disputed causal, patient-position and adequacy-of-reassessment propositions.**

Strong or generally reliable domains include:

- objective examinations;
- test results;
- prescriptions;
- dates and direct institutional acts;
- clinician statements as evidence of what the clinician recorded/thought.

Materially qualified/impeached domains include:

- patient agreement;
- patient preference;
- absence of disagreement;
- adequacy of Podiatry as resolution;
- whether the feet were established as separate;
- whether peripheral neuropathy was established;
- whether later reassessment adequately reopened the original partition;
- retrospective explanations presented as though contemporaneous;
- downstream institutional reliance on inherited propositions.

The record can be factually accurate sentence by sentence yet still misleading in **architecture**, through categorisation, omission of conflict, derivative repetition and failure to reconnect later facts to earlier premises.

This is the next major prospective hinge.

If one independent student credibly corroborates the essential protest, the position changes immediately:

- the neurologist's pre-discharge knowledge of unresolved disagreement becomes independently supported;
- the absence of the protest from the discharge document becomes a demonstrated material omission;
- `good understanding` can no longer carry any implication of global agreement;

- later clinicians can be understood as inheriting a materially incomplete account.

> **How did the foundational clinical document come to omit a material pre-discharge challenge remembered independently by multiple contemporaneous witnesses?**

That still would not automatically prove dishonest intent. The hierarchy of possible explanations would include:

- ordinary compression;
- inadvertent omission;
- belief the protest was clinically immaterial;
- conscious decision not to include a challenge that complicated the management narrative.

But the evidential burden would have shifted substantially.

The next witness exercise must therefore separate:

- occurrence;
- substance;
- timing;
- attendance;
- exact response;
- whether any contemporaneous documentation was made;
- whether the students were on their first day/rotation as recalled.

No witness should be supplied with the later case theory before recollection is taken.

# 23. The current case sequence

The whole case can now be stated as follows:

> **A mixed foot/movement presentation enters Neurology → severe young-onset Parkinsonism and dystonic-looking lower-limb findings are recognised → PRKN/PARK2 is a live patient-specific possibility → a senior-linked policy seeks to hold levodopa back insofar as possible → the feet remain expressly `Not clear` → Podiatry is operationalised → the discharge summary states that firm genetics usually will not actively change management and that prolonged dopaminergic avoidance will apply `whatever` the genetic cause → the patient reports directly challenging the unresolved-foot/Podiatry disposition before discharge → that challenge is absent from the durable discharge narrative → Galway asks Healy while preserving the existing hold-back preference → Healy says probable PARK II, recommends testing and describes cautious relatively low dopamine replacement rather than over-use → the diagnostic content is incorporated but no visible policy-level reconciliation follows → Procyclidine withdrawal/restoration makes the disputed foot/lower-limb domain neurologically pharmacologically modifiable → April 2018 records that signal, suspected PARK2, anticipated major levodopa benefit and continued policy maintenance → later parkinsonian/OFF/dystonic/medication-state foot evidence accumulates → local treatment changes repeatedly occur → no identified global historical revision occurs → by 2025 dopaminergic benefit is directly acknowledged and therapeutically used, then interpreted as pain modulation `of whatever cause` so that a peripheral generator can remain → governance later preserves prior conclusions → 2026 genetics confirms PRKN-caused Parkinsonism and dystonia.**

The governing question is:

> **Why was evidence that repeatedly made the feet less `Not clear` never allowed to reopen the complete decision architecture that depended on keeping them sufficiently separate?**

The cleanest falsification test is:

No identified record presently performs this global operation.

The absence of any one such note proves little. The cumulative absence after repeated major stress tests is probative.

# 29. Current strongest conclusion

> **The July 2017 management plan began as an intelligible response to genuine uncertainty and real treatment risk. But the discharge document simultaneously treated the plan as sufficiently stable that firm genetic diagnosis would usually not actively change management and prolonged dopaminergic avoidance would apply `whatever` the genetic cause. No global reopening rule has been identified. > **Management increasingly appears to have become the organising constant. New evidence was permitted to change care inside the architecture, but the architecture governed what that evidence was permitted to mean globally and retrospectively.**

The final unresolved state-of-mind question is not whether the relevant facts were noticed. By 2025 they plainly were.
