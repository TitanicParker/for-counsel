# Case Argument and Surviving Record — 13 August 2026

## Purpose

This is the controlling contraction document for the present case.

It does not attempt to retell nine years of care. It asks a narrower question:

> **After targeted impeachment of the disputed narrative, what is left standing in the paperwork, and what argument do those surviving documents make?**

The case is now best presented as:

> **Decision → contradiction → preservation → reconstruction.**

The argument does not require proof that every foot symptom had one neurological cause, that Podiatry was inherently wrong, that immediate levodopa was mandatory in 2017, or that dishonesty/fraud is already established.

It depends on a much smaller documentary proposition:

> **A senior-linked July 2017 treatment architecture was created while the foot-neurological relationship remained expressly unresolved. Later evidence repeatedly weakened the practical separation of the feet from the neurological case and altered treatment, yet no identified document performs a corresponding global reconsideration of the original decision, its patient-position account, or its historical legitimacy. When that architecture later required defence, the retrospective account changed meaning at several points in ways that improved defensibility.**

---

## 1. The decision

The July 2017 discharge record is still one of the strongest documents in the case because it contains the hospital's own adverse admissions.

It records:

- severe young-onset Parkinsonism;
- real structural foot pathology;
- dystonic-looking / footdrop-like lower-limb posture expressly described as `NOT FOOT DROP`;
- PRKN/PARK2 as a live possibility;
- possible peripheral neuropathy rather than an established peripheral cause;
- the relationship between foot/leg symptoms and the neurological disorder as `Not clear`;
- Podiatry/Chiropody as the operative route for the feet;
- a strategy of avoiding levodopa and dopamine agonists `for as long as possible`;
- alternative symptomatic approaches first;
- genetics as usually not actively changing management;
- treatment caution applying `whatever` the genetic cause;
- the possibility that levodopa might work very well and sustainably;
- the express prospective warning: `we may regret that we did not start this earlier`;
- senior/colleague-linked caution and a record of `good understanding` of the rationale `at least for now`.

The decision therefore was not merely "do not start levodopa today." It was a management architecture.

What the record does **not** identify is a global reopening rule. It specifies local titration and stop rules for individual drugs, but no identified criterion by which the architecture itself had to be reconsidered if:

- PARK2 became more likely;
- Healy materially differed;
- Podiatry failed, ended or worsened symptoms;
- the feet proved medication-responsive;
- OFF dystonia emerged;
- peripheral neuropathy remained unsupported;
- disability or pain became extreme;
- or the patient challenged the disposition.

That missing reopening rule is not a separate theory. It is part of the argument about whether the plan was genuinely provisional.

## 2. What `good understanding` can actually prove

The phrase `good understanding` survives as evidence that Monaghan recorded a contemporaneous assessment of comprehension.

It does **not** prove by itself:

- agreement;
- consent to maximum postponement;
- acceptance of Podiatry as sufficient resolution;
- absence of dissent;
- understanding of senior/collective decision ownership;
- understanding of the role of genetics or Healy in potentially reopening the policy;
- understanding of the absence of a defined reopening trigger;
- continuing agreement over later years.

The internal mismatch matters:

> **The patient's understanding is attached to `at least for now`; the governing management language is `for as long as possible`.**

Those propositions are not naturally equivalent.

Accordingly, the hospital may rely on `good understanding` to prove that the clinician recorded an impression of comprehension of the immediate rationale. It may not use that sentence, without independent support, to prove informed acceptance of the full long-term architecture.

If the reported bedside protest is later independently corroborated, the significance increases sharply: the phrase could remain literally true as to comprehension while the overall narrative would become materially misleading if known dissent was omitted.

## 3. The first test: Healy

The Monaghan-to-Healy correspondence is a critical surviving primary source because it reveals the policy before external review:

> `Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?`

This shows two things simultaneously:

1. a pre-existing collective/senior-linked policy of holding back levodopa; and
2. an apparent external escape clause.

Healy then supplied a materially different formulation:

- probable PARK II;
- test PARK II first, then a panel if negative;
- patients with PARK II may manage for long periods with relatively low dopamine replacement;
- they may become dyskinetic if treatment is `over-used`.

Healy did **not** order immediate levodopa and did not provide a patient-specific titration schedule.

The argument therefore does not overstate him.

The point is narrower:

> **"Relatively low replacement; avoid over-use" is not the same proposition as "withhold for as long as possible."**

The record subsequently adopts the probable/suspected PARK2 branch, but no identified note formally adjudicates Healy's treatment formulation against the pre-existing maximum-deferral policy.

That makes Healy a natural test of whether the architecture was genuinely open to being changed.

---

## 4. The second test: Procyclidine and April 2018

The Procyclidine withdrawal/restoration episode materially strengthens the argument because it links the disputed foot/lower-limb domain to neurological pharmacological change.

The record supports that withdrawal was followed by rapid change at the outside-left-foot phenomenon, greater `shifty` / `unrelaxed` deterioration and broader worsening, with improvement after restoration/re-escalation.

This does not prove that every corn, callus or pain sensation was neurological. It does not prove what levodopa would have done.

But by April 2018 Monaghan personally knew:

- the disputed foot domain was medication-sensitive;
- PARK2/PRKN remained suspected;
- levodopa could make the patient `very much better`;
- and yet the policy remained: `we do not want to do that – at least for quite some time`.

That is the first clear documentary threshold for **conscious policy maintenance**.

---

## 5. The feet as the organising phenotype

The strongest patient-centred causal formulation is not "all foot symptoms were Parkinsonian."

It is:

> **The feet were the principal functional and experiential interface through which structural pathology, pain, abnormal movement, medication state, standing and walking repeatedly converged.**

The GP's 2017 referral presented one integrated neurological question involving feet, gait initiation, tone, toe clawing, dexterity and threatened function.

The hospital identified both neurological disease and real structural foot pathology, but the relationship remained `Not clear` while Podiatry became operative.

Podiatry itself was reasonable for corns, calluses, pes planus, hammertoes, pressure, footwear and mechanical pain.

The weakness is not the referral. It is the absence of demonstrated longitudinal integration around it:

- no explicit owner of the unresolved whole;
- no visible synthesis plan;
- no clear return/reopening trigger;
- no demonstrated proposition-level reconciliation when neurological evidence accumulated.

This distinction is important because it defeats the false binary that the case requires proving Podiatry "wrong."

## 6. What 2021 does and does not prove

The hospital will reasonably point out that Sinemet began in 2021.

That fact survives impeachment and must be accepted.

But drug commencement is not the same as phenotype formulation.

The visible 2021 record documents:

- undertreatment concern;
- removal of Kemadrin because of ocular/visual pressure concerns;
- cautious Sinemet initiation and escalation;
- Comtess use;
- dyskinesia/chorea and impulse-control concern;
- a desire not to increase too far.

What it does **not** visibly document is a structured foot-centred response model tied to:

- pain;
- clawing/curling;
- foot throw;
- gait initiation;
- first steps;
- dose onset/wearing-off;
- overnight medication absence.

The safe proposition is:

> **The 2021 regimen was not shown to have been designed or evaluated around the disabling foot phenotype. It may have been a reasonable general starting regimen; whether it was clinically wrong is an expert question.**

This gives a precise answer to the defence "but he was on Sinemet from 2021."

## 7. The 2023 warning threshold

By 2023, ordinary uncertainty no longer explains the whole course.

The continuity record now contains:

- severe bilateral burning feet/lower legs;
- major quality-of-life burden;
- possible OFF dystonia;
- concern about insufficient levodopa;
- morning/later-day worsening without Sinemet;
- nurse assessment of undertreatment / OFF-related leg pain and shuffling;
- Stalevo-facilitated movement and pain relief;
- inability to sustain later activity;
- express clinician-patient `misalignment` around causal explanation.

There is genuine counterevidence:

- structural pathology remained real;
- Lyrica helped some pain;
- patient accounts varied at times;
- one reported ten-day medication cessation did not produce an obvious Parkinsonian change;
- dyskinesia constrained escalation;
- mixed mechanisms remained plausible.

Those points justify uncertainty.

They do **not** justify treating the competing neurological model as though it had not become materially testable.

By 2023 the record itself shows conscious awareness that the inherited causal organisation was under pressure.

## 8. The 2025 turning point

The 2025 note is one of the strongest surviving documents because the patient's temporal account finally becomes the organising basis for a targeted therapeutic experiment.

The note maps:

- last Sinemet around 8 p.m.;
- bedtime around 1 a.m.;
- rising around 8:45 a.m.;
- `murder in the feet`;
- the first 150 footsteps as `torture`;
- hobbling from bed and difficulty tying runners;
- relief once Parkinson medication reached the system;
- the expectation that more Sinemet would probably produce more relief;
- dyskinesia as the principal counterweight.

Nighttime controlled-release Sinemet was then prescribed around that history, with an early-morning option discussed. Later `profound relief` was acknowledged.

The critical change was not merely the formulation.

> **The patient's lived temporal observations acquired direct prescribing authority.**

This supports the contributory causal proposition that earlier systematic use of the patient's longitudinal foot-state information might have exposed the medication-timing mismatch sooner.

It does **not** prove that CR was mandatory in 2021 or that the same response would necessarily have occurred then.

## 9. What the later defensive record changes

The later defensive record contains several directional transformations that matter because they change the historical impression while leaving the underlying primary sources available.

### Regret

2017:

> `we may regret that we did not start this earlier`

Later:

> the patient may wish treatment had been started earlier.

The subject and responsibility change.

### Counihan

Formation:

- senior/colleague authority;
- `Myself and Tim`.

Accountability:

- advice was `only what I already knew`;
- Counihan says he had `not been involved in your care`;
- regulator narrows this to `not directly involved`.

The surviving argument is that authority appears stronger at formation than at accountability. Missing meeting records prevent a final allocation of role.

### Healy

The existence of `a second opinion` is later invoked. That is literally true.

But the surviving Healy source does not establish endorsement of maximum postponement. It establishes probable PARK2 and cautious low-dose replacement with avoidance of over-use.

### Genetics

The record moves from PRKN/PARK2 being plausible/probable/suspected to a later idiopathic formulation without an identified documentary bridge.

The 2026 PRKN result does not prove earlier misconduct. It makes the missing bridge materially important.

### Pregabalin

The source chronology shows GP initiation for severe pain and escalation through several doctors.

The later account says pregabalin was introduced in part to spare further Sinemet increase.

That later rationale may genuinely describe Neurology's later continuation strategy. It does not naturally describe the original initiation.

### Pain `of whatever cause`

By 2025 dopaminergic foot benefit is accepted, but historical causal force is contained by the explanation that dopamine modulates pain `of whatever cause`.

That may be clinically sincere. Its forensic significance is that it allows treatment to change without requiring historical reclassification.

## 10. What targeted impeachment does to the record

The whole chart is not discarded.

The record remains strong evidence of:

- objective examination findings;
- tests;
- prescriptions;
- dates;
- direct recorded observations;
- the fact that clinicians made particular decisions;
- contemporaneous adverse admissions against the hospital's later position.

But presumptive narrative reliability is withdrawn or reduced in the disputed domains where the same decision-maker or institution is effectively asked to certify its own completeness or legitimacy.

Those domains include:

- patient agreement and acceptance;
- completeness of disclosure;
- absence of dissent;
- decision ownership and authority;
- causal allocation of the feet;
- treatment-purpose reconstruction;
- specialist validation;
- completeness/fairness of the historical narrative;
- independent validity of later governance conclusions where source genealogy is not demonstrated.

The handling rule is:

> **The hospital may prove that a clinician recorded X. It may not automatically prove that X was complete, true in the broader sense later asserted, independently corroborated, or sufficient to establish historical legitimacy.**

Repetition from a common originating source is not independent corroboration.

## 11. The organising motive

The strongest present comparative state-of-mind hypothesis is that protection of the July 2017 decision became the organising interest of the later defensive record.

The reason is directional consistency:

- clinician regret becomes patient hindsight;
- senior authority contracts at accountability;
- Healy's existence is retained while the treatment distinction is not adjudicated;
- probable/suspected PARK2 hardens toward idiopathic certainty;
- pregabalin acquires a more coherent retrospective purpose;
- dopaminergic response is accepted without equivalent historical force;
- later dystonia is described as `subsequent` rather than expressly integrated with the earlier lineage.

The strongest fair classification remains:

- strong organising-motive hypothesis;
- best overall explanation of the directional pattern;
- reasonable and substantial basis to investigate reckless reconstruction in 2025;
- knowing reconstruction unresolved;
- deliberate concealment, conspiracy and legal fraud not proved.

The case does not need a stronger motive allegation in order to succeed evidentially.

## 13. Six issues for counsel

The case should now be argued through six issues only.

### Issue 1 — What exactly was decided in July 2017?

Was this a short provisional pause, or a maximum-deferral architecture with no defined global reopening rule?

### Issue 2 — What did the patient actually understand and accept?

Does `good understanding ... at least for now` prove informed acceptance of `for as long as possible`? Presently, no.

### Issue 3 — Did external and pharmacological evidence genuinely reopen the policy?

Healy, PARK2 suspicion and Procyclidine created early opportunities for reconsideration. April 2018 shows continued policy maintenance.

### Issue 4 — Were the feet adequately integrated into Neurology over time?

Podiatry was reasonable for structural components, but the unresolved relationship lacked a visible integrated ownership/reopening architecture. By 2023, OFF/dystonic and medication-state evidence made systematic testing necessary as a clinical question.

### Issue 5 — What does the 2025 CR episode prove?

It proves that a patient-centred temporal account directly organised a targeted formulation experiment followed by reported profound relief. It supports earlier treatability and contributory delay, but not a specific earlier mandatory regimen without expert evidence.

### Issue 6 — Can the later hospital record prove the legitimacy of the earlier hospital decision?

Not by itself in the disputed narrative domains. Later representations must be traced to source, tested against contemporaneous wording and separated from derivative institutional repetition.

## 15. The argument now

The case can now be stated without a sprawling mechanism theory:

> **The hospital received an integrated foot-gait-tone problem and identified severe young-onset Parkinsonism, but left the relationship of the feet to the neurological disorder expressly unresolved. At the same time it adopted a senior-linked policy of withholding ordinary dopaminergic treatment for as long as possible, treated genetics as generally management-neutral, and recorded only that the patient understood the rationale `at least for now`. The first external specialist review and the first pharmacological perturbation both produced evidence capable of qualifying that architecture, yet by April 2018 the treating neurologist expressly maintained the policy while acknowledging that levodopa could make the patient very much better. Over the following years the feet repeatedly acquired neurological significance—parkinsonian foot throw, OFF dystonia, undertreatment, medication-state pain and shuffling, persistent toe curling, and finally clear Sinemet-responsive morning foot disability. Those facts changed treatment. The record does not identify the corresponding act by which they changed the historical interpretation of the original decision. In 2025, when the patient's overnight and first-step history was finally allowed to organise prescribing, targeted controlled-release Sinemet was followed by reported profound relief. When the history later became the subject of complaint and regulatory defence, several formulations changed in a common direction: clinician regret became patient hindsight, senior authority was minimised, Healy's existence was retained without visible adjudication of his treatment distinction, probable PARK2 became idiopathic certainty without an identified bridge, and later pregabalin strategy was projected backward over its origin. Those changes do not prove dishonesty. They do materially impeach the later record as a self-sufficient account of patient agreement, causal allocation, decision authority and historical legitimacy. The hospital's own primary documents remain admissible and powerful; what they no longer do is prove the completeness and legitimacy of the narrative merely because the hospital authored and repeated it.**

## 16. Final working rule

The project should now proceed by contraction.

For each new document, ask only:

1. Does it materially change one of the six counsel issues?
2. Does it independently prove or disprove a disputed proposition?
3. Does it add genuine corroboration, or merely repeat an existing source?
4. Does it alter the surviving paperwork spine?
5. Does it change the expert-evidence target?

If not, file it as support and do not allow it to enlarge the case.

> **The case is now strongest when it uses the hospital's own surviving primary documents affirmatively, withdraws automatic narrative privilege only where justified, and keeps the argument focused on the few propositions that remain decisive after impeachment.**
