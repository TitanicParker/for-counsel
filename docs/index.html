Counsel-facing case orientation

The corrigibility and reliance case

This is not a hindsight misdiagnosis case. It is a case about a medical record that preserved uncertainty in words, while allowing a provisional diagnostic frame to acquire durable clinical, statutory, administrative, and interpretive authority.

Central strand

Documentation is not corrigibility.

The central proposition is that the patient was extensively recorded, but not allowed to become governing in the record. The problem is not that the clinicians failed to document uncertainty. The problem is that the documented uncertainty was deprived of operative force.

Central formulation: the institution accepted a foot-centred gait failure, recorded atypicality, genetic suspicion, dystonic-looking gait, preserved cognition, medication caution, and unresolved foot symptoms, but nevertheless allowed a Parkinsonian frame to become authoritative before preserving a meaningful route back to correction.

The case should therefore be read as a corrigibility and reliance case. A historically accurate record can become operationally misleading when later relied upon without qualification. The record did not merely remember the patient; it governed him.

Two supporting rails

The argument runs on two rails.

Rail one: sequence and entitlement to finality

The first rail is that finality arrived before inquiry had earned it. The GP referral presented an open, foot-centred gait failure. The hospital record then fixed Parkinson's Disease as the primary diagnosis while preserving unresolved features that made ordinary closure unsafe.

The legal point is not that Parkinsonism was implausible. The legal point is that plausibility was allowed to become authority too soon.

Rail two: continuing reliance and failure to reopen

The second rail is that later contradiction was absorbed without being allowed to reorganise the case. Procyclidine response, Sinemet response, repeated foot pain, dystonic phenomena, patient challenge, advanced-treatment questions, and later genetic confirmation all operated as reopening signals.

The system's characteristic move was not denial. It was containment: accept the fact, preserve the frame.

Evidence map

How the record gives counsel a clean path through the case

Each point expands into the documentary significance and links back to the redacted source record.

1. Entry: the case begins through the feet

The case does not begin with a patient asserting a theory about Parkinson’s disease. It begins with a GP referral for urgent assessment of a deteriorating young man whose body was already presenting a complex gait, tone, foot, and functional problem.

The referral records an unusual festinant and stiff gait, bilateral foot issues, callouses and corns, difficulty initiating movement, increased tone in the upper and lower limbs, brisk reflexes, dexterity decline, and work-threatening deterioration. The GP does not declare a final diagnosis. The referral asks whether the condition is “parkinsonian or other underlying diagnosis.”

The initiating problem was not simply “possible Parkinson’s.” It was a foot-centred gait failure occurring in a young man whose function and employment were beginning to collapse. The feet were not incidental background. They were the visible, practical, disabling route through which the case entered care.

Clean proposition: the accepted scope of the referral included the feet. If the final record did not resolve the feet, it could not safely treat the diagnostic sequence as complete.

2. Discharge: the same artefact records uncertainty and performs finality

The discharge summary is the hinge document because it does two incompatible things at once. It records uncertainty, atypicality, and incomplete inquiry; but it also performs diagnostic, administrative, therapeutic, statutory, and interpretive finality.

On one side, the document states “Primary Diagnosis: Parkinsons Disease.” It sets a treatment plan, arranges follow-up, discusses employment and occupational health, records LTI certification, and creates a patient-facing narrative designed to consolidate understanding.

On the other side, the same document records features that should have restrained finality: very young onset, an atypical presentation, minimal tremor, prominent symmetry, preserved cognition, possible autosomal-recessive or genetic Parkinsonism, a gait that appears dystonic, unresolved leg and foot symptoms, and caution around levodopa or dopamine agonists.

The grammatical asymmetry matters. The diagnosis is declarative. The separation of the initiating foot problem is modal: the feet “may have been” separate. Finality and uncertainty coexist in the same artefact, but only one of them governs.

Clean proposition: the discharge document did not lack uncertainty; it subordinated uncertainty to finality.

3. 2018: medication response becomes a reopening signal

The 2018 note is important because it shows the body returning to the record through medication response. The patient had stopped procyclidine, and the first thing he noticed changing rapidly was the return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon. Symptoms worsened; he felt more “shifty” and “unrelaxed” off the medication.

The note also records that he could be “very much better” if levodopa or another dopamine agonist were given, while also stating that this route was being avoided for clearly set-out reasons. This is not just a medication-management detail. It is a reopening signal.

If the clinician records that the patient could be substantially better with levodopa or another dopamine agonist, but continues to defer that route, then deferral becomes more than cautious treatment management. It also becomes deferral of clarification.

Clean proposition: 2018 supplied evidence that the feet were medication-sensitive and therefore not safely peripheral; the record treated this as management complexity rather than a reason to reopen the hierarchy of explanation.

4. 2021: the DBS narrowing exposes the elasticity of the frame

The 2021 DBS statement is powerful because it reveals the elasticity of the Parkinson’s frame.

The patient was being treated as having young-onset Parkinson’s disease. That frame was broad enough to justify years of medication caution, levodopa sparing, dyskinesia risk management, impulse-control concern, long-term planning, Sinemet caution, and the continuing authority of the Parkinsonian diagnosis. But when DBS was raised, the frame narrowed abruptly. The patient was told that DBS was “only ever” for tremor and would not help anything else.

The point is not that DBS was necessarily indicated. The point is that it should not have been excluded by a tremor-only rule. This patient’s dominant problem had never been tremor. The record had known that from the beginning.

Clean proposition: DBS did not have to be right; it had to be accurately framed. Instead, it was wrongly narrowed in a way that preserved the existing pathway.

5. 2023: the misalignment becomes explicit

By 2023, the implicit dispute becomes explicit. The record is no longer merely carrying unresolved foot symptoms in the background. It records severe foot-centred difficulty, crisis-level pain, and an express misalignment between the clinician’s Parkinson’s frame and the patient’s belief that the feet remained separate, central, or insufficiently explained.

The note also acknowledges that the very first symptoms were in the feet. That means the record has now internalised the dispute. The patient’s objection is no longer merely remembered, implied, or reconstructed. It appears inside the clinical record as a recognised interpretive conflict.

The 2023 material also matters because it shows the cost of non-corrigibility. The patient’s severe bilateral burning foot and lower-leg symptoms are described as extreme, function-limiting, and psychologically dangerous.

Clean proposition: by 2023 the record itself recognised misalignment, but recognition did not become reopening.

Open 2023 neurology source record

6. 2025: contradiction is accepted and contained

The 2025 note is devastating because it records the contradiction in its most compressed form. The patient’s central concern is foot pain. He describes the feet as disturbing him since 2005; he hobbles out of bed; the first steps are torture; the Parkinson’s medications relieve the feelings in the feet once they hit his system.

The record then states that Sinemet should not do anything for pain, while also accepting that it relieves the experience of pain and that increasing Sinemet would probably provide further relief. It then restrains that possibility because increasing Sinemet would likely worsen dyskinesia.

The official rebuttal performs the institutional version of the same move. It accepts suffering, apologises, accepts that Sinemet helps the pain experience, and justifies cautious Sinemet escalation. But it preserves the governing idiopathic Parkinson’s frame.

Clean proposition: the 2025 material shows the institution accepting nearly every factual premise needed for reopening while refusing the structural consequence of reopening.

Counsel issue statement

The question for legal review

The issue is whether a healthcare institution may record uncertainty, rely on the record as settled, and then deny the recorded uncertainty any practical route back to correction.

In a complex diagnostic case, especially where the patient is young, atypical, and the record itself preserves unresolved features, a healthcare institution must not allow a provisional diagnostic frame to acquire durable clinical or administrative authority without preserving a meaningful route for correction, qualification, or reopening.

That is the strand. The two rails are sequence and reliance: finality before completed inquiry; continuing use of the resulting record despite repeated reopening signals.
