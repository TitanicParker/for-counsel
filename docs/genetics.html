2017–2026

PRKN was an early hypothesis before it became a later diagnosis

The genetic result is important because it gives later etiological coherence to features already present in the record. It must not be used to rewrite what clinicians knew in 2017.

The early record already raised recessive and PRKN disease

The 2017 clinical material considered the unusual age of onset, marked symmetry, akinetic-rigid phenotype, preserved cognition and lack of a dominant family history in genetic terms. PRKN/PARK2 was expressly identified as a possible cause, alongside other recessive Parkinsonism genes.

The record therefore did not treat genetic Parkinsonism as an unimaginable possibility. It was already part of the working diagnostic architecture.

By 2018 the wording became more specific

The April 2018 working record discusses whether to perform isolated PARK2/PRKN testing or a broader gene panel and records the specific PARK2/PRKN possibility as what “we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause”. It also says Prof Healy would be content with the isolated test or panel approach if the patient wished to proceed.

That wording directly establishes what the treating record attributed to the clinicians at that time. It does not independently establish Prof Healy’s exact words unless the native Healy correspondence is produced.

Testing was not treated as immediately management-changing

The same record explains a reason for delay: genetic confirmation was not thought likely to change current management, and there was an attraction to waiting for broader whole-genome knowledge. The patient could nevertheless request testing at any time.

This is relevant adverse evidence. It provides a contemporaneous clinical rationale rather than a documentary silence.

2026: later specialist confirmation

In February 2026 a movement-disorders specialist reported that genetic testing confirmed two pathogenic Parkin variants and concluded that the patient’s Parkinsonism and dystonia were due to compound heterozygous mutations in the Parkin gene.

That result settles an etiological proposition that had remained unconfirmed in the earlier record: the later specialist attributed both Parkinsonism and dystonia to PRKN/Parkin.

What the result does not do

The 2026 result does not establish that every historical foot symptom was dystonia. It does not establish that all pain was dopaminergic. It does not prove negligence. It does not mean the 2017 clinicians possessed the later genetic answer.

Its legitimate retrospective significance is narrower: it gives coherence to an early PRKN hypothesis and confirms that dystonia and Parkinsonism shared a Parkin cause in this patient.

The question for expert evidence

The useful question is not “why did they miss PRKN?” The record itself shows that they considered it. The expert question is what significance the early PRKN/dystonia architecture should have had for testing, referral, treatment strategy and later reconsideration of the unresolved lower-limb/foot phenotype.

Hindsight boundary. Later genetic confirmation may strengthen the importance of an earlier hypothesis, but it cannot be treated as knowledge available before testing established it.
