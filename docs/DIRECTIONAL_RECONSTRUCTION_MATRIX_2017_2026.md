## Core fact / inference boundary

The following propositions should be treated as **documentary facts or direct source propositions**, subject to the ordinary limits of the source wording:

1. **Monaghan and `Tim` favoured withholding levodopa.** Monaghan wrote to Healy: `Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?`
2. **Monaghan recognised prospectively that withholding levodopa might later be regretted.** The discharge summary states: `It is possible that he will do very well with levodopa and in a sustainable way whenever it is started. Indeed in time to come—we may regret that we did not start this earlier.`
3. **Healy supplied a materially distinguishable cautious replacement model rather than an explicit titration instruction or immediate start order.** He assessed probable PARK II, recommended testing, and wrote that PARK II patients can manage for long periods with `relatively low doses of Dopamine replacement therapy`, while becoming dyskinetic if `over-used`.
4. **Monaghan subsequently retained the pre-existing deferral policy.** By April 2018 he wrote that the patient could `obviously ... be very much better` if given levodopa or another dopamine agonist, but `we do not want to do that – at least for quite some time`, while also recording PARK2/PRKN as what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause` and stating that genetic confirmation did not particularly change current management.

The stronger conclusion is an **inference**, not a direct quotation:

> **It is a strong documentary inference that Healy's treatment implications were neutralised in practice because the governing plan had already acquired controlling force.**

This inference does **not** require claiming that Healy ordered immediate levodopa, that Galway deliberately ignored an instruction, or that improper motive is proved. It rests on the documented sequence: a pre-existing maximum-deferral preference, an express invitation to Healy to advise otherwise, a materially distinguishable cautious-use formulation, adoption of Healy's diagnostic branch, and no identified policy-level reconsideration before the pre-existing deferral rule is affirmatively restated.

## The governing directional pattern

The concern is not merely that later accounts contain isolated inaccuracies or imprecise paraphrases.

The stronger documentary pattern is directional:

> **The source of authority expands when the original decision requires legitimacy and contracts when responsibility for its consequences requires allocation.**

And:

> **The clinicians' acknowledged risk of having chosen wrongly becomes, retrospectively, the patient's anticipated dissatisfaction with a choice that had supposedly been fully explained.**

Across the record, the apparent transformations repeatedly move in the same direction:

- collective authority legitimates the original restriction;
- individual judgment later carries responsibility for implementation;
- Healy's existence legitimates independent review while his destabilising distinction is not visibly adjudicated;
- diagnostic uncertainty hardens retrospectively where that supports the later account;
- treatment-response evidence is allowed prospective therapeutic effect while denied equivalent retrospective force;
- known risks of symptomatic deprivation are answered with narrower progression reassurance;
- and responsibility for the architecture becomes progressively harder to locate when the architecture is challenged.

This does not itself prove dishonest motive. It does support a focused state-of-mind inquiry because repeated semantic and historical shifts appear to operate in one protective direction rather than randomly.

## Core proposition for counsel / expert use

> **It is a fact that Monaghan and Tim favoured withholding levodopa; a fact that Monaghan recognised this might later be regretted; a fact that Healy supplied a materially different cautious low-dose replacement model rather than an explicit titration instruction; and a fact that Monaghan subsequently retained the pre-existing deferral policy. It is a strong documentary inference that Healy's treatment implications were neutralised because the governing plan had already acquired controlling force.**

The inference is strengthened by the surrounding architecture:

- the discharge summary treated firm genetic diagnosis as usually not actively changing management;
- the therapy policy was written to apply `whatever` the genetic cause;
- Monaghan's referral expressly invited Healy to disturb the hold-back position;
- Healy strengthened the PARK2 branch and described restrained replacement rather than maximum postponement;
- no identified note adjudicates that treatment distinction;
- April 2018 adopts the PARK2 suspicion while affirmatively preserving prolonged non-use despite anticipated major benefit.

The strongest falsification evidence would be a contemporaneous record showing that Galway explicitly considered Healy's low-dose replacement formulation, explained why it did not alter the patient-specific policy, and set a defined condition or threshold for ending the `for as long as possible` deferral.
