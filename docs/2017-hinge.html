2017

The hinge was not failure to recognise Parkinsonism. It was whether an unresolved problem stayed genuinely open.

The record contained severe young-onset Parkinsonism, a dystonic-looking lower-limb abnormality, genuine structural foot disease and a live PRKN/PARK2 hypothesis. The relationship between the feet and neurological syndrome remained “Not clear.”

1. The case arrived open

The GP record did not begin with a settled Parkinson's diagnosis. It began with bilateral foot pain, abnormal gait and real structural pathology. By June the GP was considering Neurology specifically for the patient's “gait, feet and 'muscle tension' issues”; Occupational Health echoed the gait concern; increased leg tone was documented; and hospital neurological assessment followed.

Monaghan's contemporaneous correspondence to Healy later records that Liam was 39, may have had symptoms for approximately 13 years, and that this was his first time being seen by Neurology.

2. Severe Parkinsonism was recognised quickly

The inpatient examination documented markedly increased symmetrical tone, gross bradykinesia, rigidity and a strikingly abnormal gait. The lower-limb appearance was described as footdrop-like and somewhat suggestive of dystonic posture — expressly “NOT FOOT DROP”. Structural abnormalities including pes planus, hammertoes, corns and calluses were also real.

3. A levodopa-deferral strategy became controlling early

The treatment strategy was to hold levodopa and dopamine agonists back because Liam was very young and because of genuine concern about dyskinesia, impulse-control disorder and dopamine dysregulation.

Monaghan's contemporaneous letter to Healy captures the Galway position directly:

“Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?”

The question is not whether caution had a rationale. It did. The question is what evidence was capable of reopening the strategy once it existed.

4. By 6 July Podiatry was already being routed “as Parkinson's”

The GP record now supplies a hard contemporaneous fact:

“Phoned podiatry to discuss case - will mark as priority case and as Parkinson's - should be called mid September.”

This does not tell us what the written referral said or what Podiatry ultimately understood itself to be treating. It does show that, on the GP side, Podiatry was not being framed as unrelated routine foot care.

5. PRKN was already inside the case

The July 2017 record expressly considered PRKN/PARK2 because of very young onset, symmetry, akinetic-rigid pattern, absence of dominant family history and preserved cognition.

Monaghan supplied Liam and his GP with Galway departmental genetics material describing Parkin/PRKN disease using features including young onset, dystonia, symmetry and excellent levodopa responsiveness.

6. The feet nevertheless remained “Not clear”

The record considered whether the leg/foot symptoms might be separate, peripheral or neuropathic, while also documenting dystonic-looking movement and real structural disease. Its own conclusion on the relationship was: “Not clear.”

That is the correct starting point. The case does not need to rewrite 2017 as `all dystonia`. It asks what operationally happened to a question Neurology itself had not resolved.

7. Administrative Parkinson's classification was already operating during admission

A Long Term Illness Scheme form is visibly dated 9 July 2017 and uses Parkinson's disease as the qualifying clinical category. The completed discharge state also lists Parkinson's Disease as primary diagnosis.

This establishes an administrative/operative category before later etiological clarification. It does not establish that genetic or atypical possibilities had been excluded.

8. The reported protest is fixed to Monday 10 July

Liam now fixes the reported omitted bedside protest to Monday 10 July 2017, St Anthony's Ward, approximately 10:00–12:00. He says four medical students were together at his bedside on the first day of a new placement batch when he asked them to summon the neurologist.

He recalls challenging discharge with the analogy of receiving a “full NCT and [being sent] out on bald tires”, and recalls the response: “it's easy for you to get a podiatry appointment as me.”

This remains patient-source evidence pending independent corroboration. The new date, ward, time and first-day-batch details materially improve the witness-identification route.

9. The DaTSCAN was abnormal “as expected”

The 13 July addendum states:

“It is abnormal as was expected, with marked findings in both basal ganglia.”

The scan therefore confirmed an existing expectation of a dopaminergic Parkinsonian disorder. It did not itself resolve whether the aetiology was idiopathic or genetic. The underlying DaTSCAN indication still described the walk as “almost dystonically unusual” and suspected an autosomal-recessive Parkinsonian disorder.

10. Healy then personally saw Liam and recorded probable PARK II

On 12 September 2017 Prof Daniel G. Healy wrote after specialist review:

“I think probably has Park II and I would suggest testing that initially. If it is negative I would go on to test a panel.”

He also wrote:

“patients with PARK II tend to manage quite well for long periods of time with relatively low doses of Dopamine replacement therapy. They can become quite dyskinetic if over-used.”

Healy did not expressly order immediate levodopa commencement. But his actual wording now creates a new treatment-governance question: after Galway had expressly asked him to advise if he disagreed with holding levodopa back, what process considered his distinction between relatively low-dose dopamine replacement and over-use?

Read the materialised Healy source

11. By 2018 the diagnostic branch had strengthened but the treatment strategy had not visibly reopened

The April 2018 Neurology record states that PRKN was what we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause. The same note says Liam could “obviously ... be very much better” if given levodopa or another dopamine agonist, while also stating that “we do not want to do that — at least for quite some time.”

By then the issue was not whether PRKN had been contemplated or whether an external specialist had entered the case. The unresolved question is why the pre-existing treatment strategy remained controlling.

12. The later etiological transition is now itself a question

The source sequence now shows: PRKN/PARK2 considered in 2017; Healy saying probable PARK II in September 2017; Galway saying in 2018 that PARK2 is what we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause; and a March 2025 complaint response stating that the belief/understanding was idiopathic Parkinson's disease.

No presently identified source explains when, why or on what evidence that etiological transition occurred.

13. 2026 genetics answers the etiological question

St Vincent's later confirmed two pathogenic Parkin variants and concluded that Liam's Parkinsonism and dystonia were due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.

The later genetic result does not prove that every historical foot symptom was dystonia. It does establish that the PARK2 branch already visible — and later called probable by Healy — was the causal branch.

Updated controlling question. After Healy personally reviewed Liam, said he probably had PARK II, recommended PARK II testing first and described long-term relatively low-dose dopamine replacement as compatible with the condition, what process caused Galway's pre-existing levodopa-deferral strategy and unresolved foot partition to return to an open state?
