The hinge: an open problem becomes an operative framework

The 2017 record is strongest when read as a sequence rather than as a single finished artefact.

The referral did not arrive with a settled diagnosis

The June 2017 GP referral described a young man whose gait, movement and dexterity had deteriorated enough to threaten work. Both feet were materially within the presenting problem: corns, calluses, bilateral foot difficulty and an abnormal gait sat alongside stiffness, increased tone, brisk reflexes and trouble initiating movement. The referral asked whether this was Parkinsonian or some other underlying diagnosis.

That matters because the hospital accepted an open diagnostic problem. The feet were not merely a later complaint appended to an already-settled neurological case.

Neurology found real and severe Parkinsonism

The inpatient neurological assessment then supplied strong evidence of Parkinsonism: markedly increased tone, gross bradykinesia and a strikingly abnormal gait. Later DaTSCAN imaging supported a presynaptic dopaminergic disorder. Any account of the case that treats Parkinsonism as invented would be inconsistent with the record.

But the same assessment also described a footdrop-like posture as “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP”, and recorded structural foot abnormalities, burning and other lower-limb symptoms.

The relationship of the feet to the neurological syndrome remained unresolved

The clinical record considered whether the leg and foot symptoms might be separate, perhaps peripheral, or related in some other way. The recorded conclusion was not a firm separation. It was: “Not clear.”

This is the central documentary hinge. The record knew enough to say that the relationship was uncertain, while the practical pathways nonetheless had to move forward.

Parkinson’s disease became the operative diagnosis

The completed discharge material states Parkinson’s disease as the primary diagnosis and organises treatment strategy, follow-up, work advice and LTI around that framework. The decision to delay levodopa and dopamine agonists was deliberate and reasoned, principally because of the patient’s young age and concern about future treatment complications.

That treatment caution is real evidence in favour of the clinicians’ position. The legal and expert issue is not whether they had reasons. It is whether the same degree of provisionality was carried into the classification of the unresolved lower-limb and foot problem.

Podiatry became an operative pathway

The same discharge plan asked for outpatient Chiropody/Podiatry referral and records that an SHO had tried to arrange this in hospital. That may have been an entirely reasonable response to obvious local foot pathology.

The unresolved question is what the Podiatry pathway meant for the neurological uncertainty already recorded as “Not clear”. Was Podiatry adjunctive? Was it expected to resolve the local component while Neurology retained the broader question? Or did the feet become treated in practice as sufficiently separate? The presently available 2017 record does not answer that decisively.

PRKN was inside the early architecture

The record also considered very young onset, marked symmetry, lack of a dominant family history and preserved cognition in genetic terms. PRKN/PARK2 was expressly raised as a possible cause. That is important because the later 2026 Parkin result was not a wholly novel category imported into the case by hindsight.

It remains equally important not to overstate this. A live PRKN hypothesis is not a diagnosis, and the 2017 clinicians did not possess the later genetic result.

The discharge summary is a cumulative artefact

The operative discharge/LTI state preceded the formal 13 July DaTSCAN result. The imaging result was then incorporated by addendum into the completed document. That is not evidence of improper alteration; cumulative clinical records commonly gather later information.

Forensic reading nevertheless requires a distinction between the final appearance of the artefact and the decision-state that existed at each earlier point. The finished summary can appear more integrated than the chronology of decisions can presently be shown to have been.

Working hypothesis. The most useful description is not “the diagnosis was false”. It is that a clinically plausible Parkinsonian diagnosis became administratively and therapeutically operative while important questions about aetiology and the painful foot/lower-limb phenotype remained unresolved. Whether that was appropriate is for expert evidence.
