March 2025

A treatment event changes the patient’s understanding

March 2025 matters twice: clinically, because Sinemet CR was deliberately introduced into the severe morning-foot problem; evidentially, because the result appears to have changed how the preceding history was understood.

10 March: the feet are the central clinical problem

The clinic note begins with a familiar feature of the long record: the “one thing” on the patient’s mind is foot pain. He describes the feet as disturbing him since 2005, severe difficulty on getting out of bed, and the first approximately 150 steps as “torture”.

The note also records a treatment-state observation plainly: once the Parkinson’s medication reaches his system, there is relief in the feelings in the feet. The neurologist states that once Sinemet takes effect the feet feel better “from a pain perspective”.

The clinician does not simply relabel the pain as Parkinsonian

The same note expressly resists a simplistic interpretation. It says pain is not really a direct part of Parkinson’s, acknowledges that dystonia can be painful, and states that the particular pain being described is not, in the clinician’s view, a direct Parkinsonian phenomenology.

That is important qualifying evidence. The March note itself contains both the observed levodopa relationship and a competing clinical explanation.

But more Sinemet is expected to help

The clinician nevertheless records little doubt that increasing Sinemet would probably provide some further relief from the pain, while warning that doing so would likely worsen dyskinesia. The patient also raises the possibility that mornings are especially bad because he has gone overnight without Parkinson medication.

The note then considers slow-release Sinemet at night and concludes: “First of all, we will start Sinemet controlled release”.

The intervention is therefore not incidental to the foot problem. It is deliberately introduced into the morning symptom architecture, including pain, while preserving a competing explanation about the pain generator.

The subsequent response is described as profound

In the later complaint response, the treating neurologist says the 10 March prescription was “also to try to help the morning experience including of pain” and records that the escalation had given “profound relief currently”.

That later statement is important because it comes from the treating clinician’s own retrospective account. It proves what he represented in March 2025 about the purpose of the prescription and the result then being experienced. It does not by itself determine the mechanism of pain or what should have happened in 2017.

19 March: the complaint follows the discovery event

Nine days after the 10 March clinic, the formal complaint states that review was being sought after a recent discovery that the condition was dopamine-responsive and says this had not been explored during the course of care.

That sequence is evidentially important:

10 March targeted treatment → experienced change → 19 March complaint linking the discovery to the historical care.

The complaint proves the patient’s changed understanding and the timing of that understanding. It does not prove that the historical allegation is medically or legally correct.

Why the sequence matters for limitation

March 2025 is therefore also an adverse limitation point. Whatever later PRKN confirmation or disclosure material may add, the 19 March complaint is strong evidence that by that date the patient had connected a new treatment-response understanding with criticism of the preceding years.

Counsel will need to distinguish later discovery of stronger proof from the statutory date on which the essence of a potential claim became reasonably ascertainable.

Evidence boundary. March 2025 establishes a deliberately targeted dopaminergic intervention into the severe morning-foot problem, a subsequently reported profound response, and a contemporaneous change in the patient’s understanding. It does not establish that all historical foot pain was dystonia, that levodopa would have produced the same result throughout the preceding years, or that earlier treatment deferral was negligent.
