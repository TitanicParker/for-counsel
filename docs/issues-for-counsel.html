Questions requiring professional determination

Issues for counsel

The documents establish a sequence. They do not themselves determine standard of care, breach, causation, limitation or legal entitlement. This page identifies the questions that remain after the source record has been organised.

Working centre. The case is not that Parkinsonism was invented or that PRKN should have been diagnosed in 2017. The narrower issue is what happened when a clinically plausible Parkinsonian diagnosis became the operative framework for care while important questions about aetiology and the painful foot/lower-limb syndrome remained unresolved.

1. The 2017 classification

The first expert question is whether the 2017 combination of severe bilateral Parkinsonism, very young onset, atypical features, dystonic-looking lower-limb posture, possible recessive disease, PRKN/PARK2 discussion and unresolved foot symptoms required any particular degree of diagnostic provisionality, subspecialist input, testing or planned reconsideration.

The documents show that Parkinson’s disease became the operative diagnosis. They also show that the relationship between the foot/lower-limb symptoms and the neurological syndrome was recorded as uncertain. What the documents cannot decide is whether that combination represented reasonable working practice or premature crystallisation.

2. The Podiatry hinge

Podiatry may have been entirely reasonable. Obvious local foot pathology existed and later records preserve mechanical and neuropathic possibilities. The unresolved question is narrower: when Podiatry became the operative pathway for the feet, what happened to the neurological uncertainty already recorded as “Not clear”?

The controlling evidence gap remains the original referral and complete Podiatry pathway. Until those records are available, the repository should not assume either that Podiatry represented a reasonable adjunct or that it displaced neurological inquiry without justification.

3. Levodopa deferral and clarification

The record contains an articulated rationale for delaying and limiting levodopa exposure in a young person because of dyskinesia and dopamine-related complications. It also records that levodopa or another dopamine agonist might work very well, and later says the patient could be “very much better” if such treatment were used.

Counsel will require expert evidence on whether treatment deferral was reasonable in itself, whether the chosen strategy required an explicit reopening plan, and whether later treatment-state evidence should have altered the diagnostic or management frame.

4. The early treatment-response evidence

The short 2017 Procyclidine withdrawal episode is important because at least part of the foot/lower-limb phenomenon changed rapidly when neurological medication was stopped and improved again when treatment was resumed. That does not prove that a corn was neurological or that all foot pain was dystonia.

The expert question is what significance a movement-disorders neurologist would attach to that early pharmacological modifiability in the context of the unresolved 2017 lower-limb phenotype.

5. The longitudinal reopening threshold

The later record is mixed rather than linear. It contains foot sensitivity, cramping, lower-limb movements characterised as parkinsonian, OFF-related concerns, severe pain, benefit from toe supports and Lyrica, a period off Parkinson medication without a clear Parkinsonian difference, persistent toe curling, and repeated medication-timing effects.

The question is cumulative: at what point, if any, should the original unresolved relationship have been formally reconsidered, qualified, referred or tested differently?

6. The 2021 DBS information

In 2021 the patient was told that DBS was “only ever for the tremor element” and would not help anything else. The repository does not assume DBS was indicated for this patient at that time. The issue is whether the categorical explanation was an inaccurate general proposition, harmless shorthand for a sound patient-specific view, or evidence of an unduly narrowed advanced-therapy frame.

This requires independent movement-disorders evidence and, if available, any patient-specific DBS rationale not yet located.

7. March 2025 and the significance of the Sinemet CR response

The 10 March 2025 record expressly links severe morning foot pain to medication timing, predicts some further pain relief from increased Sinemet, and introduces controlled-release Sinemet at night. The later complaint response records “profound relief currently.”

The medical question is what mechanism best explains that response. The legal and limitation question is different: what did that event permit the patient reasonably to understand about the preceding years, and when did actionable knowledge arise?

8. PRKN and hindsight

The 2026 specialist result establishes compound heterozygous pathogenic Parkin mutations and attributes both Parkinsonism and dystonia to them. The early record had already raised PRKN/PARK2 as a possible cause, and by 2018 recorded it as what “we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause.”

The correct question is not whether clinicians should somehow have possessed the 2026 result in 2017. It is whether the earlier genetic/dystonic architecture had any significance for testing, referral, treatment strategy or later reconsideration.

9. Patient information and reliance

The patient was not undocumented or simply ignored. He was copied into long specialist letters, counselled about treatment, and given categorical information on issues including DBS. That makes the content of the information material.

Counsel should consider the relevance of reasonable reliance on patient-facing specialist explanations to consent, knowledge, limitation and the practical authority of the clinical record.

10. Governance and independent revalidation

QPS correspondence represented that the complaint would be reviewed through IMF/PAR/SIMT structures and described an independent incident-management process. SIMT later recorded no deficit in care.

The present materials do not disclose the complete PAR, source schedule, agenda, minutes or evidence pack. The appropriate proposition is therefore not that SIMT failed to review the case, but that independent proposition-level revalidation of the disputed foot/Podiatry hinge has not yet been demonstrated from the materials presently available.

11. Record accuracy, completion and later qualification

Historical clinical records can accurately record what a clinician believed at the time even if later evidence qualifies the interpretation. The data-protection issue is therefore not simply whether an old opinion is “wrong”. Counsel should test whether later information created any obligation of supplementation, contextualisation, restriction or downstream qualification under the applicable legal framework.

12. Causation and damage

Even if expert evidence identifies a departure from reasonable practice, causation remains separate. The record contains underlying disease progression, mixed foot mechanisms, treatment risks and periods of ambiguous medication response. Any damages case will need to distinguish injury attributable to an alleged failure of reconsideration or treatment timing from the natural history of PRKN-related Parkinsonism/dystonia and independent mechanical or neuropathic pathology.

13. Limitation and date of knowledge

The chronology contains an important adverse point for the claimant: by 19 March 2025 the formal complaint expressly linked a recent discovery of dopamine responsiveness to criticism of the preceding care. That is a serious candidate date for knowledge of a broad historical claim.

Later PRKN confirmation and later source discoveries may add proof or sharpen causation, but they should not automatically be treated as postponing limitation. Counsel should analyse the statutory test against what was reasonably ascertainable at each stage.

14. Documentary integrity and later retrospective accounts

Some later accounts simplify earlier uncertainty. The 2025 material includes categorical “idiopathic Parkinson’s disease” language despite earlier PRKN discussion, retrospective “we have known” formulations, collective references to departmental advice, and reliance on a cumulative discharge artefact.

These matters support credibility questions, not an allegation of dishonesty. The present evidence is better characterised as defensive retrospective reconstruction with some potentially material compression unless stronger state-of-mind evidence emerges.

15. Evidence still required

  • original 2017 Podiatry referral and complete Podiatry pathway;
  • complete PAR and source schedule/attachments;
  • SIMT agenda, minutes and circulation/source pack;
  • native EHR/discharge version history;
  • native Complex Case Meeting record and attendance/provenance;
  • any separate native Prof Dan Healy opinion or correspondence;
  • native LTI timing/processing material where available;
  • Article 18 operational/audit trail;
  • any separately held trainee/teaching records.

Pleading and presentation cautions

  • Do not plead this as a simple hindsight misdiagnosis case.
  • Do not say Parkinsonism was false; the record strongly supports genuine Parkinsonism.
  • Do not say all foot symptoms were dystonia.
  • Do not say Podiatry was necessarily wrong before the Podiatry record is obtained.
  • Do not say levodopa deferral was negligent without expert evidence.
  • Do not say DBS was necessarily indicated; test the accuracy and significance of the information supplied.
  • Do not back-project the 2026 PRKN result as knowledge available in 2017.
  • Do not convert institutional repetition into independent corroboration.
  • Do not allege concealment, dishonesty, conspiracy or bad faith on the present record.
The strongest expert question is not whether the patient “really had Parkinson’s”. It is what medically coherent explanation accounts for the repeated neurological treatment-state changes in the foot/lower-limb syndrome, in a patient later proven to have PRKN-caused Parkinsonism and dystonia, and when that evidence should have caused the original unresolved classification to be reconsidered.
