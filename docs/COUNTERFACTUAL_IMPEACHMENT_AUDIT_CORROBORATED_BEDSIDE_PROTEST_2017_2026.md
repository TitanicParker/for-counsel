# Counterfactual Impeachment Audit

## What happens to the case if the 10 July 2017 bedside protest is independently corroborated?

**Date:** 13 August 2026  
**Repository revision examined:** `e5bdbf2c5caff66ce82a6f3a40e65cb8ab63c135`  
**Baseline A:** [Management-Plan Invariance Audit, 2017–2026](MANAGEMENT_PLAN_INVARIANCE_AUDIT_2017_2026.md)  
**Counterfactual B:** one or more independent medical-student witnesses credibly corroborate the essential bedside event specified below.  
**Status:** counterfactual documentary, governance and source-reliability analysis. This is not a finding that corroboration has occurred. It is not a finding of negligence, dishonesty, causation or unlawful conduct.

---

## Executive finding

Independent corroboration would not merely add another clinical fact. It would change the evidential character of the foundational discharge document.

Baseline A established a strong case of path-dependent management from the record alone: July 2017 diagnostic uncertainty coexisted with a settled operational architecture; no global reopening rule was identified; later evidence repeatedly altered local treatment without producing an identified historical reconstruction; and by 2025 conscious interpretive accommodation was documented, although improper motive was not proved.

Counterfactual B adds one fact only:

> **Before the discharge account was completed or transmitted, the treating neurologist personally knew that the patient considered the central foot problem unresolved, rejected Podiatry as an adequate answer to the whole problem, and directly challenged the adequacy of discharge on that basis.**

Comparison with the finished discharge account would then prove a second, distinct proposition:

> **The durable document omitted that material challenge while recording `Not clear`, Podiatry, prolonged levodopa deferral, the clinician's explanatory framework and the patient's `good understanding`.**

The immediate evidential consequences would be:

1. the Podiatry disposition could no longer safely be described as uncontested or consensual;
2. `good understanding` could prove comprehension at most, not agreement, satisfaction or absence of protest;
3. the treating neurologist's actual knowledge of unresolved disagreement would move to **10 July 2017**, rather than being inferred only from later cumulative evidence;
4. the patient would have supplied a practical global reopening signal even though the formal plan contained no reopening rule;
5. the omission would become a plausible upstream mechanism by which later clinicians inherited an artificially stable account;
6. later medication-response evidence would read not merely as contradiction of an uncertain partition, but as confirmation of a concern expressly raised and omitted at inception;
7. the discharge summary would remain reliable for objective findings, tests, prescriptions and the fact of clinician decisions, but would be materially impeached as a neutral or complete narrative of patient position, decision process and consensual closure;
8. Model B—path-dependent management—would remain strongly supported, but would no longer be sufficient by itself to explain the **origin** of the inherited documentary frame;
9. the case would move materially toward Model C at an earlier date: actual knowledge of disagreement and a demonstrable omission in July 2017, conscious policy maintenance by April 2018, and conscious interpretive accommodation by 2025.

What corroboration would **not** prove is equally important. It would not prove that the omission was deliberate, that the neurologist understood the feet to be dystonia, that levodopa should have been started, that the patient's clinical interpretation was correct, or that later harm was caused by the omission. Those propositions require further evidence.

The strongest permissible adverse inference would be:

> **The foundational record materially understated known disagreement at the precise point where acknowledged uncertainty became operative partition, thereby making a contested management architecture appear more settled and consensual than the underlying event permitted.**

The still-premature inference would be:

> **The clinician deliberately falsified or suppressed the protest in order to mislead future clinicians or protect the management decision.**

## 1. Baseline A: the strongest record-only case

Before adding Counterfactual B, the record establishes:

- severe young-onset Parkinsonism, marked rigidity and bradykinesia;
- an abnormal gait with a footdrop-like appearance considered somewhat dystonic and expressly not true foot drop;
- genuine local foot pathology, including corns, calluses, pes planus and hammertoes;
- burning pain and an unresolved foot/movement relationship expressly recorded as `Not clear`;
- operative Podiatry allocation despite unresolved causation;
- deliberate, senior-linked levodopa/dopamine-agonist deferral for as long as possible;
- PRKN/PARK2 specifically contemplated;
- genetics described as usually not actively changing management;
- therapy caution expressed as applying `whatever` the genetic cause;
- no identified global reopening rule;
- the later Galway question to Healy: `Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa ... unless you'd advise otherwise?`;
- Healy's probable-PARK-II assessment, testing recommendation and relatively-low-dopamine-replacement formulation;
- Procyclidine withdrawal/restoration effects involving gait, tension and the disputed foot domain;
- affirmative continuation of the policy in April 2018 despite recognition of likely major levodopa benefit;
- later parkinsonian foot throw, sensitivity, toe curling, OFF dystonia, undertreatment, medication-state pain and profound Sinemet CR relief;
- repeated local updating without an identified global historical reconstruction.

Sources: [2017 discharge record](docs/source-records/discharge-summary.html); [Monaghan-to-Healy](docs/source-records/clinician-to-healy-2017-myself-and-tim.md); [Healy response](docs/source-records/healy-response-2017-09-12.md); [2017 treatment-response emails](patient-source/CONTEMPORANEOUS_TREATMENT_RESPONSE_EMAILS_2017.md); [longitudinal Neurology records](docs/source-records/README.md).

### 1.1 Strongest benign explanation under Baseline A

The July plan was a rational response to a very young patient with severe disease, real structural feet, uncertain causation and substantial future dyskinesia/impulse-control risk. Podiatry could appropriately treat actual corns and deformity. Genetics might reasonably have limited immediate therapeutic value where all available treatments were symptomatic. The clinicians investigated broadly, titrated cautiously, responded to toxicity, introduced levodopa when undertreatment and loss of Kemadrin required it, treated OFF states, escalated pregabalin and ultimately prescribed CR coverage for severe morning symptoms. A sincere mixed-mechanism view could accommodate both local pathology and dopaminergic pain modulation.

This is a substantial defence. Counterfactual B does not erase it.

### 1.2 Strongest path-dependence explanation under Baseline A

An initially defensible plan became self-stabilising. Diagnostic uncertainty remained open in language while management became settled in operation. New evidence was processed locally, but no event visibly reopened the original foot partition, Podiatry ownership, genetics neutrality, treatment threshold or historical account. The inherited chart supplied continuity and therefore amplified its own original assumptions.

This was the strongest overall model before corroboration.

## 2. Counterfactual B: the corroborated event and what it proves

For this audit only, assume one or more independent medical students credibly corroborate that:

- the patient asked the students to obtain the treating neurologist before discharge;
- the neurologist attended;
- the patient directly challenged discharge because the foot problem remained unresolved;
- he conveyed that the problem bringing him to Neurology had not been dealt with;
- he used the NCT/bald-tyres analogy or a recognisable equivalent;
- the neurologist's answer substantially relied on Podiatry;
- the exchange occurred before the final discharge document was completed or transmitted.

The witness proves the **event**, not its medical meaning.

### 2.1 Proposition 1—protest

The neurologist personally knew before discharge that:

- the patient regarded the feet as unresolved;
- Podiatry was not regarded by the patient as an adequate answer to the whole problem;
- the patient was challenging the adequacy of discharge;
- the operational partition was contested, not consensual;
- `Not clear` was a live problem requiring ownership, not merely an abstract differential.

The patient did not need to name dystonia, PRKN, neuropathy or levodopa. He communicated the clinically important proposition available from his knowledge state:

> **The present plan has not resolved the problem that brought me into Neurology.**

### 2.2 Proposition 2—omission

The final document can then be compared with a proved event. The document does not record the protest or the patient's rejection of Podiatry as a sufficient resolution.

Thus:

> **The witness proves the protest; the document comparison proves the omission.**

This is stronger than a patient's allegation that something was left out. It is a demonstrable discrepancy between the event and the durable narrative.

## 3. The omission as an independent documentary event

### 3.1 What the discharge account transmitted

The account transmitted to the GP, patient and future clinicians:

- the severe neurological phenotype;
- objective findings and investigations;
- the `Not clear` foot relationship;
- the possible separate/peripheral explanation;
- Podiatry as an operative disposition;
- prolonged levodopa deferral;
- genetics and PRKN reasoning;
- the treatment rationale;
- `good understanding` of why dopaminergic treatment was delayed;
- the clinicians' narrative of the case and plan.

### 3.2 What it omitted under Counterfactual B

It omitted:

> **The patient's direct, contemporaneous challenge to the adequacy of the unresolved-foot/Podiatry disposition and to discharge while that problem remained unresolved.**

### 3.3 Materiality

The omission would be material because the omitted event concerned:

- the central unresolved clinical domain;
- the service-allocation answer given to that domain;
- the adequacy of discharge;
- the patient's position concerning the plan;
- whether future clinicians should understand `Not clear` as passively tolerated or actively contested.

The omission would alter the apparent meaning of the document in four ways.

First, it would make Podiatry appear accepted when it was expressly challenged.

Second, it would permit `good understanding` to be read as harmony rather than simultaneous comprehension and disagreement.

Third, it would make the plan appear to have closed without a live dispute requiring follow-up.

Fourth, it would deprive later readers of the fact that the patient had already identified the very ownership/integration problem that later evidence repeatedly exposed.

The documentary effect is demonstrable. Clinical-outcome causation remains unproved.

## 4. `Good understanding` after corroboration

Under Counterfactual B, `good understanding` may still prove that the patient understood the clinician's explanation for delaying levodopa or dopamine agonists.

It cannot prove:

- agreement with that explanation;
- acceptance of the duration or consequences of deferral;
- acceptance of Podiatry as sufficient;
- satisfaction that the feet had been dealt with;
- agreement with a separate/peripheral causal partition;
- absence of protest;
- consensual closure of the admission problem.

Indeed, corroboration would demonstrate that understanding and disagreement coexisted.

That has an important evidential consequence:

> **A literally true statement of understanding could become affirmatively misleading when used as a complete account of patient position while a known, material disagreement was omitted.**

The phrase itself would not become false. Its unqualified use as evidence of acceptance would become unsafe.

## 5. The revised July 2017 state

Baseline A stated:

> **The documentary state was `Not clear`; the operative state was partition.**

Counterfactual B adds a third layer:

> **Documentary uncertainty + operative partition + explicit patient challenge to the partition.**

This changes the quality of the uncertainty. It was not merely a clinician's unresolved differential that could safely be carried forward in the background. It had immediate implications for discharge, ownership, communication and follow-up because the patient was expressly saying the allocation did not resolve the problem.

An accurate record intended for the GP, patient and future clinicians would reasonably be expected to show some combination of:

- that the patient remained concerned;
- that Podiatry was not regarded by him as resolving the entire issue;
- that the neurological relationship remained uncertain;
- what was explained in response;
- which specialty retained responsibility for integration;
- what follow-up or trigger would revisit the relationship.

No such documentary reconsideration is identified.

## 6. The patient supplied a practical global reopening signal

Baseline A found no formal global reopening rule. Under Counterfactual B, the patient supplied a human reopening signal in practice.

He did not propose a treatment. He challenged the sufficiency of the governing architecture:

> unresolved core problem → Podiatry offered → patient says this does not deal with the problem → discharge challenged.

A genuinely provisional architecture could have responded in several ways without agreeing with the patient:

- reassess before discharge;
- record the disagreement;
- explain why Podiatry remained appropriate and limited;
- state that Neurology retained responsibility for the unresolved relationship;
- identify a concrete review trigger;
- return the issue to the senior/complex-case structure;
- alter timing or focus of follow-up.

This audit does not prescribe which clinical response was required. It asks what evidence would exist if genuine reconsideration occurred.

Expected documentary evidence would include:

- a note of the challenge;
- a reasoned response to it;
- clarification of Podiatry's scope;
- an ownership/follow-up plan;
- or a statement that the plan was reconsidered and retained for specified reasons.

None is identified in the finished discharge account.

## 7. How much of benign path dependence survives?

Much of the clinical defence survives:

- treatment risk remains real;
- structural pathology remains real;
- Podiatry may remain appropriate;
- mixed mechanism remains plausible;
- the patient may understand the rationale while disagreeing;
- omission can occur without bad faith;
- later clinicians may innocently rely on the record they received.

What weakens substantially is the explanation that the foundational architecture arose and persisted solely through ordinary uncertainty, fragmented care or neutral documentation inheritance.

The continuity neurologist would no longer be merely unaware that the partition was contested. He would have actual personal knowledge before discharge.

The important distinction is:

> **Downstream inheritance may remain innocent even if the upstream inherited document was materially incomplete.**

Later clinicians could innocently treat Podiatry as accepted, `Not clear` as tolerable and later foot complaints as local developments because the foundational account did not disclose the challenge. The omission would therefore become a plausible upstream mechanism for later path dependence.

This does not prove that the omission caused every later decision. It proves that the informational baseline available to later readers differed materially from the underlying event.

## 8. Healy reconsidered under Counterfactual B

The revised sequence would be:

1. feet expressly `Not clear`;
2. patient directly contests unresolved feet and Podiatry before discharge;
3. challenge omitted from the foundational account;
4. Galway asks Healy for advice while disclosing its existing desire to hold levodopa back;
5. Healy says probable PARK II and recommends testing;
6. Healy describes relatively low dopamine replacement and avoidance of over-use;
7. diagnostic content is incorporated;
8. prolonged deferral survives.

The omission changes the informational context of Healy's role.

If the protest had been accurately recorded and transmitted, Healy or any later reader could have understood that:

- the lower-limb/foot relationship was not only clinically unresolved;
- its operational disposition was actively disputed;
- the patient considered it central to the admission;
- any specialist phenotype/treatment assessment potentially bore on that unresolved domain.

The current source does not establish what clinical material Healy actually received beyond the correspondence, imaging/video and review. It must not be assumed that he lacked all foot information or that he would have changed his conclusion.

The lost opportunity is narrower:

> **The foundational narrative did not preserve the patient-position fact that would have signalled to an external specialist that the existing allocation itself required examination.**

This makes the absence of an explicit Galway reconciliation after Healy more significant, not because Healy ordered levodopa, but because the external escape route operated on a documentary foundation that omitted a live challenge to the plan.

## 9. The Procyclidine event reconsidered

Under Baseline A, Procyclidine withdrawal/restoration was an early neurological signal contradicting an uncertain partition.

Under Counterfactual B, it becomes:

> **A neurological medication-response signal occurring shortly after the patient expressly challenged the partition that kept the feet operationally outside Neurology.**

The expected longitudinal synthesis would connect:

1. `Not clear`;
2. patient says the feet remain unresolved;
3. Podiatry offered as answer;
4. challenge omitted;
5. neurological medication withdrawal changes tension, gait and the disputed foot domain;
6. medication restoration/re-escalation produces major recovery.

The combination increases the event's required significance. The medication response is no longer merely new information. It is information responsive to a known, pre-existing dispute about the adequacy of the partition.

If no synthesis follows, benign explanations remain possible—documentation compression, clinical belief in mixed mechanisms, uncertainty about what Procyclidine response proved—but simple lack of awareness becomes substantially less plausible.

- the original `Not clear` relationship;

- Podiatry as the answer contested by the patient;

Baseline A found conscious policy maintenance. Counterfactual B strengthens that finding in a precise way:

- **dishonest motive:** not proved.

The earliest date for actual knowledge that the architecture was contested moves to 10 July 2017. The earliest strong documentary date for conscious maintenance in the face of materially contrary information remains April 2018, now with a much richer knowledge base.

## 12. Longitudinal effect of the omission

The omission did more than reduce completeness if the discharge account became the inherited baseline for later care and review.

It could have:

- made Podiatry appear accepted;
- made `Not clear` appear clinically tolerable rather than actively contested;
- converted a live ownership problem into background uncertainty;
- made later foot complaints appear separate, recurrent or new rather than continuation of an unresolved admission domain;
- deprived later clinicians of a reason to connect neurological foot evidence backward;
- allowed local treatment without historical reopening;
- strengthened the original architecture through repetition in later notes and reviews.

The demonstrable documentary effect is that later readers lacked a material patient-position fact.

The probable epistemic effect is that the record offered fewer reasons to question the original allocation.

The clinical causal effect—whether any clinician actually would have acted differently—remains counterfactual and requires evidence from those actors and independent experts.

## 14. The seed of its own impeachment

The document itself acknowledges:

- `Not clear`;
- structural and possible peripheral explanations;
- dystonic-looking gait;
- PRKN possibility;
- genetic uncertainty;
- genetics usually not changing management;
- Podiatry;
- prolonged levodopa deferral applying `whatever` the genetic cause.

Those admissions make the missing protest **more** material, not less.

If the document had declared the feet clearly separate on objective grounds, a patient's disagreement might still matter, but it would not expose an unresolved premise in the document's own reasoning. Here, the patient allegedly challenged the operational consequence of a relationship the document itself admitted was unresolved.

The document would therefore contain the seed of its own impeachment:

> **It acknowledges that the central relationship was unresolved, yet omits independent proof that the patient challenged converting that unresolved state into discharge plus Podiatry.**

The omission bears directly on the precise hinge the document leaves open.

## 15. Four possible explanations for the omission

### Explanation 1—ordinary documentation compression

**Supporting evidence**

- discharge documents cannot record every conversation;
- the record is already exceptionally long;
- clinicians may summarise decisions rather than interpersonal exchanges;
- Podiatry and `Not clear` were recorded, so the subject itself was not concealed.

**Contrary evidence**

- the omitted event directly challenged discharge and the central unresolved disposition;
- extensive counselling and patient-understanding language was included;
- recording assent-context while omitting material dissent creates asymmetry.

**Discriminating evidence**

- usual documentation practice;
- other contemporaneous progress notes;
- whether other objections were routinely recorded;
- clinician explanation of relevance and selection.

### Explanation 2—inadvertent omission

**Supporting evidence**

- high workload, delayed transcription and long admission documentation;
- no direct evidence of a decision to omit;
- the exchange may not have been remembered at finalisation.

**Contrary evidence**

- event was direct, vivid, proximate to discharge and involved a challenge to the plan;
- document includes detailed patient counselling and position claims.

**Discriminating evidence**

- drafting/version history;
- contemporaneous ward notes;
- testimony about when and how the summary was compiled;
- whether the exchange appeared in any precursor document.

### Explanation 3—belief that the protest was clinically immaterial because Podiatry was arranged

**Supporting evidence**

- genuine structural pathology made Podiatry reasonable;
- clinician may have regarded the objection as dissatisfaction rather than new clinical information;
- the neurological plan and Podiatry could have been considered complementary.

**Contrary evidence**

- the record itself says `Not clear`;
- patient challenged Podiatry as insufficient to resolve the problem;
- no retained-Neurology ownership or review trigger is documented.

**Discriminating evidence**

- clinician testimony;
- actual Podiatry referral scope;
- whether Neurology intended to revisit the feet;
- subsequent handling of Podiatry failure/worsening.

### Explanation 4—conscious decision not to record a challenge that complicated the narrative

**Supporting evidence**

- inclusion of `good understanding` and detailed clinician reasoning alongside omission of direct dissent;
- omission makes the plan appear more stable and consensual;
- later preservation of the architecture despite contrary evidence;
- later defensive narrative may increase the significance of the original omission.

**Contrary evidence**

- no direct evidence of intent;
- long document openly records uncertainty and possible future regret;
- clinician later disclosed extensive records and acknowledged the patient's experience;
- omission can arise without self-protective purpose.

**Discriminating evidence**

- drafts, version history and internal communications;
- testimony from clinician, students and staff;
- whether the protest was discussed elsewhere;
- evidence of deliberate selection or alteration;
- governance source packs and whether the omission was later recognised.

No explanation can presently be selected solely from Counterfactual B. Explanation 4 becomes a legitimate hypothesis; it does not become the default conclusion.

## 17. Re-ranking the three models

### Model A—ordinary adaptive care

**What survives**

- rational treatment caution;
- real structural feet;
- possible mixed pain mechanisms;
- extensive investigation and treatment adaptation;
- no automatic duty to accept the patient's causal view;
- plausible inadvertent or compressed documentation.

**What weakens**

- the plan can no longer be portrayed as unchallenged;
- `good understanding` cannot support acceptance;
- simple lack of awareness that Podiatry was inadequate for the patient is defeated;
- later neutral inheritance begins from an incomplete document.

**Confidence:** **Moderate for clinical decisions in isolation; low as a complete account of the documentary chronology.**

**Strongest innocent explanation:** clinician reasonably believed Podiatry and Neurology were complementary, considered the protest clinically non-dispositive, and omitted it through compression or inadvertence.

**Strongest adverse inference:** care responded to symptoms, but the record excluded dissent that would have forced the plan to remain visibly provisional.

**Deciding evidence:** clinical expert opinion, Podiatry scope, drafts, witness detail, clinician explanation and contemporaneous ownership plan.

### Model B—path-dependent management

**What survives**

- it remains the strongest description of longitudinal treatment and documentation;
- downstream actors may innocently inherit and reproduce the foundational account;
- ordinary cognitive and institutional mechanisms can still explain much of the later course.

**What strengthens**

- a concrete upstream mechanism appears: the inherited record omitted the foundational challenge;
- documentary path dependence is no longer merely passive repetition of an uncertain note; it begins with loss of material conflict.

**What weakens**

- Model B alone does not explain why the originating clinician's document omitted known disagreement;
- purely innocent inheritance cannot explain the upstream discrepancy without an additional omission account.

**Confidence:** **Very high as the overall longitudinal mechanism.**

**Strongest innocent explanation:** upstream inadvertence/clinical-immateriality judgment followed by innocent downstream inheritance.

**Strongest adverse inference:** the architecture became self-stabilising partly because the originating document stripped it of the fact that it was contested.

**Deciding evidence:** origin and drafting evidence plus proof of what later decision-makers actually reviewed.

### Model C—knowing frame preservation

**What strengthens**

- actual knowledge of disagreement is proved in July 2017;
- material omission is demonstrable;
- later contradictory evidence responds to the exact domain contested;
- April 2018 conscious maintenance occurs against known prior objection;
- 2025 accommodation fits a longer sequence of preserving meanings.

**What remains unproved**

- that the omission was purposive;
- that the clinician recognised the protest as medically valid;
- that later interpretations were selected to protect professional interests;
- dishonest motive.

**Confidence:** **Moderate as an explanation beginning with conscious non-integration; high for conscious accommodation by 2025; low for deliberate deception.**

**Strongest innocent explanation:** conscious clinical disagreement with the patient's view, sincere belief in Podiatry/mixed causation, and non-culpable omission.

**Strongest adverse inference:** the clinician knew the architecture was contested, omitted the contest, and repeatedly assigned later facts meanings that preserved the architecture.

**Deciding evidence:** drafts, communications, explanations contemporaneous with the event, later use of the summary, governance source selection and independent expert evidence.

### Revised ranking

1. **Model B remains the strongest whole-course mechanism, now with a proved upstream documentary defect under Counterfactual B.**
2. **Model C moves materially closer and begins earlier: actual knowledge in July 2017, conscious maintenance/non-integration strongly supported by April 2018, conscious accommodation proved by 2025.**
3. **Model A remains substantial for clinical rationality and innocent omission, but can no longer explain the documentary course without confronting the material discrepancy.**

Independent proof does not make Model C globally proven. It makes Model B alone insufficient to explain the origin of the frame and requires an account of knowing disagreement plus omission.

## 18. The accurate-document counterfactual

A neutral entry could have said:

> **Patient remains concerned that the foot symptoms which prompted admission have not been adequately explained and does not regard Podiatry alone as resolving this issue. The relationship to Parkinsonism remains uncertain. This concern and the proposed plan were discussed before discharge.**

This wording would not concede that the patient was medically right, that Podiatry was inappropriate or that discharge was unsafe. It would preserve the existence of the disagreement.

Had that proposition appeared:

- **Healy** would be read as reviewing a case with a contested unresolved lower-limb allocation;
- **Procyclidine withdrawal** would be seen as early evidence responsive to the recorded concern;
- **April 2018** would require visible reconciliation of the concern, Healy and treatment response;
- **2020** parkinsonian foot throw would be an explicit reopening event;
- **2023** OFF/undertreatment findings would be continuation of the recorded unresolved question;
- **2025** Sinemet/CR benefit would demand comparison with the original contested partition;
- **PRKN confirmation** would be read against a preserved history of unresolved neurological ownership rather than a seemingly accepted Podiatry disposition.

The treatment might or might not have changed. The evidential meaning and the questions future clinicians were prompted to ask would have changed substantially.

## 19. Three harms to record integrity

### 19.1 Epistemic harm

Future clinicians would be deprived of knowing that:

- the patient identified the feet as unresolved before discharge;
- he did not consider Podiatry sufficient;
- the allocation was actively contested;
- later neurological foot evidence had a direct antecedent dispute.

This could affect interpretation. It does not prove altered treatment outcome.

### 19.2 Governance harm

Later reviewers could assess the 2017 plan as if:

- the patient understood and did not object;
- Podiatry was a settled disposition;
- the original uncertainty was carried forward without a contemporaneous challenge;
- later complaint represented retrospective dissatisfaction rather than an omitted initial objection.

Any PAR/SIMT review relying on the discharge account without the corroborated event would begin from an incomplete decision-process record. Its conclusion would require revalidation; it would not automatically become invalid.

### 19.3 Narrative harm

The omission would alter the apparent relationship among patient, clinician and plan:

- clinician as comprehensive narrator;
- patient as understanding recipient;
- Podiatry as accepted solution;
- uncertainty as peacefully managed;
- later conflict as subsequent rupture.

The corroborated event would show instead that the conflict existed at inception and was absent from the durable story.

## 21. Direct answers to the fifteen final questions

### 1. How flawed is the longitudinal record on its face?

Materially vulnerable as a self-sufficient causal narrative, but reliable for many direct clinical facts. On its face it supports path dependence and local rather than global updating; it does not prove a foundational omission.

### 2. Strongest benign explanation under Baseline A?

Reasonable caution in an exceptionally young patient, genuine mixed structural/neurological pathology, sincere belief that Podiatry and Neurology were complementary, active symptom management and ordinary cognitive/documentary path dependence.

### 3. What changes immediately if one independent student corroborates?

The event occurred; the neurologist had actual knowledge of unresolved disagreement; Podiatry was contested; the challenge preceded final documentation; and comparison proves a material omission. Weight depends on witness quality and precision.

### 4. What if all four independently corroborate?

If genuinely independent, consistent and uncontaminated, multiple corroboration would greatly reduce mistaken-memory and reconstruction explanations, strengthen detail and timing, and make the event exceptionally difficult to dispute. It still would not itself prove why the clinician omitted it.

### 5. Does corroboration transform omission into demonstrable material omission?

Yes. Witness evidence proves the event; the final document proves non-recording; relevance to the central `Not clear`/Podiatry/discharge decision establishes materiality.

### 6. What happens to `good understanding`?

It narrows to possible comprehension of the explanation. It cannot prove agreement, acceptance, satisfaction or absence of protest. Used without the protest as a complete patient-position account, it risks being affirmatively misleading.

### 7. What happens to accepted Podiatry disposition?

The proposition is defeated. Podiatry may remain clinically reasonable, but it was not accepted by the patient as resolving the whole problem.

### 8. Could the neurologist merely have failed to appreciate that the partition was contested?

No, not at the level of awareness that the patient contested it. He might still have failed innocently to appreciate the medical significance of the contest or considered it clinically immaterial.

### 9. Does the earliest date for knowledge/conscious maintenance move?

Actual knowledge of disagreement moves to 10 July 2017. Strong conscious maintenance in the face of subsequent contrary clinical evidence remains clearest in April 2018, but is materially strengthened by the earlier knowledge.

### 10. Does omission provide an upstream mechanism for later reproduction?

Yes. Later clinicians could innocently inherit a record that had already converted contested uncertainty into apparently settled allocation. Causal effect on treatment remains to be proved.

### 11. What remains reliable and what becomes unsafe?

Objective findings, tests, prescriptions and occurrence of decisions remain largely reliable. Patient-position claims, implications of agreement, completeness of deliberation, neutrality of the management narrative and derivative summaries become unsafe without qualification.

### 12. Does ranking change?

Model B remains strongest overall but now contains a demonstrable upstream documentary defect. Model C strengthens materially and begins earlier. Model A survives as a clinical and innocent-omission explanation but loses force as a complete documentary explanation.

### 13. What adverse inference is justified?

The discharge account materially understated known disagreement and made the 2017 architecture appear more settled and consensual than the event warranted. Later accounts inheriting it require revalidation.

### 14. What adverse inference remains premature?

That the omission was deliberately designed to deceive, conceal error, protect clinicians or cause later actors to withhold care.

### 15. What more is needed before alleging deliberate dishonesty?

Evidence of intent or recklessness as to misleading effect, such as drafts/version history, internal communications, testimony showing a decision to exclude the protest, inconsistent explanations, proof the event was deliberately withheld from reviewers, or a pattern of selective alteration that cannot reasonably be explained by compression, inadvertence or clinical relevance judgments.

## Required final findings

### A. Record-only conclusion

If the protest remains uncorroborated patient-source evidence, the strongest conclusion remains Baseline A:

> **The July 2017 plan was an intelligible initial response that became a durable, path-dependent architecture. Later evidence repeatedly governed local treatment but did not visibly govern global historical reconstruction. Conscious interpretive accommodation is demonstrable by 2025; knowing self-protective preservation and improper motive are not proved. The discharge account is materially vulnerable as a self-sufficient narrative but not demonstrably impeached by an independently proved omission.**

### B. Corroborated-protest conclusion

If one or more independent witnesses credibly corroborate the essential protest:

> **The treating neurologist had actual pre-discharge knowledge that the patient regarded the central foot problem as unresolved, rejected Podiatry as a sufficient answer and challenged discharge on that basis. The finished document nevertheless recorded `Not clear`, Podiatry, the treatment architecture and `good understanding` without preserving the material disagreement. That comparison establishes a material documentary omission at the foundational hinge. It strengthens conscious non-integration from April 2018, supplies a plausible upstream mechanism for later path dependence and moves the case materially toward knowing frame preservation at an earlier date—without proving deliberate deception or improper motive.**

Independent corroboration would not merely add another fact. It would change the evidential character of the foundational discharge document itself.

The document would remain evidence of clinical findings, tests, prescriptions and the clinician's reasoning. It could no longer safely operate as neutral or complete proof of the patient's position, consensual allocation, adequacy of the recorded decision process or absence of contemporaneous challenge.

If the foundational document is materially impeached at the point where `Not clear` became operative partition, later clinical and governance accounts do not automatically become false or invalid. But any account that inherited the document without knowledge of the omitted challenge loses the benefit of treating that foundation as complete. It must be re-examined proposition by proposition against the corrected information state.

The governing conclusion is:

> **Downstream reliance may have been innocent. It was nevertheless reliance on an informational starting point that, under Counterfactual B, materially understated known conflict. Repetition cannot convert that incompleteness into independent corroboration, and governance cannot safely validate the architecture without first restoring the omitted challenge to the record it evaluates.**
