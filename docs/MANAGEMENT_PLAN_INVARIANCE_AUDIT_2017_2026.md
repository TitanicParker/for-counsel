# Management-Plan Invariance Audit, 2017–2026

## Did the management plan govern the evidence, or did the evidence govern the management plan?

**Date:** 13 August 2026  
**Repository revision examined:** `e5bdbf2c5caff66ce82a6f3a40e65cb8ab63c135`  
**Status:** documentary, clinical-governance and source-genealogy audit for counsel and independent expert review. It is not a clinical diagnosis, pleading, expert opinion or finding of negligence, dishonesty or improper motive.

---

## Executive finding

The July 2017 record contains two different levels of certainty:

- substantial uncertainty about aetiology, genetics and the relationship between the feet and the movement disorder; but
- a substantially settled operational plan: treat the recognised Parkinsonism initially with Rasagiline, Procyclidine and other symptomatic/supportive measures; route the feet to Podiatry; and defer levodopa/dopamine agonists for as long as possible because of anticipated long-term complications in an exceptionally young patient.

The management plan was not entirely immutable. Drugs were introduced, stopped, replaced, escalated and timed differently. Levodopa eventually began in 2021. Stalevo was used for OFF-related symptoms. Sinemet CR was prescribed in 2025 for the severe morning state. These are substantial examples of patient-responsive care.

But they are principally **local updates**. No identified event produced a **global revision** that expressly reconnected the historical feet to the neurological syndrome, reconsidered the Podiatry allocation, adjudicated the peripheral-neuropathy hypothesis, integrated the Procyclidine response, revisited the genetic-management premise, or reconstructed the legitimacy of the decisions built on the original partition.

The strongest fit is therefore:

> **Model B—path-dependent management—with a later transition toward Model C's narrower, provable element: conscious interpretive preservation by 2025.**

That does not mean dishonest preservation. It means that by 2025 the treating neurologist expressly knew that dopaminergic treatment altered the feet and consciously interpreted that fact as modulation of pain `of whatever cause`, an interpretation that permitted treatment change without requiring historical reclassification.

The central documentary pattern is:

> **Diagnostic uncertainty remained open in language, but management was sufficiently closed that new evidence could alter care within the architecture without visibly altering the architecture itself.**

No general reopening rule is identified in the discharge summary. The one explicit external escape clause—`unless you'd advise otherwise` in Monaghan's question to Healy—was followed by an opinion that materially strengthened PARK2 and described cautious low-dose dopamine replacement rather than indefinite avoidance. The diagnostic part was incorporated; no visible reconsideration of the prolonged-deferral policy followed.

The final answer is therefore:

> **The management plan began as an intelligible provisional response to a difficult young-onset case, but the record does not show it remaining globally answerable to the patient's evolving condition. It became a durable architecture within which later diagnostic, genetic, foot-related and medication-response evidence was principally interpreted and accommodated. New evidence governed local treatment increasingly; the inherited plan governed what that evidence was allowed to mean historically.**

## 1. The July 2017 management plan, reconstructed only from contemporaneous material

### 1.1 The working clinical state

| Question | July 2017 documentary position |
|---|---|
| Working neurological diagnosis | Severe young-onset Parkinsonism; `likely an IPD albeit with atypical features`; DaTSCAN later abnormal as expected |
| Aetiological differential | Idiopathic/synuclein-based disease, autosomal-recessive/genetic Parkinsonism, PRKN/PARK2, other monogenic conditions, Parkinson-plus disorders, metabolic/toxic possibilities |
| PRKN/PARK2 | A live and patient-specific possibility, based on very young onset, akinetic rigidity, symmetry, absence of dominant family history and preserved cognition—not a confirmed diagnosis |
| Feet | Real corns, calluses, pes planus, hammertoes, burning and pain alongside a dystonic-looking lower-limb gait |
| Foot/neurological relationship | May be separate or perhaps peripheral neuropathy; expressly `Not clear` |
| Peripheral neuropathy | Hypothesis, not established diagnosis; alcohol raised as one possible contributor |
| Treatment-risk premise | Very young patients may develop dyskinesia, impulse-control problems and other dopaminergic complications earlier or more severely |

Sources: [discharge/extended clinical record](docs/source-records/discharge-summary.html); [GP Podiatry note](docs/source-records/gp-podiatry-2017-07-06.md).

### 1.2 The operational plan before discharge

The contemporaneous record establishes the following management architecture:

1. **Start or continue lower-level symptomatic strategies**
   - Clonazepam at night for tension;
   - Rasagiline/Azilect, intended long-term even if no obvious benefit;
   - exceptionally slow Procyclidine/Kemadrin titration;
   - possible Amantadine later;
   - CoQ10 and supportive measures.

2. **Defer conventional dopamine replacement/agonist treatment**
   - Sinemet/Madopar and synthetic dopamine agonists were acknowledged as ordinary options;
   - `the greatest of care` was advised with the senior consultant and Neurology colleagues;
   - `We were likely [to] try and avoid these for as long as possible`;
   - later in the same document: `we will try to leave off on this for as long as possible`.

3. **Allocate structural/local foot management to Podiatry**
   - outpatient Chiropody/Podiatry requested;
   - an SHO had tried to arrange this in hospital;
   - on 6 July the GP recorded Podiatry priority `as Parkinson's`.

4. **Continue diagnostic work without committing management to its result**
   - DaTSCAN, imaging, laboratory investigations and neurogenetic consideration;
   - possible second opinion;
   - firm genetic diagnosis described as usually not actively changing management.

5. **Follow longitudinally**
   - Parkinson's nurse contact for interim issues;
   - gradual Procyclidine escalation;
   - a further conversation after DaTSCAN;
   - Neurology clinic follow-up in nine months.

### 1.3 What was the “management” expected not to change after a firm genetic diagnosis?

The controlling page-16 passage states:

> **`One of the chief difficulties with this is the question of whether a firm genetic diagnosis will actually actively change management? This question is (in the majority of cases) usually answered in the negative.`**

The document then identifies PRKN/PARK2 as specifically plausible in this patient and acknowledges that genetic knowledge might assist understanding of neuropathology, prognosis and **`Levodopa sensitivities?`** Yet the immediately following Therapy section says:

> **`Whatever form and whatever genetic cause is the cause`**, ordinary levodopa or dopamine agonists would be approached with extreme caution, and **`we will try to leave off on this for as long as possible.`**

The “management” was therefore not an abstract healthcare plan. On the face of the document it comprised:

- prolonged avoidance of levodopa/dopamine agonists;
- use of alternative symptomatic agents first;
- structural/local handling of the feet through Podiatry;
- supportive multidisciplinary care;
- continued investigation and follow-up without making diagnostic resolution a necessary condition of treatment revision.

The genetic paragraph and Therapy paragraph together establish an early **presumption of management invariance**: a genetic result might refine explanatory knowledge and levodopa-risk understanding, but the treatment architecture was drafted to remain substantially the same `whatever` the genetic cause.

This does not prove the plan was medically wrong. It proves the plan was already sufficiently established that the first patient/GP account treated genetic clarification as presumptively non-governing.

## 2. Diagnostic uncertainty versus management uncertainty

| Diagnostic/causal state | Management state |
|---|---|
| Severe Parkinsonism established; exact form uncertain | Parkinsonism treated symptomatically through Rasagiline, Procyclidine and Clonazepam |
| Idiopathic versus atypical/genetic disease unresolved | Ordinary levodopa/dopamine agonists deferred for as long as possible |
| PRKN/PARK2 specifically plausible but unconfirmed | Management expected usually not to change even after firm genetic diagnosis |
| Structural disease and neurological lower-limb signs coexist | Podiatry operationalised for the feet |
| Feet may be separate, peripheral or related: `Not clear` | No corresponding uncertainty about whether Podiatry should proceed |
| Peripheral neuropathy only possible | No documented objective neuropathy-confirmation plan tied to responsibility allocation |
| Future phenotype and medication response unknown | No general prospective criteria specify what would reopen the global plan |

The answer is therefore yes:

> **`Not clear` was an unresolved causal question inside an otherwise substantially settled operational architecture.**

The plan preserved uncertainty descriptively while acting on separation administratively. This is the original asymmetry:

> **The documentary state was `Not clear`; the operative state was partition.**

### 3.2 Rules not identified

No documented global reopening rule was identified specifying that management would be reconsidered if:

- PRKN/PARK2 was confirmed or became substantially more likely;
- Healy described a different cautious-treatment model;
- Podiatry failed, ended or worsened symptoms;
- neurological medication materially changed the feet;
- Procyclidine withdrawal affected the original foot site;
- dystonia became clearer;
- symptoms deteriorated or disability became intolerable;
- levodopa benefit became more likely;
- functional or psychological harm increased;
- the patient challenged the Podiatry allocation;
- peripheral neuropathy lacked objective support;
- the foot/movement relationship became less `Not clear`.

> **NO DOCUMENTED GLOBAL REOPENING RULE IDENTIFIED.**

The discharge architecture contains careful titration and monitoring rules inside the selected plan. It does not state what evidence would return the selected plan itself to an open state.

The phrase `sooner or later` is not a reopening rule. It predicts eventual use without defining a threshold. The nine-month follow-up is not a reopening rule. It schedules contact without identifying what propositions must be reconsidered.

Accordingly, on the face of the discharge architecture, no specified evidence was capable of making `Not clear` more clear **and requiring management change**.

## 4. The Counihan–Monaghan–Healy test

### 4.1 The pre-Healy Galway position

Monaghan wrote:

> **`Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?`**

([Monaghan-to-Healy correspondence](docs/source-records/clinician-to-healy-2017-myself-and-tim.md))

The source directly establishes:

1. an existing Galway policy of prolonged levodopa deferral;
2. a collective/consultative dimension involving `Tim`—identified as Counihan through later Monaghan material, not by the contemporaneous page alone;
3. an express request for Healy's treatment advice, not merely genetic laboratory assistance;
4. theoretical defeasibility: `unless you'd advise otherwise` made Healy capable of reopening the policy.

### 4.2 Healy's actual response

After examining the patient, Healy:

- said he **probably had PARK II**;
- recommended testing PARK II initially;
- recorded dramatic motor improvement on low-dose Procyclidine and Rasagiline;
- stated that PARK II patients tend to manage well for long periods with **relatively low doses of dopamine replacement therapy**;
- warned that they can become dyskinetic if **over-used**.

([Healy response](docs/source-records/healy-response-2017-09-12.md))

Healy did not order immediate levodopa. His wording can support caution. But it does not expressly endorse `hold back insofar as possible`. It describes controlled use and warns against over-use.

These are distinguishable policies:

| Galway question | Healy formulation |
|---|---|
| Hold levodopa back insofar as possible | PARK II patients may manage for long periods on relatively low dopamine replacement |
| Avoid exposure | Use relatively low exposure and avoid over-use |
| Duration defined by possibility of continued withholding | Duration defined by adequate management under restrained replacement |

Whether this difference was clinically material requires expert interpretation. Documentary exactness nevertheless requires the conclusion:

> **Healy did not endorse immediate levodopa, but neither did he expressly endorse Galway's prolonged non-use formulation. He described a materially distinguishable cautious-use model.**

If this did not amount to `advise otherwise` enough to trigger reconsideration, the record does not identify what would have.

## 5. How Healy's opinion was processed

Healy's opinion contained two separable forms of content.

| Healy content | Later Galway treatment |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic/genetic: `probably has Park II`; test it first | Incorporated: April 2018 refers to PARK2/PRKN as what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause` |
| Management: long periods on relatively low dopamine replacement; dyskinesia if over-used | No identified note explicitly compares this with `hold back insofar as possible` or alters the pre-existing prolonged-deferral policy |

By April 2018, Galway accepted Healy's diagnostic authority sufficiently to invoke him in the suspected cause. In the same note, Monaghan stated that levodopa could make the patient `very much better` but **`we do not want to do that – at least for quite some time`**.

This is evidence of selective effect, even if not selective attention:

- the diagnostic content entered the explanatory record;
- the potential management implication did not visibly reopen the treatment rule.

The chronology therefore supports:

> **The pre-existing Galway plan governed the practical interpretation of Healy's opinion more strongly than Healy's opinion governed the plan.**

That conclusion could be defeated by a contemporaneous reconciliation showing that Galway considered Healy's wording, concluded it supported continued deferral on patient-specific grounds, and set a review threshold. No such record has been identified.

## 6. April 2018: the major stress test

The April 2018 note must be read as one knowledge state. Monaghan simultaneously knew or recorded:

- Healy's probable-PARK-II position;
- PARK2/PRKN as what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause`;
- a two-week Procyclidine withdrawal because of blurred vision;
- rapid return/change at the outside-left-foot/corn site;
- broader worsening and the patient becoming more `shifty` and `unrelaxed`;
- substantial relief of tension and improved function on neurological medication;
- that levodopa or another dopamine agonist could make the patient `very much better`;
- that **`we do not want to do that – at least for quite some time`**;
- that genetics did not particularly change current management, so there was `no great loss` in not knowing then.

([2018 Neurology record](docs/source-records/neurology-notes-2018.html))

The Procyclidine event did not prove that the corn itself was neurological, that every foot pain was dystonia or that levodopa would have produced the same effect. It did establish neurological pharmacological modifiability in the disputed foot/lower-limb domain.

By April 2018 this was no longer merely untouched uncertainty. The clinician expressly recorded new information, expressly recognised possible major levodopa benefit and affirmatively restated the existing policy.

The defensible documentary conclusion is:

> **April 2018 shows conscious maintenance of a pre-existing policy in the presence of information capable of challenging its assumptions.**

This is not proof of improper motive or professional breach. It is stronger than `the issue may not have been noticed`.

## 7. The genetics-invariance test

### 7.1 Chronology

1. **July 2017:** firm genetic diagnosis usually expected not actively to change management.
2. **July 2017:** PRKN/PARK2 already specifically plausible.
3. **September 2017:** Healy says probable PARK II and recommends testing it first.
4. **April 2018:** PARK2/PRKN is what `we/Prof Healy suspect`; knowing still said not particularly to change current management; `no great loss` in not knowing.
5. **March 2025:** treating response states a belief in idiopathic Parkinson's disease; no identified bridge explains the movement away from probable/suspected PARK2.
6. **February 2026:** two pathogenic Parkin variants confirm that Parkinsonism and dystonia are due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.

([discharge record](docs/source-records/discharge-summary.html); [Healy response](docs/source-records/healy-response-2017-09-12.md); [2018 record](docs/source-records/neurology-notes-2018.html); [official rebuttal](docs/source-records/official-rebuttal.html); [PRKN result](docs/source-records/genetics-prkn-2026-02-04.md))

### 7.2 Circularity

The record supports the following potential loop:

> PRKN suspected → confirmation treated as management-neutral → testing deferred → existing policy survives → neurological treatment evidence accumulates → no global reopening identified → diagnostic uncertainty persists → unchanged management remains explainable within the unresolved state.

There are clinically coherent facts that partly break the loop:

- in 2017 genetic testing had limitations;
- no treatment was proved disease-modifying;
- the principal available treatments were symptomatic;
- young age and dyskinesia sensitivity supplied a real reason for caution regardless of genotype;
- later actual dyskinesia supported the reality of that risk.

Those points explain why genotype might not dictate a different drug automatically. They do not fully break the governance circle, because the discharge record itself said genetic knowledge might aid understanding of levodopa sensitivity, while Healy's probable-PARK-II opinion supplied a patient-specific treatment description. Even if the same medicine remained appropriate, the result could have changed dose philosophy, phenotype attribution, counselling, monitoring, causal integration and the threshold for reconsideration.

The safe conclusion is:

> **The record contains a rational reason why genetics need not mechanically dictate treatment, but no adequate documentary bridge showing why increasing PRKN confidence had no material effect on the broader management architecture.**

## 10. The reported protest as a challenge to the plan

The patient reports that on 10 July 2017, before expected discharge, he asked four medical students to summon the neurologist, used the analogy of receiving a full NCT but being sent away on bald tyres, and challenged discharge with the feet unresolved. He recalls receiving a Podiatry response. ([patient statement log](patient-source/PATIENT_STATEMENT_LOG.md))

This is first-person patient evidence, not independent proof that the exchange occurred. The four students have not been independently identified and interviewed in the repository.

### If the protest occurred

It was a direct pre-discharge challenge to the adequacy of the architecture: the patient was saying that the problem central to his lived presentation remained unresolved and that Podiatry did not answer it adequately.

The final record preserves:

- `Not clear`;
- Podiatry;
- prolonged levodopa deferral;
- `good understanding` of the treatment rationale;

but not the alleged opposition to the adequacy of that arrangement.

The effect would be to make a contested plan appear unchallenged and consensually understood. `Good understanding` cannot safely be expanded into agreement with causal classification or service allocation.

### If the protest cannot be proved

The central case remains:

- contemporaneous `Not clear` plus operative Podiatry;
- no documented global reopening rule;
- Healy's probable-PARK-II opinion;
- Procyclidine foot-domain response known by April 2018;
- affirmative continuation of the treatment policy;
- later parkinsonian/OFF/dystonic and dopaminergic-response evidence;
- no identified global revision.

The protest would sharpen the foreclosure case, but the invariance case does not depend on it.

## 11. What would have defeated the original plan?

### 11.1 The strongest defence

The plan had genuine adaptive thresholds at the medication level:

- intolerance could stop or replace a drug;
- lack of obvious Procyclidine benefit could trigger a test stop;
- eventual clinical need could bring levodopa in;
- dyskinesia could limit escalation;
- OFF symptoms could change timing or formulation;
- severe morning symptoms could justify controlled release.

The patient's later course activated several of those implicit clinical thresholds. Levodopa was introduced when he felt undertreated and Kemadrin had to be removed. Stalevo was used for OFF-related symptoms. CR targeted the overnight gap.

### 11.2 No identified falsification conditions for global propositions

| Proposition | Evidence apparently sufficient to change it | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Start levodopa earlier | No prospective threshold documented; eventual undertreatment/medication substitution led to 2021 start | **Implicit threshold only** |
| Reconsider Podiatry | No failure, worsening or endpoint rule documented | **No falsification condition identified** |
| Recognise neurological foot component | Recognition occurred repeatedly, but no rule linked recognition to historical reclassification | **Locally accepted; global consequence unspecified** |
| Downgrade peripheral neuropathy | No stated objective or response criterion | **No falsification condition identified** |
| Prioritise PRKN testing | Patient preference could trigger testing; management significance discounted | **Preference rule, not management-reopening rule** |
| Alter diagnosis | No documented bridge from probable PARK2 to IPD or rule for revising either | **No clear criterion identified** |
| Revisit discharge interpretation | None identified | **No falsification condition identified** |

The original plan could be defeated locally by intolerance, undertreatment and symptom timing. Its historical causal architecture had no visible falsification rule.

That is why later evidence could produce more medication without producing more historical clarity.

## 12. The plan through 2020, 2023 and 2025

### 12.1 2020: neurological recognition without repartition

The continuity neurologist described a left lower-limb `throw` and expressly classified it as parkinsonian because dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion power were normal. He also recorded exquisite foot sensitivity, excessive movement and an urge to move the feet. A local corn improved with bunion covers—real evidence supporting a structural component. ([2020 Neurology record](docs/source-records/neurology-notes-2020.html))

This made the model more clearly mixed and more neurologically integrated. The record identifies no reconsideration of the original Podiatry partition.

### 12.2 2023: the partition becomes a live clinical problem

The record contains:

- severe bilateral burning pain and extreme distress;
- pregabalin escalation from 25 mg twice daily to 600 mg/day, yet ongoing debilitating pain;
- toe clawing/cramping and OFF dystonia reasoning;
- possible insufficient levodopa;
- Stalevo facilitating evening activity and being experienced as helping pain;
- clinician-patient `misalignment` about Parkinsonism versus a separate foot problem;
- pain and shuffling worse when Sinemet was absent;
- a nurse assessment, after discussion with Monaghan, that the patient appeared undertreated and pain/shuffling were OFF-related;
- Stalevo initiation in response.

([2023 Neurology record](docs/source-records/neurology-notes-2023.html))

Management responded. It did not remain indifferent to the patient. But it responded by treating new manifestations within the inherited categories. No global note reconnects this material to the 2017 `Not clear` partition and Procyclidine event.

By this point passive non-recognition is no longer an adequate default explanation. The same continuity clinician possessed enough mutually reinforcing information to know that the historical relationship required explicit adjudication.

### 12.3 2025: acknowledged relationship, therapeutic use, historical containment

The March 2025 note records:

- extreme morning foot pain and first steps as `torture`;
- relief when Parkinson medication reaches the system;
- the patient's knowledge that Sinemet improves the feet;
- the clinician's expectation that more Sinemet would probably provide further relief;
- a targeted Sinemet CR prescription for the overnight/morning state.

The later response acknowledges `profound relief currently`. ([2025 Neurology record](docs/source-records/neurology-notes-2025.html); [official rebuttal](docs/source-records/official-rebuttal.html))

The retained explanation is that Parkinsonism can magnify the experience of pain `of whatever cause`. This is medically plausible in principle. It could be part of a valid mixed model.

Its patient-specific strength depends on questions the record does not visibly answer:

- What positive evidence established a peripheral generator?
- What objective evidence established neuropathy?
- What would falsify the modulation account?
- Where was the component model documented before the historical management was challenged?
- How did it integrate the 2017 Procyclidine response?
- How did it account for Podiatry being recorded as worsening the foot?
- How did it interpret severe pain persisting at maximum pregabalin dose, while preserving the genuine later fact that Lyrica did substantially reduce pain?

The response explanation accepted the neurological relationship but assigned it only modulation authority. It therefore allowed therapeutic integration without historical reconstruction.

## 13. Was management genuinely patient-responsive?

### Strong evidence that it was

- The 2017 record is extensive and demonstrates considerable diagnostic effort.
- Real treatment risks were recognised and later materialised, particularly dyskinesia sensitivity and adverse effects.
- Procyclidine was carefully titrated and altered for visual toxicity.
- Alternative dopaminergic strategies were attempted.
- Sinemet was introduced in 2021 when the patient felt undertreated and Kemadrin had to be removed.
- Medication was adjusted for OFF symptoms and activities.
- High-dose pregabalin was used for severe pain.
- The nurse and neurologist responded to missed-medication pain/shuffling with Stalevo.
- Sinemet CR was prescribed in March 2025 specifically to help the morning experience including pain.
- The clinician acknowledged the benefit and expressed genuine concern and sorrow.
- Structural, neuropathic, psychological, toxic, genetic and functional possibilities were considered rather than ignored.

These facts make Model A plausible and make allegations of indifference or simple non-treatment unsustainable.

### Strong evidence that the plan became the organising constant

- The first patient/GP account says firm genetics usually will not actively change management.
- It identifies PRKN as plausible but says `whatever` the genetic cause, prolonged dopaminergic avoidance remains.
- No global reopening rule is documented.
- Galway asks Healy whether to continue holding back; Healy gives a distinguishable low-dose replacement model; no explicit reconciliation follows.
- By April 2018 Healy's diagnostic conclusion and the Procyclidine foot signal are known, major levodopa benefit is recognised, and the old policy is affirmatively restated.
- Genetics is deferred partly because it is said not to change management.
- Later neurological foot facts repeatedly change treatment but not the historical partition.
- Peripheral neuropathy retains causal authority without identified objective establishment or falsification criteria.
- The 2025 modulation explanation accepts treatment response without giving it backward classificatory force.
- The disclosed SIMT outcome confirms caution and treatment chronology but does not show an independent reconstruction of the hinge.
- QPS later says a new dystonia diagnosis does not require reopening prior governance outcomes.

The evidence supports responsive clinical care **inside** a persistent architecture. Those are not mutually exclusive findings.

- functional and psychological severity.

### Constants—things unusually stable

- primacy of caution about dopamine exposure and escalation, even after levodopa began;
- absence of an identified global reopening rule;
- preservation of a possible peripheral generator;
- capacity of Podiatry/local pathology to retain explanatory authority without a disclosed endpoint reconciliation;
- genetics treated as weakly management-governing;
- reluctance to give later facts retrospective force against the 2017 architecture;
- local updating without an identified global historical revision.

The literal `no levodopa` state was **not** constant: it ended in 2021. The more durable constant was the governing priority assigned to dopaminergic caution and the absence of backward reconstruction when dopaminergic relationships became clear.

## 17. Three competing models

### Model A—ordinary adaptive care

**Theory:** The original plan was appropriate; clinicians reasonably treated a mixed structural, possible neuropathic and neurological problem while minimising serious long-term dopaminergic risks.

**Strongest evidence**

- genuine structural pathology;
- careful, extensive 2017 reasoning;
- rational young-age risk concerns;
- later actual dyskinesia and adverse effects;
- multiple treatments and investigations;
- pregabalin benefit;
- eventual levodopa, Stalevo and CR adaptation;
- clinician responsiveness and acknowledged pain relief.

**Strongest counterevidence**

- genetics declared presumptively management-neutral before resolution;
- no global reopening rule;
- Healy's distinguishable low-dose treatment formulation not visibly reconciled;
- April 2018 affirmative policy maintenance despite convergent contrary information;
- repeated later neurological foot evidence without historical integration;
- retained peripheral generator lacks identified objective foundation or falsification rule.

**Earliest support:** July 2017.  
**Confidence:** **Moderate as an account of many individual decisions; low-to-moderate as a complete longitudinal explanation.**

**Would be strengthened by:** independent expert support for the full longitudinal approach; contemporaneous component-level mixed model; objective neuropathy evidence; documented review thresholds; PAR showing independent proposition-level testing.

**Would be defeated or weakened by:** evidence that key decisions persisted without review despite recognised changed assumptions; proof Podiatry failed and Neurology knew but retained the allocation; evidence the later modulation model arose only defensively without clinical support.

### Model B—path-dependent management

**Theory:** An initially defensible plan became self-stabilising. Later evidence changed treatment locally but did not adequately reopen the original architecture.

**Strongest evidence**

- July 2017 management invariance language;
- diagnostic uncertainty coupled with operative settlement;
- missing global reopening rules;
- selective practical effect of Healy's opinion;
- April 2018 combined knowledge and affirmative maintenance;
- longitudinal local/global updating table;
- absence of any identified global revision through 2026.

**Strongest counterevidence**

- real medication and diagnostic adaptation;
- levodopa introduction in 2021;
- active OFF-state treatment;
- continued investigations and openness to rare overlap disease;
- plausible mixed-mechanism account.

**Earliest support:** July 2017 architecture; materially strengthened by April 2018.  
**Confidence:** **High as a documentary/governance model.**

**Would be strengthened by:** Podiatry records showing failure without reintegration; native meeting records; complete PAR confirming reliance on the treating narrative; expert evidence that listed events ordinarily required global reassessment.

**Would be defeated by:** contemporaneous records showing repeated explicit reconsideration of the whole architecture, component-level classification, rational retention or rejection of each hypothesis, and clear change criteria.

### Model C—knowing frame preservation

**Theory:** By an identifiable point, clinicians understood that evidence threatened the original architecture and consciously interpreted it in ways that preserved the plan's defensibility.

**Strongest evidence**

- April 2018: the clinician records the contradictory evidence and expressly maintains the policy;
- 2023: the same clinician holds cumulative knowledge across multiple modalities and records `misalignment`, OFF dystonia and possible insufficient levodopa;
- 2025: direct acknowledgement of Sinemet benefit and expected further benefit, followed by deliberate `pain of whatever cause` interpretation;
- increased peripheral-neuropathic certainty in the defensive response compared with the conditional clinic wording;
- governance refusal to permit later diagnosis retrospective effect.

**Strongest counterevidence**

- the interpretive model may be medically sincere and correct;
- mixed mechanisms are plausible;
- caution remained responsive to real dyskinesia risk;
- clinicians did change treatment and did not conceal the dopaminergic benefit;
- no direct evidence establishes self-protective or dishonest purpose.

**Earliest support:** **April 2018 for conscious policy maintenance; 2023 for strong cumulative recognition; 2025 for proved conscious interpretive accommodation.**  
**Confidence:** **High that conscious accommodation occurred by 2025; moderate that it constitutes frame preservation beyond sincere mixed-mechanism judgment; low on improper motive.**

**Would be strengthened by:** evidence the modulation theory arose principally after complaint; internal communications linking interpretation to defence of earlier care; absence of any contemporaneous objective peripheral model; knowing omission of contrary material from governance review.

**Would be defeated by:** contemporaneous pre-complaint documentation of the same component-specific model, positive peripheral evidence, defined falsification criteria, and proof the old architecture was repeatedly reconsidered independently.

### Ranking

1. **Model B—path-dependent management: strongest overall fit.**
2. **Model A—ordinary adaptive care: strong for local decisions, incomplete globally.**
3. **Model C—knowing frame preservation: established narrowly as conscious accommodation by 2025; broader motive-based form not proved.**

Model B must not be collapsed into Model C. Path dependence can arise through ordinary cognition, continuity habits, reasonable risk aversion and documentation inheritance without bad faith.

## 19. Final counterfactual

### If the patient's evolving condition had governed the architecture throughout, the expected record would contain

- a prospective reopening rule in 2017;
- an explicit response to Healy's treatment wording;
- a component-level analysis after Procyclidine withdrawal;
- a statement defining Podiatry as adjunctive or explaining transfer of causal ownership;
- Podiatry feedback and a documented response to failure/worsening/end of pathway;

- explicit historical review after 2023 OFF/undertreatment findings;

Absence of any one item proves little. The cumulative absence across each major contradiction is probative.

### If preservation of the architecture governed later interpretation, the expected pattern would be

- diagnostic uncertainty remaining verbally open while operative allocation stays stable;

- medication responses acted on therapeutically but given non-disruptive meanings;

- governance validating care through inherited notes without visibly rebuilding the disputed hinge;
- later diagnosis accepted prospectively but denied retrospective governance effect.

That predicted pattern closely matches the actual chronology. This is cumulative-pattern reasoning, not inference from silence alone.

## 20. Direct answers to the twelve final questions

### 1. What exactly was the “management” genetics would usually not actively change?

It was the already-selected architecture of alternative initial symptomatic therapy, prolonged levodopa/dopamine-agonist avoidance, Podiatry management of structural/local feet, supportive multidisciplinary care and continued investigation/follow-up without making genetic resolution a prerequisite to treatment change.

### 2. Was the plan provisional and falsifiable?

It was provisional at the level of individual drugs and eventual levodopa use. It was not shown to be globally falsifiable. No documented rule states what would reopen the partition, Podiatry allocation, peripheral hypothesis, genetics neutrality or historical interpretation.

### 3. Did Healy materially `advise otherwise`?

He did not order immediate levodopa. He did provide a materially distinguishable model—probable PARK II managed for long periods with relatively low dopamine replacement while avoiding over-use. Whether that legally or clinically amounted to `advise otherwise` requires expert interpretation, but it was sufficient to require explicit reconciliation with Galway's prolonged non-use position. None is identified.

### 4. How were the diagnostic and treatment parts treated?

The diagnostic part was incorporated: `we/Prof Healy suspect` PARK2/PRKN. The treatment part did not visibly reopen the pre-existing hold-back policy.

### 5. By April 2018 did Monaghan consciously maintain the policy?

Yes, at the documentary level. He recorded information capable of challenging it and affirmatively stated that levodopa could make the patient very much better but `we do not want` it for quite some time. This proves conscious maintenance, not improper motive.

### 6. What evidence was permitted to make `Not clear` clearer?

Multiple events made it clearer clinically: Procyclidine withdrawal/restoration, the parkinsonian foot throw, toe curling, OFF dystonia, missed-Sinemet pain/shuffling, the nurse's undertreatment assessment, Sinemet benefit, CR relief and PRKN confirmation. No discharge rule specified that any of them would change the global plan.

### 7. Did management architecture change accordingly?

Treatment changed repeatedly. The historical architecture did not visibly change accordingly.

### 8. Global reassessment or local accommodation?

Principally local accommodation. No global revision has been identified.

### 9. Strongest evidence of genuine patient responsiveness?

The strongest evidence is the repeated medication adaptation: careful Procyclidine titration, replacement because of ocular harm, levodopa commencement when undertreated, Stalevo for OFF-related symptoms, high-dose pain treatment and targeted Sinemet CR for severe morning feet, with acknowledged profound benefit.

### 10. Strongest evidence that the plan became the organising constant?

The July 2017 sequence: genetics usually would not change management; PRKN specifically plausible; `whatever` the cause, leave levodopa off as long as possible. The April 2018 sequence then repeats the same rule after Healy, probable PARK2 and the Procyclidine foot signal.

### 11. Which model best fits?

Path-dependent management best fits the whole record. Knowing frame preservation is established more narrowly by 2025 as conscious interpretive accommodation, but improper motive is not proved.

### 12. What would falsify that conclusion?

Contemporaneous records showing explicit global reconsideration; a patient-specific component model with objective peripheral support and falsification criteria; documented reconciliation of Healy's treatment view; proof that Podiatry remained adjunctive within retained Neurology ownership; and an independent PAR/SIMT reconstruction addressing the complete contradictory sequence would materially falsify or weaken it.

## Required final finding

> **The July 2017 management plan began as a clinically intelligible response to genuine uncertainty and serious treatment risk. But it was formulated with a presumption of invariance—firm genetics usually would not change management, and prolonged dopaminergic avoidance would apply `whatever` the genetic cause—without a documented global reopening rule. Later care was materially responsive at the level of drugs, doses and immediate symptoms. The record does not show equivalent responsiveness at the level of historical causal classification, Podiatry ownership, peripheral-neuropathy authority, genetic significance or the legitimacy of the decisions built upon the original partition.**

Accordingly:

> **The plan did not remain merely a provisional response continuously governed by the evolving condition. It became a durable architecture within which evolving evidence was interpreted. New evidence increasingly governed local treatment, but management increasingly governed what that evidence was permitted to mean globally and retrospectively.**

Persistence does not prove invalidity. But it does not prove clinical validity either. The burden of explanation is now precise:

> **Show the documented mechanism by which materially contrary evidence could change the plan—and show where that mechanism was actually used.**

On the present repository, no such global mechanism or completed global revision has been identified.
