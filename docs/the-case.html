Counsel orientation

The case is now relatively simple at its centre.

The question is not whether Parkinsonism was real or whether every foot symptom was neurological. It is whether an unresolved lower-limb/foot problem remained genuinely open after a deliberate levodopa-deferral strategy became controlling.

1. What was recognised

In July 2017 severe young-onset Parkinsonism was recognised quickly. The presentation was markedly symmetrical, predominantly akinetic-rigid and minimally tremulous. The gait was strikingly abnormal. A lower-limb appearance was described as somewhat suggestive of dystonic posture and expressly not true foot drop.

PRKN/PARK2 was specifically considered because of the patient's age, symmetry, phenotype, family-history pattern and preserved cognition.

2. What remained unresolved

The feet also contained real structural pathology: corns, calluses, pes planus and hammertoes. Pain, burning and abnormal lower-limb movement were present within the same history.

The record's own answer to the relationship between the foot/leg phenomena and the neurological syndrome was “Not clear.”

This matters. The case does not need to convert `Not clear` retrospectively into `all dystonia`. It asks what happened to a question that Neurology itself had not resolved.

3. What treatment strategy became controlling

Levodopa was deliberately deferred because Liam was very young and because of genuine concern about dyskinesia, impulse-control disorder and dopamine dysregulation. The rationale was clinically intelligible and later dyskinetic sensitivity means it should not be caricatured.

The question is whether that legitimate caution was sufficiently revisitable as the information state changed.

4. Why PRKN knowledge sharpens the question

The discharge package itself contains Galway departmental genetic material, co-authored by Prof Timothy Counihan, describing Parkin/PRKN disease in terms including young onset, dystonia, symmetry and excellent levodopa responsiveness. Monaghan selected that material as relevant enough to send with Liam's own case explanation.

By April 2018 the treating record stated that PRKN was what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause`, while also saying Liam could `obviously ... be very much better` with levodopa or another dopamine agonist and that `we do not want to do that — at least for quite some time`.

Given that PRKN was already suspected, a dystonic lower-limb phenotype was already recorded, and the PRKN material supplied with Liam's discharge package described excellent levodopa responsiveness, what was supposed to cause the decision to withhold levodopa to be reconsidered — and did that trigger ever exist?

5. The Tuesday and Monday patient-source events are different

Liam recalls that after the Tuesday Complex Case Meeting the neurologist told him: “My boss told me not to give you the good stuff. And in time I fear we may come to regret that.” This is preserved as patient-source evidence and does not by itself establish the meeting's content or who `my boss` meant.

Separately, Liam fixes the reported bedside protest to the following Monday morning, immediately before expected discharge. He recalls asking medical students to summon the neurologist and challenging discharge with the analogy of receiving a “full NCT and [being sent] out on bald tires”. He recalls the neurologist responding: “it's easy for you to get a podiatry appointment as me.”

The two events must not be merged.

6. Podiatry is now the immediate evidential hinge

Podiatry may have been entirely appropriate for genuine structural pathology. What matters is what problem was actually referred while Neurology's own relationship question remained `Not clear`.

The awaited records should answer: what did the referral say, who sent it, what information travelled, what Podiatry thought it was treating, what treatment was delivered, what changed, and whether information returned to Neurology.

7. The later record tests the old partition

The following years show a mixed phenotype rather than a simple one. A local corn could respond to local measures while a footdrop-like lower-limb throw was explicitly called Parkinsonian. OFF/dystonia reasoning later entered the record. Lyrica could alter pain without eliminating toe curling. Dopaminergic timing affected movement and pain experience.

This mixed evidence is not adverse to the case. It is why a categorical separation required repeated testing rather than inheritance.

8. March 2025 generated disruptive information

In 2025 the record states that the feet improved once Parkinson medication reached the system, that more Sinemet would probably provide further pain relief, and that dyskinesia limited how far dopamine could be pushed. Night-time Sinemet CR was then directed at the severe morning-foot problem.

The significance is not that this proved every historical foot symptom Parkinsonian. It demonstrated that the inherited partition was inadequate and caused Liam to reopen the prior history.

9. 2026 genetics supplied etiological coherence

A movement-disorders specialist then confirmed two pathogenic Parkin variants and concluded that Liam's Parkinsonism and dystonia were due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.

That finding does not rewrite 2017. It establishes that the eventual cause belonged to a diagnostic architecture the treating team had already entered years earlier.

10. What counsel and experts actually need to decide

The central medical question is whether the levodopa-deferral strategy remained reasonable and sufficiently open to reconsideration as the phenotype and treatment-response evidence changed. The central documentary question is whether the completed discharge package makes sequential reasoning appear more integrated and settled than the contemporaneous decision states actually were. The central Podiatry question is what happened to the unresolved foot problem once it crossed into that pathway.
