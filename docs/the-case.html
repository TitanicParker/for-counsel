Counsel orientation

The case began with the feet. The framework formed before the feet were understood.

Severe Parkinsonism was real and recognised quickly. The investigation concerns what happened when an early treatment decision and a later administrative record made that Parkinsonian framework progressively harder to reopen while the painful foot and lower-limb phenotype remained unresolved.

1. An open problem entered the hospital

On 29 June 2017 a GP urgently referred a 39-year-old man whose gait, movement and dexterity were deteriorating badly enough to threaten his employment. Both feet were materially inside the presenting problem: bilateral foot difficulty, corns and calluses, an abnormal gait and work-limiting deterioration sat beside increased tone, stiffness, brisk reflexes and impaired movement initiation. The referral did not supply a final diagnosis. It asked whether the condition was “parkinsonian or other underlying diagnosis”.

The hospital therefore did not inherit a settled Parkinson’s case with incidental foot complaints. It inherited an unresolved neurological presentation in which the feet were already part of the problem requiring explanation.

Evidence: F0001–F0011 · GP referral

2. Severe Parkinsonism was recognised almost immediately

The neurological findings were substantial: marked symmetrical tone, gross bradykinesia, rigidity and a strikingly abnormal gait. Later DaTSCAN imaging supported a presynaptic dopaminergic disorder. The case does not depend on diminishing those findings. Parkinsonism was genuine.

What remained difficult was the lower-limb phenotype. The record describes a footdrop-like appearance “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture – NOT FOOT DROP”, alongside pes planus, hammertoes, corns, calluses, burning and pain. The same early architecture considered recessive Parkinsonism and PRKN/PARK2. Yet when the relationship between the leg/foot symptoms and the neurological syndrome was addressed, the recorded answer remained: “Not clear.”

Evidence: F0012–F0025 · 2017 discharge / extended consult record

3. The first lock formed in hours, not at the end of a mature eight-day inquiry

The current decision-state reconstruction places the first substantive neurological assessment and the senior/departmental decision to hold back levodopa roughly fourteen hours apart. The exact clock-time reconstruction remains dependent on the native record, but the compressed sequence matters. The treatment position appears to have crystallised before the later, fuller investigation of the painful-foot phenotype and before the developed PRKN/foot reasoning visible in the completed record had been assembled.

That makes the levodopa decision important for more than treatment timing. Once levodopa was treated as off the table, the admission no longer had the most obvious dopaminergic-response route available for interrogating a painful, dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon whose relationship to the neurological syndrome was still being worked out.

First lock — clinical/therapeutic. Did an early treatment strategy begin to determine how the still-emerging phenotype was classified, rather than the completed phenotype determining the treatment strategy?

The repository does not assume that an inpatient levodopa challenge was mandatory. That is an expert question. The investigative point is narrower: therapeutic deferral may also have constrained what could be learned during the admission.

4. The second lock formed over days and weeks

Parkinson’s disease then became sufficiently definite, operationally, to govern treatment strategy, work advice, LTI, discharge planning, follow-up and patient-facing explanation. Podiatry became the practical pathway for the feet. Yet the narrative still contained uncertainty about exact aetiology, atypicality, PRKN/PARK2, dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenology, pain, burning and the neurological relationship of the feet.

The finished discharge artefact is cumulative. The operative discharge/LTI state preceded the formal 13 July DaTSCAN result, which was later incorporated by addendum. That result was real evidence of a dopaminergic disorder. But it was later evidence placed into the same final documentary object as the diagnosis, treatment rationale, work/LTI consequences, referrals and collective language.

The result is a document that can appear more integrated and retrospectively confirmed than the underlying decision sequence can presently be shown to have been at each earlier moment.

Second lock — administrative/documentary. Uncertainty remained clinically while certainty governed administratively. The working question is whether the completed record supplied retrospective coherence and authority to a framework formed on a thinner evidential state.

5. The protest is part of the hinge

Liam reports that he protested at the bedside when the feet were being separated from the neurological problem and that four medical students were present. The completed record does not preserve that protest. It does preserve collective language, senior/departmental authority and the statement that Liam had a “good understanding” of why levodopa was being delayed.

If the protest occurred as described, the bedside decision-state contained a material counter-position that the final documentary state does not show. The four students therefore matter not as character witnesses, but as a defined potential eyewitness class.

The live task is to identify them independently through teaching, placement or ward-round records and obtain their first recollections before exposing them to Liam’s later reconstruction. Independent recognition, presence, disagreement, subject matter, remembered words and clinician response have different evidential values and must remain separately recorded.

Protest witness investigation

6. PRKN was not missed; it was already inside the architecture

This is not a case in which a genetic explanation appeared from nowhere years later. PRKN/PARK2 was expressly considered in the early diagnostic architecture. By April 2018 the working record described a specific PRKN test or gene panel as an available route and referred to PRKN as what “we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause”.

The precise information flow now matters. What did Monaghan know when levodopa was first deferred? What had Counihan been told? What did the Complex Case Meeting actually hear? What was sent to Healy? Did Healy receive the painful-foot history, the dystonic-looking lower-limb findings and the “Not clear” relationship? What did he reply, and when?

Those are provenance questions about the first lock, not decorative missing paperwork.

Evidence: F0021 and later PRKN/Healy facts · 2017 Healy correspondence · 2018 Neurology

7. The first reopening evidence arrived almost immediately

Within months, a medication withdrawal created a natural treatment-state comparison. Procyclidine was stopped because of visual symptoms. The later April 2018 history records that the first thing noticed changing rapidly involved the outside-left-foot/corn site; symptoms worsened and Liam felt more “shifty” and “unrelaxed”. A separate October 2017 email independently preserves the withdrawal, restart, incomplete control on a lower dose and subsequent escalation, while describing a striking overall physical improvement as “living in a miracle”.

The importance of that episode is not that it proves a corn was neurological. It is that part of the unresolved foot/lower-limb experience changed with neurological medication very early, after the second lock had already created a stable working history.

Evidence: F0041–F0048 · Treatment-response chapter

8. The next eight years test the inherited classification

The feet did not disappear after 2017. They repeatedly re-entered Neurology through cramps, sensitivity, abnormal lower-limb movement, medication timing, OFF reasoning, toe curling, pain and patient challenge. By 2023 the clinical record expressly described a “misalignment” over the feet. The same period also contains adverse evidence: structural pathology remained real, toe supports and Lyrica helped, and a ten-day period off Parkinson medication did not produce a neat Parkinsonian change while severe foot and leg pain persisted.

That mixed evidence is not a weakness to hide. It is the point. The longitudinal question is whether each new event was used to reopen the inherited 2017 classification, or whether it was repeatedly interpreted inside a documentary framework that already appeared settled.

2020–2024: the longitudinal return

9. March 2025 was a therapeutic rupture

On 10 March 2025 severe morning foot pain was again central. The record says the feet improved once Parkinson’s medication reached the system. The neurologist maintained that the pain was not directly Parkinsonian, while recognising painful dystonia, predicting that more Sinemet would probably provide further relief and introducing night-time Sinemet CR into the severe morning-foot problem.

The subsequent response was later described by the treating neurologist as “profound relief currently”. Nine days after the clinic, Liam made a formal complaint saying a recent discovery of dopamine responsiveness had changed his understanding of the preceding history.

March 2025 therefore matters twice: as a clinical event in the foot problem, and as evidence of when the patient’s own historical interpretation changed. It is also serious adverse evidence for limitation analysis.

March 2025 chapter

10. The 2026 result supplies genetic coherence, not hindsight permission

In February 2026 a specialist reported two pathogenic Parkin variants and concluded that Liam’s Parkinsonism and dystonia were due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations. The result gives later etiological coherence to Parkinsonism and dystonia that had already coexisted in the earlier record.

It does not prove that every historical foot symptom was dystonia. It does not make the 2026 answer available to clinicians in 2017. Its significance is that the later confirmed cause belongs to the same diagnostic architecture that earlier clinicians had already entered when they discussed PRKN/PARK2.

PRKN and the hindsight boundary

11. Institutional review inherited the documentary object

The complaint later moved through QPS, IMF/PAR and SIMT processes. SIMT recorded a no-deficit outcome. That institutional conclusion is part of the evidence and must be treated seriously.

But the provenance question follows the same logic as the clinical one. If the 2017 record omitted a material contemporaneous protest, or if later-completed material supplied coherence to earlier decisions, a review that principally inherited that record could also inherit those features unless it independently reconstructed the disputed hinge.

The complete PAR, source schedule, SIMT agenda, minutes and circulation pack are not presently available. The live question is therefore what independent historical evidence, if any, entered at the governance stage — particularly on the foot/Podiatry transition, protest, treatment-response history and later reopening evidence.

Institutional response and source genealogy

12. The mechanism under investigation

The first lock may have constrained what could be learned. The second lock made the resulting state appear settled. The inherited record then became the starting point for eight years of later care and later review.

This is a mechanism hypothesis, not an allegation of motive. The investigation does not need dishonesty, concealment or bad faith to explain path dependency. A clinically plausible early decision can become difficult to revisit simply because later actors inherit a completed record that already contains diagnosis, treatment rationale, apparent senior authority, patient understanding, administrative implementation, referral pathways and later objective confirmation.

The questions for counsel and experts are therefore about standard, reopening, information, causation, limitation and documentary provenance: when should the unresolved foot/lower-limb relationship have been revisited; what should have triggered that reconsideration; what information did each actor actually possess at the relevant time; and what consequences, if any, followed from the durability of the inherited classification?
