Counsel orientation

The case is now simpler — and more specific.

The central question is not whether Parkinsonism was real or whether every foot symptom was neurological. It is why an early levodopa-deferral strategy and unresolved foot partition remained controlling after the evidential state moved toward probable PARK2.

1. The presentation was unusual from the start

Liam was 39 at first Neurology presentation. Monaghan's contemporaneous Healy correspondence records a possible symptom history of approximately 13 years and expressly says this was Liam's first time being seen by Neurology.

The inpatient phenotype was severe, markedly symmetrical and predominantly akinetic-rigid. The gait was strikingly abnormal and the lower-limb appearance was described as somewhat suggestive of dystonic posture and expressly not true foot drop.

2. The feet were mixed, not simple

There was real structural pathology: corns, calluses, pes planus and hammertoes. There was also abnormal lower-limb movement, pain and burning. The record itself did not resolve the relationship. It said “Not clear.”

That phrase remains the correct 2017 state. The case does not need to convert it retrospectively into `all dystonia`.

3. Podiatry was already being routed “as Parkinson's”

The newly supplied GP record contains a 6 July 2017 entry:

“Phoned podiatry to discuss case - will mark as priority case and as Parkinson's - should be called mid September.”

This does not tell us what the written referral said or what Podiatry ultimately believed it was treating. It does establish that the GP-side pathway was already being framed within the Parkinson's presentation.

4. Levodopa deferral was deliberate

Galway deliberately held levodopa/dopamine agonists back because of genuine concern about dyskinesia, impulse-control disorder and dopamine dysregulation in a very young patient. Monaghan's contemporaneous letter to Healy states:

“Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?”

The issue is not whether caution had a rationale. It did. The issue is whether the balance was ever recalculated when new information arrived.

5. PRKN/PARK2 moved from possibility to probable specialist diagnosis

The July 2017 Galway record specifically considered PRKN/PARK2. Then, after personally reviewing Liam on 12 September 2017, Prof Daniel G. Healy wrote:

“I think probably has Park II and I would suggest testing that initially. If it is negative I would go on to test a panel.”

Healy also recorded that PARK II patients can manage for long periods with relatively low doses of dopamine replacement therapy, while becoming dyskinetic if over-used.

Healy did not expressly order immediate levodopa. But his wording creates a narrower question than before: what happened to the pre-existing `hold back` policy once the specialist expressly invited to advise on it had concluded probable PARK II and described cautious dopamine replacement rather than simple non-use?

6. The DaTSCAN confirmed an expected abnormality, not the genetic subtype

The 13 July addendum says the scan was abnormal “as was expected”. The same source still described an atypical, autosomal-recessive possibility. The scan confirmed a dopaminergic Parkinsonian disorder; it did not settle the genetic aetiology.

7. By 2018 the lock is explicit

The April 2018 record says PRKN/PARK2 is what we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause. In the same note, Monaghan writes that Liam could “obviously ... be very much better” with levodopa or another dopamine agonist but that “we do not want to do that — at least for quite some time.”

That is the cleanest expression of the central problem: the diagnostic branch strengthened while the treatment strategy did not visibly reopen.

8. The omitted-protest witness route is now narrowly defined

Liam now fixes the reported bedside protest to Monday 10 July 2017, St Anthony's Ward, approximately 10:00–12:00. He says four medical students were together on the first day of a new placement batch and witnessed the encounter. This remains patient-source evidence pending independent corroboration, but the witness class is now realistically identifiable.

9. The later record creates an unexplained etiological transition

The source sequence now reads: PRKN/PARK2 considered in 2017; Healy says probable PARK II in September 2017; Galway says in 2018 that PARK2 is what we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause; yet the 25 March 2025 complaint response states that the belief/understanding was idiopathic Parkinson's disease.

No presently identified document explains when, why or on what evidence the earlier PARK2 hypothesis was abandoned.

10. 2026 genetics resolves the aetiology

St Vincent's confirmed two pathogenic Parkin variants and concluded that Liam's Parkinsonism and dystonia are due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.

The later result does not prove that every historical foot symptom was dystonia. It does establish that the PARK2 branch already identified in 2017 — and called probable by Healy — was the causal branch.

Current controlling question. After Healy personally reviewed Liam, said he probably had PARK II, recommended PARK II testing first and described relatively low-dose dopamine replacement as compatible with the disease, what process caused Galway's pre-existing levodopa-deferral strategy and unresolved foot partition to return to an open state?
