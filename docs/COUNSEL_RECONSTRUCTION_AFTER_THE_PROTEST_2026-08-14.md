# For Counsel — Reconstruction After the Protest

**Working date:** 14 August 2026  
**Status:** counsel-facing reconstruction and reader architecture  
**Purpose:** to restate the case after the 14 August 2026 analytical breakthrough: the decisive object is not only the 2017 discharge summary, the levodopa decision, or the later treatment history, but the **institutional moment immediately after the patient's bedside protest** and the information-governance structure that followed from it.

> **This is not a pleading, expert report, clinical diagnosis, finding of negligence, dishonesty or causation.** It is a source-controlled reconstruction designed to show counsel what the case now appears to be, why the newer architecture is stronger than the earlier formulations, what remains to be proved, and what questions should be put to witnesses and experts.

---

# Reader entry point

This case is easiest to misunderstand when it is presented as a dispute about a drug.

It is not simply:

- levodopa should have been started earlier;
- Parkinson's disease was misdiagnosed;
- the feet were ignored;
- Podiatry was inappropriate;
- one neurologist failed to notice dystonia;
- a later successful prescription proves what should have happened eight years earlier.

The case is more coherent, and more serious, when understood as a problem of **clinical information architecture**.

A young man entered hospital through a combined problem of feet, gait, tone, initiation and loss of function. Severe Parkinsonism was recognised. Real structural foot pathology was also recognised. Their relationship was expressly recorded as **`Not clear`**. Yet while the documentary state remained uncertain, the operative state became divided: Parkinsonism belonged to Neurology; the feet were routed toward Podiatry; ordinary dopamine replacement was deliberately held outside the immediate treatment strategy.

Then, before discharge, the patient says he challenged that arrangement directly.

That is the hinge.

If the four medical students corroborate the encounter, the treating neurologist knew before discharge that the patient did **not** regard the central foot problem as adequately resolved and did **not** regard Podiatry as a sufficient answer to the neurological admission. The durable discharge account nevertheless does not preserve that challenge.

The later record then shows an extraordinary asymmetry. The feet repeatedly become neurologically informative. They change with neurological medication. A later foot throw is called parkinsonian. OFF-related pain and shuffling are recognised. Levodopa changes treatment decisions. Eventually the patient's exact overnight and first-step foot experience is used to redesign dopamine coverage and is followed by profound relief.

Yet the later evidence repeatedly changes treatment **without visibly changing the historical meaning of the case**.

The present governing sentence is therefore:

> **The body was allowed to change the prescription. It was not visibly allowed to change the history.**

The new question is sharper still:

> **Who owned the duty to change the meaning of the case, rather than merely change the patient's medication?**

# Part I — What the repository already established

## 1. The original problem was integrated before it was divided

The 29 June 2017 GP referral did not present a clean neurological diagnosis beside a clean Podiatry diagnosis. It described an evolving whole:

- unusual festinant/stiff gait;
- bilateral foot problems;
- difficulty initiating movement;
- increased tone and stiffness;
- brisk reflexes and possible cogwheeling;
- toe clawing;
- discomfort;
- loss of dexterity and work function;
- concern for a Parkinsonian or other underlying neurological disorder.

The patient had initially attributed much of his walking difficulty to corns/calluses and poor feet. The GP's referral question was therefore not merely, **Does this man have Parkinsonism?** It was also, in practical terms:

> **Are the feet and the gait part of the same underlying neurological process, or are they separate?**

That question remained alive during admission.

Neurology identified severe young-onset Parkinsonism. The examination contained a striking abnormal gait, marked rigidity and bradykinesia, and a lower-limb appearance described as suggestive of dystonic posture and expressly **`NOT FOOT DROP`**. The same admission also documented genuine structural pathology including pes planus, hammertoes, corns/calluses and pressure-related problems.

The record did not resolve the relationship. It said:

> **`Not clear`.**

That wording is not incidental. It is the evidential state at the foundation of the case.

The critical early transformation was therefore:

> **Documentary state: unresolved.**  
> **Operative state: partitioned.**

Podiatry could properly deal with structural feet. Neurology could properly deal with Parkinsonism. The problem was what happened to the unresolved **relationship** between the two.

---

## 2. The GP had already treated Podiatry as part of the Parkinson's picture

On 6 July 2017 the GP contacted Podiatry and recorded that the referral would be marked as a priority **“as Parkinson's.”**

That matters because it shows that even the apparently peripheral pathway did not begin as a clean declaration that the feet were unrelated to the neurological case. The GP was still carrying an integrated picture.

The patient was also administratively entering a Parkinson's state before leaving hospital. The Long Term Illness documentation is dated 9 July. The institutional diagnosis was therefore already operating administratively while the relationship of the feet to the movement disorder remained unresolved.

This chronology becomes important once the 10 July protest is restored. It permits a clean evidential boundary:

- **before the protest:** referral, examination, diagnosis, Podiatry expedite, administrative Parkinson's status, treatment architecture forming;
- **the protest:** direct challenge to the adequacy of the foot disposition;
- **after the protest:** completion and transmission of the durable institutional narrative.

This does not mean that every post-10-July fact is false. It means that post-protest **clinician-authored narrative concerning the disputed patient position** has a different reliability problem from objective tests, prescriptions and examination findings.

---

## 3. The treatment frame crystallised before the uncertainty was resolved

The repository already demonstrated that the early treatment architecture involved prolonged caution with levodopa and dopamine agonists.

The later Monaghan-to-Healy correspondence contains the clearest short statement:

> **“Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?”**

This is important not because it proves any particular person dictated a negligent plan. It shows that by the time Healy was asked, Galway already had:

- an existing treatment preference;
- a collective or consultative dimension;
- an open-ended temporal formulation — **“insofar as maybe possible”**;
- a theoretical escape clause — **“unless you'd advise otherwise.”**

The discharge reasoning similarly treated prolonged deferral as a policy capable of surviving substantial diagnostic uncertainty. Genetics might refine description, but the treatment architecture was already written as broadly stable **whatever** the eventual genetic cause.

The repository's earlier question was therefore:

> **What evidence was authorised to reopen the frame once it had been set?**

No clear global reopening rule has been identified.

There were rules for changing things **inside** the plan — titrate procyclidine, stop drugs for adverse effects, alter individual prescriptions, obtain investigations, use Podiatry, consider levodopa “sooner or later.”

But no visible rule said that the **plan itself** had to reopen if:

- PRKN/PARK2 became more likely;
- specialist advice described a different dopamine-replacement strategy;
- neurological medication changed the feet;
- the Podiatry pathway failed to resolve the disability;
- dystonia became clearer;
- pain or disability became intolerable;
- later dopamine treatment materially altered the disputed foot domain;
- the patient directly challenged the adequacy of the allocation.

That absence becomes much more consequential after the protest.

# Part II — The protest is not merely an omitted complaint

## 4. What the patient says happened

The patient reports that on 10 July 2017, before expected discharge, four medical students were at the bedside. He asked one of them to find the supervising/duty neurologist. The neurologist returned.

The patient used a vivid roadworthiness analogy. The substance was:

> **You have put me through the neurological equivalent of a full roadworthiness test and are sending me out on bald tyres.**

The point was not that the patient had already worked out PRKN, dystonia, OFF phenomena or dopamine pharmacology.

The point was much simpler and clinically more important:

> **The thing that brought me here is still not resolved, and Podiatry does not answer what I am asking Neurology to answer.**

The patient recalls the neurologist responding briskly that it was as easy for the patient to get a Podiatry appointment as it was for him.

The student witnesses remain the critical independent evidential target. Until corroborated, this is patient-source evidence. If corroborated in substance, the status of the foundational record changes materially.

---

## 5. The most important moment may be the moment after the encounter

The central discovery of the 14 August 2026 conversation was that the case contains an under-examined institutional moment:

> **What happened after the patient challenged the plan, but before the plan became durable history?**

This is different from asking whether the protest occurred.

Assume the protest occurred and was witnessed. The neurologist has now been summoned back to the bedside and publicly confronted by a patient who says the discharge does not adequately address the central presenting problem.

The encounter ends.

The patient is no longer part of the institutional conversation.

What remains is:

- the treating neurologist;
- the senior/collective architecture associated with the treatment plan;
- the hospital whose discharge record will become the durable account.

At that point several questions exist whether or not anyone formally names them:

1. Do we reopen the neurological treatment question?
2. Do we reopen the foot question?
3. Is Podiatry still sufficient as the practical answer?
4. Does the senior-supported treatment frame need to be returned upstream?
5. Is a movement-disorders opinion needed before or immediately after discharge?
6. Who owns the unresolved relationship between structural feet and neurological lower-limb phenomena?
7. What must the record say about the patient's disagreement?
8. If the plan remains unchanged, what will cause it to reopen later?

The striking feature of the surviving record is not merely that the same outcome emerged.

It is that the **adjudicative bridge** is difficult to locate.

A well-governed bridge would look something like:

> Patient challenges discharge because feet remain unresolved → issue is clinically reconsidered → reasons are recorded for retaining or changing the plan → patient's disagreement is preserved → responsibility and reopening conditions are specified.

Instead, the durable state appears to preserve the architecture while losing the challenge.

That is the new hinge.

# Part III — What a hospital-governance lens made visible

## 6. The first governance stress test

During the 14 August conversation, the scenario was put prospectively to an imagined senior hospital manager: **You are standing immediately after the protest. Manage the situation.**

The resulting answer was revealing because it did not require anyone to assume the patient was right about causation.

The governance logic was:

- pause discharge long enough for senior reassessment;
- distinguish structural from possible neurological lower-limb components;
- make Neurology retain ownership of the integration question;
- treat Podiatry as parallel rather than substitutive;
- reconsider the dopamine-deferral strategy as a current patient-specific decision;
- preserve the substance of the patient's disagreement in the record;
- identify who owns follow-up;
- define reopening triggers;
- if treatment later changes the feet, actively revise the earlier formulation rather than merely append a treatment fact.

The key sentence was:

> **Referral to Podiatry does not close the neurological formulation.**

The other critical sentence was that leaving the treatment unchanged could still be defensible, **but the burden would be to demonstrate that the decision was consciously remade after the patient's challenge.**

That reframed the evidential question.

The issue is no longer merely:

> Why did the hospital retain Podiatry and dopamine deferral?

It is:

> **Where is the visible adjudication showing that the patient's challenge was processed before the inherited architecture was allowed to continue?**

Continuation is not the same thing as renewal.

“Podiatry” shows continuation.

“We reconsidered his objection and nevertheless retain Podiatry because X, Y and Z, with review if A, B or C occurs” would show conscious renewal.

The distinction is profound.

---

## 7. The reverse-engineering stress test

The second governance exercise worked backwards from what actually happened.

The expert was given the observed longitudinal pattern and asked:

> **What information-management structure would have to exist for the original disposition to survive while increasingly contradictory neurological evidence was treated locally?**

The answer was perhaps the strongest conceptual result of the day:

> **“Local updating without global updating” is not merely a description of the outcome; it is very close to the governance structure required to produce it.**

The expert then identified the missing middle after the protest.

For the disagreement to disappear from the durable account, one of several mechanisms could suffice:

- the exchange was treated as transient dissatisfaction and not preserved;
- the disagreement was compressed into broader language about understanding and Podiatry;
- the clinician reconsidered internally but documented only the outcome;
- the disagreement was absorbed into the clinician's own interpretation, leaving the institutional conclusion but not the patient's challenge.

None of those mechanisms requires fabrication of a test or invention of an examination finding.

What matters is the absence of the **documentary bridge**.

The expert described the key property as **visible adjudication**.

That becomes a central counsel question:

> **If the patient's challenge was consciously reconsidered, where is the documentary residue of that reconsideration?**

# Part IV — Why `Not clear` could survive almost anything

## 8. Uncertainty without actionability

The second governance stress test identified a subtle but powerful systems mechanism:

> **The system could preserve uncertainty without making uncertainty actionable.**

`Not clear` is a remarkably durable state if nobody defines what would make it clearer.

It can coexist with structural pathology.

It can coexist with neurological medication responsiveness.

It can coexist with dystonia.

It can coexist with OFF phenomena.

It can coexist with Podiatry failure.

It can coexist with levodopa benefit.

It can even coexist with later genetic confirmation.

Unless the system contains a falsification or reopening rule, each new fact can be added beside the old uncertainty without forcing a reconstruction.

The necessary distinction is:

> **Descriptive uncertainty** is not the same as **an owned unresolved problem**.

If nobody owns the proposition:

> What is the evolving relationship between these structural feet and this movement disorder?

then Podiatry can own feet, Neurology can own Parkinsonism, nursing can own OFF timing, the GP can own pain escalation, and every participant can act sensibly within a local mandate while the cross-domain question belongs to nobody.

That is how local rationality can produce global incoherence.

---

## 9. The absence of a falsifier is more important than the persistence of a hypothesis

The case should not be overstated as though a peripheral or mixed explanation was illegitimate.

Mixed pathology was always possible and remained possible later. Structural foot disease was real. Local measures could help. Pregabalin later helped substantially. Pain could have more than one generator.

The stronger question is:

> **What would have falsified or materially revised the operative separation?**

No clear answer has been found.

Would procyclidine changing the disputed foot domain do it?

Would a parkinsonian foot throw do it?

Would OFF-related foot pain do it?

Would severe morning worsening when dopamine coverage was absent do it?

Would profound relief from a targeted dopamine formulation do it?

If every possible answer can be absorbed without changing the historical frame, then `Not clear` has ceased to function as genuine uncertainty. It has become a **non-falsifiable stabiliser**.

This is one reason the omitted protest matters so much. A preserved protest would itself have operated as a standing question attached to the file:

> **The patient disputes whether the feet have been adequately integrated. Reassess if later evidence bears on this.**

Without that flag, subsequent events can arrive as isolated new facts instead of answers accumulating to an old unresolved question.

---

# Part V — The procyclidine event becomes more important after the protest is restored

## 10. The first contradiction arrived early

Neurology's own treatment generated an early natural experiment.

Procyclidine produced a dramatic improvement in the patient's overall movement state. The patient described the effect in extraordinary terms: his walk returned, his body loosened, dexterity improved, and he felt as though he were “living in a miracle.”

Withdrawal then altered the state again. The patient later reported that the familiar outside-left-foot phenomenon changed rapidly along with broader bodily tension and movement. Restoration improved the system again.

The essential point is not that procyclidine proved every foot feature neurological.

The important fact is that the disputed foot/lower-limb domain was **neurologically modifiable** and moved with the same treatment state that altered the Parkinsonian syndrome.

Locally, the medication was restored and adjusted.

Globally, no identified source shows that the original foot/Podiatry allocation was reopened.

Before today's insight, this looked like an early missed integration opportunity.

After the protest is restored, it becomes something stronger:

> **Neurological medication generated evidence bearing directly upon the unresolved question the patient had already challenged before discharge.**

That changes the epistemic meaning of the event.

It is no longer merely an interesting medication response.

It is potential evidence answering a preserved dispute — except that the dispute was not preserved in the durable narrative.

---

# Part VI — Healy, the promise of levodopa, and the control state of 2018

## 11. Healy did not simply contribute a diagnosis; he was asked about the governing treatment position

Galway asked Healy a treatment question against an already existing policy:

> **“Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?”**

Healy's response strengthened probable PARK2 and described patients with PARK II as managing for long periods with **relatively low doses of dopamine replacement therapy**, with concern about dyskinesia if over-used.

The later Galway record incorporates the PARK2 idea but does not visibly reformulate the original deferral policy around the specialist's treatment language.

By April 2018 Monaghan records that the patient could be **“very much better”** if given levodopa or another dopamine agonist, while maintaining that **“we do not want to do that — at least for quite some time.”**

The sentence immediately before that impression is important. The neurologist is not describing a man in catastrophic decline. He is describing a patient whose walk is “actually very good,” with good arm swing and overall movement characterised as relatively excellent.

That creates an unexpectedly important control state.

The patient had gone from severe illness to an unusually good functional period on the existing treatment. The clinician simultaneously acknowledged that conventional dopamine replacement could produce a still better state.

The meaning experienced by the patient was therefore not simply:

> We are denying you something you need now.

It also contained a future promise:

> **There is a better treatment state available later. Imagine how good you may be when we eventually use it.**

The patient's later life choices make that promise visible.

---

## 12. The dog is not a sentimental detail; it is a functional marker

When Sinemet finally entered the treatment system in 2021, the patient bought a dog within weeks.

By 2022 he was walking for hours.

That act belongs in the counsel narrative because it captures how the patient understood the treatment transition.

He had spent years inside a strategy that withheld the treatment class described as capable of making him “very much better.” When levodopa finally arrived, he behaved like a man whose future mobility had opened.

He organised his life around walking.

This matters when the feet later become the domain of severe suffering.

The later deterioration is not merely another symptom. It occurs against the background of a treatment promise whose human content was **the restoration and preservation of walking**.

The dog therefore becomes a functional exhibit of expectation, trust and lived response.

It links:

- the 2018 “very much better” statement;
- the eventual introduction of Sinemet;
- the patient's belief that the deferred future had arrived;
- the later collapse of the same foot-centred function.

This is one of the conversation's new human insights and should not be lost in a technical medication chronology.

# Part VII — The 2021 problem was not merely that levodopa arrived late

## 13. Drug commencement is not phenotype formulation

One of the most important analytical improvements today was moving beyond the binary question:

> **Was levodopa started too late?**

The 2021 prescribing record shows that levodopa finally entered, but the feet were not visibly used as the organising response domain.

The sequence included:

- Sinemet 50/12.5 mg BD;
- rapid escalation to QDS as Kemadrin was withdrawn;
- continued Comtess/entacapone exposure;
- transition to Stalevo with increased levodopa exposure for re-emerging dystonia and altered gait;
- later reductions and altered schedules.

The prescriptions reveal an active treatment system. Drugs were added, removed, escalated, reduced, combined and redistributed.

What is not visible in the 2021 plan is a structured map of:

- foot pain before and after doses;
- toe clawing/curling;
- foot throw;
- morning walking;
- onset and wearing-off in the feet;
- overnight absence of medication;
- first-step function;
- a predefined formulation/timing change triggered by the feet.

That leads to a stronger proposition:

> **The medication problem is not merely whether levodopa was delayed. It is whether dopamine therapy, once introduced, was adequately formulated and evaluated around the patient's principal longitudinal disease interface.**

This is why “low dose” and “hold back as long as possible” both require operational translation. Neither phrase answers:

- dose size;
- frequency;
- timing;
- formulation;
- coverage;
- symptom targets;
- response criteria;
- tolerability limits;
- what the patient should notice and report.

The later 2025 success demonstrates the importance of those dimensions because the drug itself was not new. What changed was the **phenotype-to-regimen model**.

# Part VIII — Delegation and the continuity neurologist

## 14. The prescriptions expose the governance structure

Looking at the case through prescriptions produced another new insight.

The continuity consultant remains named over the case, but much of the operational medication work increasingly appears through others.

Patrick Browne, ANP, started or altered major Parkinson medication steps, including the 2021 Sinemet pathway. David Folan later received patient reports, identified undertreatment/OFF phenomena and proposed changes. Healy supplied external specialist reasoning. Podiatry occupied the structural-foot pathway. Senior local authority helped shape the original strategic architecture.

This means the correct criticism is not:

> **The neurologist personally failed to perform every clinical task.**

The stronger systems question is:

> **What was the one task that could not safely be delegated?**

The answer is **longitudinal synthesis**.

The continuity neurologist's unique role was not merely to prescribe, authorise or take notes. It was to ask what all incoming evidence did to the model of the disease over time.

The governance stress test captured this distinction neatly:

> A consultant who receives observations and approves local treatment changes is functioning as a **treatment coordinator**.

> A true longitudinal owner must also function as an **epistemic integrator**.

That term is useful because it identifies the alleged missing function without caricaturing the clinician as inactive.

A great deal happened.

The question is whether the person holding continuity responsibility repeatedly changed the **treatment** while failing to change the **meaning** of the case.

---

## 15. Senior authority and the one-way ratchet

The senior/collective dimension matters because it may explain how a provisional policy became unusually sticky.

If a treatment architecture originates in or is strongly supported by senior authority, a continuity consultant may remain technically free to alter it while psychologically or institutionally treating it as something that must be returned upstream before it can be reversed.

The governance stress test described the danger as a **one-way ratchet**:

> the authority of the senior decision is remembered; its provisionality is forgotten.

That gives counsel a concrete governance inquiry rather than a personality theory:

- Who originated or approved the treatment architecture?
- Who had authority to reopen it?
- Did Monaghan understand that he could independently reformulate it?
- Did contradictory evidence return to Counihan or a Complex Case Meeting?
- If not, was Monaghan expected to reconcile the contradictions himself?
- Where is the record of that reconciliation?

The case is therefore not reduced to whether a non-specialist should have referred once.

It concerns **ownership of the patient, origin of the policy, authority to revise the policy, and responsibility for carrying contradictory information back through the authority structure**.

# Part IX — The feet become increasingly informative while history remains stable

## 16. The later chronology now reads as answers to an omitted question

Once the protest is restored, the chronology changes character.

### 2017
The patient asks, in substance: **Why are you discharging me without resolving the feet?**

### 2017–18
The disputed foot/lower-limb state changes with procyclidine withdrawal and restoration.

### 2020
A foot/lower-limb throw is expressly characterised as parkinsonian with normal power.

### 2022
The feet require 20–25 minutes to “warm up” most mornings; medication timing is increasingly relevant to function.

### 2023
Severe bilateral burning pain becomes a safety-level problem. The GP reports a “death wish” and asks for urgent review before “we get into trouble here.” Movement-disorders staff later assess the Parkinson's state as undertreated and leg pain/shuffling as OFF-related.

### 2024
Pain and toe curling separate further by treatment response; medication timing continues to matter.

### 2025
The overnight interval and first footsteps are finally mapped explicitly. The patient reports that the feet improve once Parkinson medication reaches the system. More Sinemet is expected to provide further relief. Nighttime controlled-release Sinemet is used around that temporal account. Profound relief is later acknowledged.

### 2026
PRKN genetics provides later etiological coherence to Parkinsonism and dystonia.

If the protest had remained visible, each event would be read as evidence bearing on a preserved unresolved question.

Without the protest, each event can appear as something newly arising:

- now the patient has foot pain;
- now there is dystonia;
- now missed Sinemet matters;
- now medication helps the feet.

This is perhaps the most powerful causal effect of the omission:

> **The omitted protest removed the question to which the later clinical evidence was the answer.**

# Part X — The temporal asymmetry

## 17. The one-way temporal valve

The record demonstrates substantial prospective adaptability.

Medication changes repeatedly.

The system is not static in the ordinary sense.

The deeper problem is temporal asymmetry:

> **The management frame was allowed to move forward. It was not visibly allowed to move backward.**

New evidence could change what happened next.

It could change tomorrow's prescription.

It could change dose, timing, formulation and adjuncts.

But the same evidence did not acquire equivalent authority to change what earlier events meant.

This produces what can be described as a **one-way temporal valve**.

The feet could change treatment prospectively while being denied retrospective evidential force.

The 2025 CR episode is the clearest example. A patient-described temporal pattern finally acquires direct prescribing authority. Yet the success is absorbed without an identified reconstruction of the 2017 partition.

The correct retrospective operation would not be to rewrite 2017 with hindsight.

It would be:

> **Reassess an unresolved earlier proposition in light of later evidence that bears directly upon it.**

That distinction matters. Reassessment is not hindsight substitution.

---

# Part XI — The asymmetry has an organisational explanation

## 18. Prospective evidence at low threshold; retrospective force at high threshold

The governance stress test identified the exact structure:

> **Prospective therapeutic evidence can be accepted at a relatively low pragmatic threshold, while retrospective evidential force is discounted at a much higher threshold.**

For treatment:

> symptoms worsen OFF and improve ON → adjust medication.

For historical reconstruction:

> that does not prove every original foot feature was neurological; structural disease was real; pain was multifactorial → retain the inherited formulation.

Both statements can be clinically reasonable in isolation.

The problem appears when the second statement becomes a mechanism by which **no amount of accumulating evidence ever requires the historical proposition to be revisited**.

This is how the same feet can become sufficiently neurological to change a prescription while remaining strangely powerless over history.

The case should therefore avoid arguing that a later response conclusively proves one historic cause.

The stronger argument is that the response had enough evidential relevance to trigger **reassessment of an explicitly unresolved historic relationship**.

# Part XII — Why the protest becomes “radioactive”

## 19. What “radioactive” means in this analysis

The term does not mean the neurologist was literally forbidden to mention the feet.

He did mention them.

The feet were repeatedly documented.

They were treated.

Their symptoms were sometimes analysed carefully.

“Radioactive” describes something narrower:

> **Once the protest is omitted, fully integrating later foot evidence threatens to expose the significance of the original separation and the missing patient challenge.**

The protest says:

> You have not resolved the feet, and Podiatry is not an adequate answer to the neurological question I am raising.

Later evidence says, increasingly:

> neurological treatment materially alters the domain the patient was talking about.

If both propositions are allowed to coexist in the same longitudinal frame, then the later evidence points backward toward the patient's original objection.

That creates pressure on two early commitments simultaneously:

1. the treatment architecture that kept ordinary dopamine replacement outside the immediate strategy;
2. the foot/Podiatry allocation whose adequacy the patient says he challenged before discharge.

The feet can therefore be treated locally without difficulty.

What becomes difficult is allowing the feet to answer the historical question.

That is the sense in which the **contents** of the protest become radioactive for a continuity system built on the completed discharge narrative.

# Part XIII — The record-reliability breakpoint

## 20. Do not reject the whole chart; withdraw presumptive narrative authority selectively

Another important insight from the conversation was that 10 July can be treated as a **reliability breakpoint** without making the implausible claim that everything afterwards is false.

Objective and contemporaneous material can remain highly reliable:

- examination findings;
- tests and imaging;
- prescriptions;
- laboratory results;
- observed treatment events;
- what clinicians recorded they thought;
- institutional acts that demonstrably occurred.

What becomes unsafe as a self-authenticating narrative, if the protest is corroborated, is the institutional account of the disputed interpersonal and causal state:

- whether the patient accepted Podiatry as sufficient;
- whether the patient was satisfied with the discharge handling;
- whether silence demonstrates absence of dissent;
- whether “good understanding” can be read as agreement with the architecture;
- whether the clinician was unaware that the patient regarded the feet as inadequately addressed;
- whether the completed discharge is neutral and complete on the decision process;
- whether later summaries that inherit those assumptions can be treated as independent evidence of the original patient position.

This is targeted impeachment, not wholesale rejection.

It is also why later referrals and specialist correspondence containing clinician-authored narrative require provenance analysis. A scan result is one thing. A referral that selects and frames the clinical story is another.

The question becomes:

> **What problem was the later expert actually asked to solve, and what material patient-position information had already been filtered out of the inherited narrative?**

# Part XIV — The missing adjudicative layer

## 21. The strongest evidential task is now to locate the middle

The governance stress test ended with a powerful finding:

> The observable eight-year outcome is highly compatible with a system in which local evidence repeatedly updated treatment while no mechanism reliably required global updating of the inherited clinical narrative.

That structure can arise from ordinary fragmentation, hierarchy, documentation inertia, diffuse ownership and prospective workflow.

It can also contain narrower conscious decisions at particular moments.

The key is not to assume motive from structure.

The evidential task is to locate the **missing adjudicative layer**:

- Who reconsidered the protest after 10 July 2017?
- Was the senior/collective architecture revisited?
- Was Podiatry consciously reaffirmed after the challenge?
- Was the patient's disagreement communicated to anyone else?
- What happened in the interval before completion of the discharge narrative?
- Did the procyclidine response trigger any integrated review?
- Did Healy's treatment language trigger comparison with the pre-existing Galway policy?
- Did the 2020 parkinsonian foot finding go upstream?
- Did the 2021 Sinemet transition include any phenotype-based response design?
- Did the 2023 OFF assessment trigger a proposition-level reconstruction?
- Did the 2025 CR response trigger retrospective review?
- Did the 2025–26 complaint/governance process independently reconstruct the hinge, or inherit the same source state?

At each point the possibilities are finite:

1. the issue was not reconsidered;
2. it was reconsidered but not documented;
3. it was reconsidered and the original position consciously retained;
4. evidence of reconsideration exists but has not yet been disclosed or located.

Those possibilities are much more useful to counsel than a broad allegation of concealment.

They produce specific disclosure and witness targets.

# Part XV — What would have to happen for the observed architecture to persist?

## 22. The minimum information-management structure

Stripped of motive, the observed outcome requires something close to the following architecture.

### A. The original dissent does not become a durable longitudinal variable

The protest need not be expressly denied.

It merely has to disappear from the inherited summary.

Future clinicians then receive:

> uncertainty + Podiatry + treatment plan

rather than:

> uncertainty + Podiatry + patient expressly disputes the adequacy of the partition.

That changes the baseline from which all future evidence is interpreted.

### B. `Not clear` has no falsification criterion

The phrase remains compatible with virtually every later development.

No one has to decide what would make it materially clearer.

### C. Treatment response is treated as a treatment fact, not a model-testing fact

Procyclidine response can justify procyclidine adjustment.

OFF-related pain can justify Stalevo.

Morning foot pain can justify CR.

None of those acts automatically requires historical reconstruction.

### D. Synthesis remains diffuse

Podiatry sees structure.

Nurses see timing.

The GP sees suffering.

The consultant sees Parkinsonism and receives the local observations.

External specialists see the problem as framed in referrals.

No mechanism forces one person to assemble all domains into a proposition-level longitudinal review.

### E. Multiple explanations remain concurrently available

Structural pathology and neurological responsiveness are not mutually exclusive.

That genuine complexity can stabilise the system indefinitely unless somebody asks what proportion of the disabling phenotype is being misallocated or whether the old division still does useful work.

### F. Each later event is treated prospectively

2020: what do we do now?

2021: what drug do we substitute now?

2023: what do we prescribe now?

2025: what formulation will cover the morning now?

The threatening question is retrospective:

> **What does this accumulating evidence tell us about the unresolved July 2017 question?**

If the workflow never requires that question, the past remains protected by default.

### G. The explicit synthesis event never occurs

The one institutional event capable of breaking the architecture would be a genuine reconstruction:

> Rebuild the original foot problem, patient objection, Podiatry pathway, medication perturbations, dystonia evidence, OFF phenomena, specialist advice, levodopa history and later response. Now decide whether the original allocation remains defensible and update the record accordingly.

The presently available record does not identify such an event.

# Part XVI — Ordinary failure and conscious preservation must remain distinct

## 23. What ordinary healthcare organisation can explain

A substantial amount of this pattern can arise innocently.

Healthcare systems are extremely capable of producing:

- specialty fragmentation;
- poor feedback loops;
- inherited problem-list labels;
- append-only documentation;
- clinicians focused on present treatment rather than historical epistemology;
- senior decisions becoming sticky;
- diffusion of responsibility despite a named consultant;
- patient disagreements disappearing during summarisation;
- no explicit reopening rules;
- local adaptation without longitudinal synthesis.

That must remain part of the case because it is both realistic and fair.

The strongest case theory does not require every actor to be incompetent or dishonest.

Indeed, the paradox is that **local decisions can be reasonable while the global system is incoherent**.

---

## 24. Where conscious decisions indisputably exist

There are nevertheless narrower moments at which somebody necessarily made a conscious clinical or documentary choice.

Someone decided discharge could proceed after the protest.

Someone authored/finalised the discharge narrative without preserving the protest.

Someone retained Podiatry as the practical foot pathway.

Someone maintained the prolonged dopamine-deferral policy into 2018 despite recognising probable PARK2 and substantial possible dopamine benefit.

Later clinicians consciously interpreted symptoms and altered medications.

Those are conscious decisions.

They do **not** by themselves prove a conscious strategy to preserve the historical architecture.

That larger proposition would require additional evidence, such as:

- contemporaneous recognition that later evidence undermined the earlier account followed by a decision not to reopen it;
- internal communications acknowledging the contradiction and choosing a narrower historical interpretation;
- instructions to preserve an existing formulation;
- repeated active rejection of requests to annotate a known dispute;
- retrospective statements demonstrably inconsistent with information known to the same author at the time;
- evidence that the omitted protest was remembered and deliberately excluded from later reconstructions.

This is where complaint, GDPR/Article 16, QPS, Medical Council and SIMT material becomes relevant: not because institutional defence itself proves bad faith, but because later notice may reveal whether the system consciously resisted giving later evidence retrospective force.

# Part XVII — The human chronology must remain visible

## 25. The feet were the interface, not an abstract symptom category

For counsel, the clinical architecture should never be allowed to erase the lived function of the feet.

The feet were where:

- standing;
- walking;
- work;
- gait initiation;
- pain;
- tension;
- dystonic movement;
- structural pathology;
- medication state;
- recreation;
- independence

met in one place.

That is why the term **interface** remains so useful.

It does not require claiming one cause for every feature.

It explains why failure to integrate the feet had consequences beyond diagnostic taxonomy.

The patient could be dramatically better globally and still organise his experience through what the feet permitted him to do.

The dog belongs here.

Darts belong here.

Bar work belongs here.

The first 150 footsteps in 2025 belong here.

The first familiar outside-left-foot pain belongs here.

The feet are not a decorative symptom in the story. They are where the disease and the patient's life repeatedly met.

## 26. The promise and the grief

The 2018 control state has a human meaning that should be preserved for counsel even though it is not itself a legal proposition.

The patient had experienced an extraordinary recovery from the state in which he entered hospital.

The neurologist could look at that improved state and say, in effect, **you could be very much better again with levodopa**.

The patient then spent years inside the understanding that the more powerful treatment was being held in reserve.

When Sinemet eventually arrived, he bought a dog and organised his life around walking.

Later, the feet became the site of severe pain, instability, temporal cycling and fear.

By 2023 the GP was describing a “death wish” and asking for urgent review before “we get into trouble here.”

By 2025 the first footsteps were “torture,” the feet were “murder,” and the patient said he would not live with it.

Then a targeted change in dopamine coverage produced profound relief.

The emotional force of that chronology is not simply retrospective anger about a prescription.

It is grief over the possibility that the system had possessed information for years that, if integrated differently, might have changed the quality and organisation of life.

Counsel should understand that this grief is part of why the patient has reconstructed the record with such intensity. It is not a substitute for evidence. It explains the human stakes of the evidential question.

# Part XVIII — The case as it should now be put before counsel

## 27. The short counsel theory

The case can now be stated without depending on a broad allegation of misdiagnosis or delayed levodopa.

> **In July 2017 Galway Neurology correctly identified severe young-onset Parkinsonism while leaving the relationship between the patient's disabling foot/lower-limb syndrome and the neurological disorder expressly unresolved. Despite that uncertainty, the operative architecture partitioned the feet toward Podiatry and adopted prolonged dopamine-replacement deferral. The patient says he directly challenged the adequacy of that disposition before discharge in front of four medical students. If corroborated, the completed discharge record materially omitted a live disagreement that should have functioned as a longitudinal reopening signal. Thereafter neurological evidence repeatedly changed the feet and changed treatment, but the available record does not show equivalent global reconsideration of the original partition. The continuity system became prospectively adaptable while remaining retrospectively non-corrigible. The central governance question is who owned the duty to integrate later evidence into the meaning of the case rather than merely into the next prescription.**

That is the strongest present formulation.

# Part XXII — The final perspective found on 14 August 2026

## 35. From “failure to reassess” to “ownership of meaning”

The conversation began with familiar themes:

- levodopa deferral;
- Healy's advice;
- procyclidine response;
- the omitted protest;
- Podiatry;
- later pain;
- eventual CR relief.

Then the prescription record shifted the focus.

It showed an active treatment apparatus in which multiple people could execute changes while the same continuity consultant remained over the case.

That generated a new question:

> **What was the continuity role actually for?**

The answer was not “person who personally writes every prescription.”

It was “person who owns synthesis.”

Then the 2018 note and the dog supplied the human control state: the patient had recovered dramatically, was told he could be very much better with levodopa later, and when levodopa eventually arrived he built a walking life around that expectation.

Then the protest was reconsidered not only as omitted evidence but as an **institutional event**.

The question became:

> **What happened in the moment after the protest?**

That opened the missing adjudicative layer.

The hospital had a live challenge to a still-unresolved disposition. If the plan remained unchanged, a well-governed system would be expected to show that the challenge had been heard, reconsidered, reasoned through, preserved and attached to reopening criteria.

The record instead shows the architecture and not the bridge.

Then the reverse-engineering exercise revealed the deepest mechanism:

> **To produce eight years of local adaptation without global reconstruction, the system needed no grand conspiracy. It needed a stable foundational narrative, divided ownership, no owner of the cross-domain proposition, no reopening rule, distributed treatment authority, append-only documentation, senior-policy stickiness, pragmatic use of new evidence for treatment, and no mechanism forcing that evidence to operate retrospectively.**

That is the final perspective.

The case is no longer best organised around a single missed diagnosis or delayed drug.

It is organised around **ownership of meaning**.

Who had the duty to say:

> This new observation is not merely something I can treat. It changes what I should think about the case.

Who had the duty to take the feet from being a local symptom back into the longitudinal model?

Who had the duty to carry changed information upstream through the authority structure?

Who had the duty to ensure that a patient's challenge did not vanish from the history later clinicians inherited?

Who had the duty to define what would make `Not clear` clearer?

Who had the duty to permit the present to revise the interpretation of an unresolved past?

That duty is conspicuously difficult to locate in the surviving record.

And that is now the most powerful perspective found in the case.

# Part XXIII — A counsel-facing formulation of the institutional mechanism

## 36. The mechanism in one page

The proposed institutional mechanism is:

1. **An unresolved integrated problem enters hospital.**
2. **A practical partition is created before the relationship is resolved.**
3. **A senior-linked dopamine-deferral architecture reduces the immediate need to test the disputed domain through ordinary dopamine replacement.**
4. **The patient directly challenges the adequacy of the partition before discharge.**
5. **The durable record does not preserve that challenge.**
6. **The omitted challenge therefore fails to become a standing reopening query for later readers.**
7. **Neurological medication subsequently changes the same domain.**
8. **Each new observation can be acted upon locally because the system remains clinically responsive.**
9. **No explicit rule requires the local observation to be accumulated against the original partition.**
10. **Treatment execution becomes distributed across clinicians and services while synthesis remains diffuse.**
11. **The continuity consultant remains over the case but the record does not demonstrate repeated proposition-level reconstruction.**
12. **The feet therefore become increasingly important to treatment while remaining comparatively weak as historical evidence.**
13. **By 2025 the patient's lived temporal account finally governs a targeted dopamine formulation and produces profound relief.**
14. **The therapeutic success is accommodated prospectively without an identified global reconstruction of the 2017 question.**
15. **The system has therefore learned how to treat the evidence without visibly allowing the evidence to change the history.**

This is **local updating without global updating**.

It is also **prospective adaptability with retrospective non-corrigibility**.

And, from the patient's perspective, it is the experience of being trapped inside an institutional account from which his original objection had been removed.

# Part XXIV — The final counsel question

## 37. The question that organises the whole file

Counsel should be able to hold the case in one question:

> **Who owned the duty to change the meaning of the case, rather than merely change the patient's medication?**

If the answer is **Monaghan**, the continuity duty becomes central.

If the answer is **Counihan / the senior architecture**, the missing senior reconsideration records become central.

If the answer is **the movement-disorders service**, the delegation and handover record becomes central.

If the answer is **the hospital as a governance system**, the absence of any mechanism forcing reopening becomes central.

If responsibility was genuinely shared, counsel should ask why shared responsibility became **unowned responsibility**.

That question connects every part of the case:

- the GP's integrated referral;
- `Not clear`;
- Podiatry;
- the levodopa-deferral architecture;
- the protest;
- the omission;
- procyclidine;
- Healy;
- the 2018 “very much better” statement;
- the 2021 Sinemet design;
- Browne and Folan;
- the 2023 safety crisis;
- the 2025 CR response;
- the later complaint/rectification struggle;
- the 2026 PRKN confirmation.

It explains why all those apparently separate disputes are one dispute.

They concern the same longitudinal question:

> **Was new information allowed to govern the model, or only the next action taken inside the model?**

# Closing statement

This reconstruction does not require counsel to begin by accusing anyone of dishonesty.

It asks for something more basic and more testable.

A patient entered Neurology with an integrated, disabling foot–gait–tone problem. The hospital identified severe Parkinsonism but did not resolve the relationship of the feet to it. A practical separation and prolonged dopamine-deferral strategy were adopted. The patient says he challenged that arrangement before discharge in front of identifiable witnesses. The durable record does not preserve the challenge. Thereafter the same clinical domain repeatedly generated neurological and medication-state information, often sufficient to change treatment, but the available record does not show equivalent reconsideration of the historical allocation.

The result is an eight-year architecture in which the institution can remain active, responsive and locally rational while failing at the level of longitudinal synthesis.

The strongest question is not whether every earlier decision was wrong.

It is whether the hospital preserved a system in which earlier uncertainty remained **corrigible**.

If the answer is no, the mechanism matters before motive does.

If the student witnesses establish the protest, the next question becomes unavoidable:

> **What happened between that bedside confrontation and the completion of the durable discharge narrative?**

And the final question is the one the present record has not yet answered:

> **Who owned the duty to change the meaning of the case?**
