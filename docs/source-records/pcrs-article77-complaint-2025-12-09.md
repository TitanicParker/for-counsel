# 9 December 2025 — Article 77 GDPR complaint concerning PCRS

**Source type:** primary data-subject regulatory complaint  
**Controller complained of:** HSE Primary Care Reimbursement Service (PCRS)  
**Controller reference:** `DP250646`  
**Date submitted:** 9 December 2025  
**Provenance:** complaint email and attached PDF read directly from the connected Gmail account.  
**Status:** redacted source summary. The original email and PDF remain controlling. No analytical findings are made on this page.

---

Liam Moloney submitted a formal complaint to the Data Protection Commission under Article 77 GDPR concerning PCRS's processing of Long Term Illness scheme data.

The complaint invoked Articles 5(1)(d), 12, 15, 16 and 18 and alleged, among other things:

- inaccurate or misleading special-category health data concerning the date associated with Parkinson's disease / LTI eligibility;
- failure to take reasonable steps to verify the disputed date;
- refusal of rectification;
- failure to implement restriction while accuracy was contested;
- and deficiencies in transparency and access handling.

The complaint contrasted a PCRS entry associated with **9 July 2017** with hospital documentation which the complainant said showed clinical confirmation on **13 July 2017**.

It recorded a controller-side chronology beginning with a 16 November 2025 rights request and culminating in the PCRS position of 8 December 2025 that the 9 July date remained accurate **“in the context of the LTI scheme”**, with the later clinical-confirmation date added as an annotation.

The complaint asked the DPC to investigate PCRS compliance, require verification of the disputed date with the source controller if appropriate, require rectification/restriction where warranted, and address the access questions raised.

---

## Source-status boundary

This document is primary evidence of **what the data subject alleged and asked the DPC to determine on 9 December 2025**.

It is not a DPC finding that PCRS data were inaccurate, that the LTI chronology asserted by the complainant is legally or clinically correct, that PCRS breached any GDPR provision, or that the requested corrective measures are required.
