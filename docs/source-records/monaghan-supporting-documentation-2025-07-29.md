## Reproduced 2017 clinical record

The working clinical file reproduces the 2017 presentation and examination, including:

- longstanding lower-limb tension and later corns/calluses and foot pain;
- markedly increased tone, gross bradykinesia and a strikingly abnormal gait;
- a footdrop-like appearance described as somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture and expressly **not foot drop**;
- pes planus and hammertoes;
- severe young-onset parkinsonism with an open atypical/genetic differential;
- later abnormal DaTSCAN evidence of bilateral dopaminergic deficit.

The same reproduced 2017 record states that the leg symptoms, corns/calluses and burning **“may have been completely separate”** or possibly related to a peripheral neuropathy and records the conclusion **“Not clear.”**

The plan then separately requests outpatient Chiropody/Podiatry services and records that an SHO had tried to arrange this in hospital.

### Source boundary

This bundle is a 2025 reproduction of the clinician's working record. It is primary evidence of what Dr Monaghan supplied in 2025 and strong documentary evidence of the content of his retained working file. Where the repository already holds contemporaneous 2017 source pages, those earlier records remain the preferable source for proving the 2017 event itself.

## 2017 treatment reasoning and genetics

The reproduced 2017 material records a plan to proceed cautiously with conventional dopaminergic therapy because of Liam's very young age and concern about dyskinesia and impulse-control complications. It also records that the case had been considered at a Neurology Complex Case meeting and that care with later levodopa/dopamine-agonist treatment had been advised with Prof Counihan and Neurology colleagues.

The same 2017 working record expressly raises a possible **PRKN / PARK2** explanation because of the very young onset, symmetry, akinetic-rigid presentation, lack of a dominant family history and preserved cognition. It records that PRKN/PARK2 testing or a broader panel was a reasonable option and refers to Prof Healy's interest in that possibility.

### Source boundary

The 2017 material establishes that PRKN/PARK2 was a contemporaneous diagnostic possibility, not that a PRKN diagnosis had been made. The later 2026 specialist genetic result is a separate evidential origin establishing the eventual genetic diagnosis.

## April 2018 Procyclidine withdrawal history

The working file reproduces the 24 April 2018 Neurology record. It states that the Parkinson nurse had suggested stopping Procyclidine for approximately two weeks in Sept/Oct 2017 because of blurred vision and that, during that stop:

> “the first thing he noticed changing and changing rapidly was the return on the foot, of the outside left corn.”

The same passage records that symptoms worsened somewhat and that Liam was more **“shifty”** and **“unrelaxed”** off Procyclidine.

This is the same early treatment-response episode separately represented in the repository's 2018 Neurology source page and contemporaneous 24 October 2017 patient email.

---

## 2020 lower-limb / foot findings

The working file reproduces the 2020 Neurology material describing:

- a bad corn on the outside of the left foot;
- a left lower-limb “throw” with preserved dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion power;
- the clinician's conclusion that this foot-throw appearance was parkinsonian rather than true foot drop;
- exquisite sensitivity of the feet;
- excessive movement and urge to move the feet.

These are later direct clinical observations and should not be treated as retrospective proof that every component of the 2017 foot syndrome was neurological.

---

## October 2021 Sinemet and DBS material

The working file reproduces the October 2021 clinical record stating that ordinary Sinemet had first been introduced in September 2021 and increased as Kemadrin was withdrawn because of ocular-pressure/visual concerns.

The same record says Liam had been fully counselled and would receive a copy at home, followed by the categorical statement:

> “the DBS is only ever for the tremor element. It will not help with anything else.”

A narrower patient-specific clinical judgment may have been intended, but this bundle preserves the categorical wording actually recorded and supplied to the patient.

---

## March 2025 complaint-response material

The opening complaint response states that Sinemet had been known to reduce Liam's experience of pain and that Dr Monaghan's own 10 March 2025 Sinemet CR prescription was **“also to try to help the morning experience including of pain.”**

It further records that the recent Sinemet CR escalation had provided **“profound relief currently.”**

The clinician's explanation remains that pain could have mixed or peripheral causes and that Parkinsonism/dopaminergic state could magnify the experience of pain; the response also preserves the stated rationale for cautious dose escalation in a young patient because of dyskinesia and dopamine-dysregulation/impulse-control risks.

## Information supplied to the patient

Across the working file Dr Monaghan repeatedly states that his long clinic letters were intended to be copied to Liam and to create a shared understanding of what had been discussed, why decisions had been made and when.

This is evidence about the information framework the treating clinician says he deliberately supplied. It does not prove that Liam agreed with every proposition or that every discussion was fully understood in the same way by clinician and patient.
