Dear Mr. Moloney,

Thank you for your correspondence.

The Quality & Patient Safety Department has considered the governance issues raised. The complaint assessment and subsequent SIMT review were undertaken using the information, clinical presentations, and investigations available at the time, and the determinations reached were appropriate, specific to a predefined timeframe, and compliant with established governance frameworks. A subsequent diagnosis of dystonia in December 2025 does not undermine the validity of those processes or outcomes. The SIMT assesses the quality and appropriateness of care based on contemporaneous data.

In that regard, the updated diagnosis does not necessitate reopening governance reviews, altering prior determinations, or retrospectively amending historical medical records. Updated diagnoses supersede earlier working diagnoses clinically, but do not render prior governance processes unlawful or unsafe. On this basis, QPS does not identify a current governance risk arising from continued reliance on the completed processes, nor is escalation to another governance function indicated.

Thank you for bringing these matters to our attention and for the opportunity to respond.

Kind regards,

Emily Comerford
