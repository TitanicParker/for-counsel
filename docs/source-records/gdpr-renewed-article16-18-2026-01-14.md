Following DPC correspondence, Liam re-engaged with GUH and expressly framed the message as a renewed Article 16 request read with Articles 5(1)(d), 12, 18, 19 and 24 GDPR. He asserted that the Article 18 restriction invoked on 4 August 2025 remained in force and asked the controller to reconcile disputed data through rectification, annotation, qualification or lawful refusal.

The request relied on a later dystonia diagnosis and argued that this changed the accuracy/completeness position concerning historic records and downstream governance use. It also alleged that SIMT had relied on disputed data while restriction was asserted.

The requested reconciliation included the continued representation of Parkinson’s disease, omitted presenting symptoms/dystonia-consistent gait features, later pharmacological evidence, SIMT reliance on disputed data, and the characterisation of earlier rectification requests.

**Source-boundary note:** This source proves the renewed request, the allegations advanced and the continued Article 18 position asserted by the data subject. It does not prove that the later diagnosis had the legal effect claimed, that restriction had been accepted/implemented by the controller, or that SIMT processing breached GDPR.
