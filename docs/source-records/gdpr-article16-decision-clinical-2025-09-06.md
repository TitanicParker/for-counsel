Dear Mr Moloney,

I refer to your request made on 12th June, 2025 for the erasure of a record, held in your medical file at Galway University Hospital. Firstly, I wish to apologise for the delay in issuing you a decision.

Your request in summary refers to the following:

1. “Rectify all relevant documents where the absence of my core symptoms (severe, disabling foot pain) misrepresents my diagnostic journey.”
2. “Annotate all files (referrals, discharge summaries, diagnostic letters) that present a conclusive Parkinson’s diagnosis, noting the lack of dopaminergic diagnostic trial and subsequent evidence in 2025.”

The decision has been made that the personal information contained in your medical records to which your application refers is not judged to be misleading for the following reasons.

The HSE relies on Articles 6 and 9 of the GDPR as the legal basis for processing a patient’s personal and medical data. Article 6(1)(e) of GDPR is relied upon for processing the individual’s personal data which states that processing shall be lawful if “processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller”. For the HSE this lawful authority is vested in us through the Health Act 2004.

Sections 36(1) & 52(1) of the Act cover suitable and specific measures of processing and of special categories of personal data for the provision of healthcare. I am satisfied that the HSE has met its legal requirements under the Act and is in compliance with GDPR.

A diagnosis in a medical file is the reflection of the professional opinion of the doctor at that moment of time and based on the information available to that moment in time. On this basis the HSE has a lawful basis for continued processing of your medical records in accordance with Article 7(1) of GDPR.

As a consequence, it remains factually accurate to record the opinion or diagnosis that was made and what the opinion or diagnosis was, such data would not be “inaccurate” as it is a factually accurate reflection that an opinion or diagnosis occurred and what the opinion or diagnosis was at that point in time and is still necessary in relation to the purposes for which the personal data was originally collected or otherwise processed and is consistent with Article 17(3)(c) in providing comprehensive medical assessment and treatment of the individual.

In addition, a medical opinion or diagnosis provides important context for any subsequent medical treatment decisions made by Health Care providers. A prior opinion or diagnosis may explain why certain medications were prescribed or certain treatment plans were undertaken. Maintaining the medical record in its current form, serves the public interest by ensuring the service user and the treating Healthcare Professionals have a reliable record of what has taken place.

Following consideration of the provisions of the above legislation, it has been decided to refuse your request, as there is an obligation on the HSE and its staff to keep accurate records which are essential for the continuity of care and comprehensive enough to allow Health Practitioners to be fully informed of factors which impact on the provision of care to be provided.

Rights to appeal

If you are unhappy with this decision you may make a complaint in writing to the Office of the Data Protection Commission.

Nuala Madden  
SARS Co-ordinator,
