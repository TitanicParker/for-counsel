Dear Ms Fallon,

Thank you for your letter.

I am of course personally devastated to be in receipt of this.

However that is not what matters: It is the experience of the person that is at the heart of this that matters, and it is the assessment and the judgement of the Medical Council in your role to protect patients, that matters.

Mr Moloney made a complaint to University Hospital Galway in March 2025. I answered this immediately to the best of my ability to the Hospital, and in the knowledge that that reply would be copied to Mr Moloney. Mr Moloney had specified he did not want to have contact with clinicians until he was in receipt of what he requested.

The Hospital supplied me with a subsequent complaint on the 24th of April 2025. Due to technical issues, I was delayed in sending the reply to the Hospital in respect of this until the evening of the 25th of April 2025.

On the 28th of April 2025, while the second complaint was in process with University Hospital Galway, I was informed by the Hospital of a third complaint - a re-framing as he explained of the first complaint.

In answering each of the first two complaints: I have at length explained my thinking and my medical management of Mr Moloney's medical needs as per attached.

I believe these to have been correct and appropriate.

I understand and respect that Mr Moloney does not agree.

If I may summarise what I believe to be my understanding of his position in the complaints received in the Hospital: this is that I have framed everything in the context of an unproven neuropathy, being the cause of his symptoms, and I have “blamed alcohol”.

I think there is prima facie evidence as per the documentation enclosed, that this is not correct.

I think the detailed explanations explored in my responses to his complaints make this clear.

In point of fact Mr Moloney has been on treatment with Levodopa (Sinemet) since 2021 and in another forms, and we have known and documented that his has had a beneficial response for him in terms of pain. We had also tried to used synthetic Dopamine agents since 2018 (discontinued due to adverse effect/intolerances related to dopaminergic treatment)

His contention would seem to be that “Sinemet CR” (Controlled release Levodopa) started earlier would have given him sooner relief of pain and other leg based symptoms).

To be of assistance to the Medical Council I have enclosed all of the documentation to answer Mr Moloney's complaints, that I have supplied to the University Hospital Galway.

In Particular there is:

- SECTION 1 - The reply to Mr Moloney's first complaint. This explains in detail my clinical reasoning.
- This also includes (APPENDIX 1) the working document of his clinical records that I have going back to 2017.
- I asked the Hospital to provide my response to his complaint letter, unedited and un-redacted to Mr Moloney (as opposed to a draft of a Hospital Response letter formulated incorporating parts of my answers). I asked the Hospital to provide all of my working file - he requested his data, and he is of course completely entitled to this. (APPENDIX 1). This was to save Mr Moloney having to go through a separate FoI process, so he could have much of the data that he requested, and at speed.
- In APPENDIX 2 - I have enclosed Mr Moloney's original complaint to the Hospital.
- In APPENDIX 3 - I have the reply that was formulated to his additional complaint and his additional questions, when Mr Moloney expressed he was dissatisfied with the Hospital's response and closure of his case (sent by me to the Hospital on the 25th of April 2025).
- In APPENDIX 4 - I have the reply formulated to Mr Moloney's re-framing third complaint to the Hospital. It was at this time I became aware of Mr Moloney's letter to a Neurology colleague Prof Timothy J Counihan, of his concerns as regards Prof Counihan’s advice to myself (in 2017 when we had reviewed Mr Moloney's care at our Neurology Dept Weekly Complex Case meeting). Prof Counihan had no further involvement formally or otherwise in the management of Mr Moloney’s care. Prof Counihan’s advice to myself of that time was only what I already knew to be the case: the necessity for care in the commencement/escalation of Levodopa treatment in a younger person with Parkinsons Disease.
- It was at this time (28th April 2025) that I became aware of the complaint that had been made to the Medical Council, and accordingly a draft of this current document was commenced as of the 28th of April 2025, in anticipation of your now current request of 29th July 2025.
- APPENDIX 5 - Email to the Directorate of Medicine UHG
- APPENDIX 6 - GDPR request for Mr Moloney to have his Medical Records Changed or Annotated.
- (Appendix 7 - the Medical Council complaint - not enclosed, this document is reply)

I accept I may be wrong - but I think and hope that my management was correct.

From 2017, from Mr Moloney's very first inpatient evaluation - I had noted and explored with him that we would try to delay treatment with Levodopa for as long as possible, and then we would also try to delay escalation.

Indeed it was also discussed back then that he may not be happy with this delay.

This was in an effort to protect his overall health and wellbeing in the most holistic of manners. This was only done sincerely, and with his best interests at heart.

Specifically this was to avoid the risk of Dopamine treatment related complications, such as Dyskinesias (abnormal movements); Dopaminergic treatment related Impulse Control disorders / Dopa Dysregulation Syndrome.

Quite simply there is no other conceivable reason that a doctor could or would wish to delay treatment with an agent for symptomatic relief, other than to try to protect the person from adverse outcome.

This was my goal: to try to protect him from the effects of a treatment that we all knew and understood that Mr Moloney would come to need more and more of over time.

As the extensive documentation provided will show evidence of, at every step of the process in his annual reviews, everything that I explored and explained was as documented.

In each instance Mr Moloney was kept aware of everything by this being explained, and his being copied on his own letters.

In the rest of this particular document, to you as the Medical Council, I have not sought to answer the specifics of Mr Moloney’s complaints as we understood them already.

This is simply because I have explored this complex matter which has extended over the 8 years since I have known Mr Moloney, in the detailed answers to his complaints as attached in the associated document.

I have left this in because there has been an evolution of the complaints that Mr Moloney has made.

I am ethically bound and I understand legally bound in terms of "open disclosure" to lay out all the facts/interpretations as I know them to be.

Because Mr Moloney’s complaints to the Medical Council may further evolve, beyond the documentation you provided me with dated the 25th of April, I have therefore enclosed all of this documentation (clinical and complaint management), in the interests of full disclosure.

However to more specifically address aspects of the items from the complaint dated 25th of April 2025, that the Medical Council has supplied me with today 2025-07-29th

## Mr Moloney’s Complaint “II The Core Admission”

I am a general neurologist in public practice only. I have worked in UHG since 2012 (13 years as a neurologist). I have worked only in Neurology since July 2002 (i.e. for 23 years)

It is the normal expectation of any general neurologist. that they would be used to managing patients with a wide range of neurological conditions.

If I may give another example, in our own department we do not have a specific "multiple sclerosis" neurologist, but all of my colleagues manage patients with multiple sclerosis, young and old.

While there is one Neurologist in our department with a background in movement disorders, as you can understand: it would be impossible for one person to be able to manage everybody in our region with Parkinson's and other movement disorders.

(To my regret, it is now my understanding that Mr Moloney has made a Medical Council complaint against that Neurologist as well, simply on the basis of an original discussion of the need for care with Levodopa Sinemet therapy).

Furthermore in that complaint Mr Moloney states that no referral was made to a movement disorder specialist.

This is not correct.

Mr Moloney was referred on the basis of his very young age to Professor Dan Healy, Consultant Neurologist at Beaumont Hospital, for a second opinion.

## Mr Moloney’s Complaint “III Professional and legal obligations triggered.”

I can only say that I felt that Mr Moloney's management with myself was within the limits of my competence.

## Mr Moloney’s Complaint “IV What was not done.”

As explained just above Mr Moloney was seen by Professor Dan Healy.

As regards the description of Mr Moloney’s “Section IV 2. No Trial of First Line Therapy”

It was carefully explained and explored with Mr Moloney at the very beginning why we would try to defer and limit the initiation of Sinemet.

As noted above, this was to try to protect Mr Moloney from the risks of Levodopa adverse effects such as impulse control disorders, dopamine dysregulation syndrome, dyskinesia (an abnormal movement disorder).

It was explained from the outset that sooner or later this type of treatment (Sinemet Levodopa/carbidopa) would be necessary and commenced and indeed it was explained at the very beginning that Mr Moloney may then wish that we had done this earlier.

The Levodopa commencement was in 2021 (Sinemet in the ordinary form, not controlled release). Other dopamine “based” treatment had been tried already since November 2018.

I feel that all of the other aspects of section 4 are answered elsewhere.

## Mr Moloney’s Complaint “V What harm followed.”

I think that all of the aspects of this section are answered in the associated documentation.

If I may observe, it was ourselves that had prescribed the Sinemet CR in March 2025.

May I also note once again that in reality, Sinemet had been started approximately 4 years earlier already in 2021.

In this section 5 Mr Moloney talks of "the working diagnosis of 'alcohol related peripheral neuropathy'".

I think it is clear from the associated documentation as per the evidence enclosed, that we had discussed the risk of a peripheral neuropathy and the worry that Mr Moloney may have a peripheral neuropathy.

I think it will be clear to the Medical Council that there was not the diagnosis of a peripheral neuropathy to the exclusion of other considerations nor his Parkinsons disease.

This was even to the extent that tests were done for example for the overlap disorder of Fabry's disease wherein a person may have peripheral neuropathic features and Parkinsonism.

## Mr Moloney’s Complaint “VI Why this demands Scrutiny.”

In section B where Mr Moloney states "this was not caution - it was negligence".

"A subspecialist was never brought in"

- as detailed above this is not true.

"A first line treatment Levodopa was never offered".

- This is not true. Levodopa Sinemet ordinary form had been used since 2021, and Dopamine based treatment had been tried in Nevember 2018.

"Unproven diagnosis was never revisited".

- It was not definitively stated that Mr Moloney had a peripheral neuropathy. But it was I think it will be agreed a reasonable concern. I think that the enclosed documentation will prove to the satisfaction of the Medical Council and it is Expert Designates that we had Mr Moloney's diagnosis of Parkinson's front and central in every clinical consideration.

I have always myself seen Mr Moloney usually mainly on my own at his clinic visits. I have arranged his appointments in more recent years separate to the usual clinic so that he might have more time.

As per my response to his complaint it would always have been my view that we got on very well and that we were on the best of terms, with a really very good relationship.

I deeply regret this outcome on a personal and a professional level.

I will provide you with whatever else that the Medical Council request / require, as I am obliged to do, to assist you in any way possible with this matter.

I will of course be subject to whatever your determination is, and whatever your judgement will be in this matter.

I have used the Hospital email for this letter as opposed to my usual commercial email provider address (as is registered with the Medial Council), simply for data protection reasons.

I do hope this is of assistance.

If you have any questions or concerns please do not hesitate to contact me.

Thank you,

Yours sincerely,

Dr Thomas Monaghan MB BCh BAO (NUI 1999), MRCPI  
Consultant Neurologist
