Dear Data Protection Officer,

I am writing under Article 16 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) to formally request rectification and annotation of inaccurate personal data held about me within Galway University Hospitals.

Between 2017 and 2025, I was under the care of your Neurology Department, where I was assigned a diagnosis of idiopathic Parkinson’s disease. However, this diagnosis was reached without incorporating or exploring my primary presenting symptom: bilateral foot pain, which began in 2004–2005 and was the basis for my GP referral in 2017.

In 2025, following my own adjustment of dopaminergic medication (Sinemet CR), I experienced immediate and sustained relief of that symptom — effectively disproving the previously presumed peripheral neuropathy and revealing that the symptom was dopamine-responsive all along.

This finding invalidates critical assumptions underpinning the diagnosis recorded and disseminated across my medical history.

## GDPR Grounds

Under Article 16 of GDPR, I am entitled to request rectification where my data is:

- Inaccurate (e.g., omission of key symptom in referral/consultation notes),
- Misleading (e.g., implication of peripheral neuropathy without confirmatory testing),
- Or incomplete in a clinically significant way (e.g., diagnosis recorded as conclusive despite absence of diagnostic levodopa trial).

The long-standing diagnostic label in my file — and its downstream replication — does not reflect the full, accurate clinical picture, especially now that a dopamine-responsive mechanism has been demonstrated.

## Requested Actions

1. Rectify all relevant documents where the absence of my core symptom (severe, disabling foot pain) misrepresents my diagnostic journey.
2. Annotate all files (referrals, discharge summaries, diagnostic letters) that present a conclusive Parkinson’s diagnosis, noting the lack of dopaminergic diagnostic trial and the subsequent contradictory evidence in 2025.
3. Preserve all internal and external records relating to my case for further review, including MDT notes, email correspondence, and meeting records.

This request is made in good faith, in pursuit of factual accuracy and the correction of records that continue to influence how my health condition is understood, managed, and recorded.

Please confirm receipt of this request and respond within the statutory time frame of one calendar month.

Sincerely,  
Liam Moloney
