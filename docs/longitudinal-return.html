2020–2024

The inherited classification is tested again and again

The later years matter because the unresolved 2017 foot/lower-limb question kept returning inside Neurology. Each return asks the same forensic question: did new evidence reopen the inherited classification, or was it interpreted within it?

2020: the lower limb is again read neurologically

By 2020 the record again contains an outside-left-foot corn, exquisite sensitivity of the feet and abnormal lower-limb movement. A left-sided “throw” resembling foot drop was expressly characterised as parkinsonian because power for dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion was normal.

The point is not to project that later opinion backwards as proof of the 2017 mechanism. Its significance is structural: the lower limb continued to generate neurological interpretations years after the original foot/Podiatry split.

2021–2022: levodopa finally enters the treatment history

Sinemet was introduced in 2021 as the first levodopa drug and escalated cautiously. The risk architecture that had governed 2017 remained visible: young age, dyskinesia/chorea and concern about long-term dopaminergic complications.

By 2022 the patient described the feet as taking twenty to twenty-five minutes to “warm up”, persistent soreness around corns and calluses, and near-resolution of cramping. Medication was adjusted around functional demands.

This is exactly the sort of evidence that an inherited classification must be able to absorb without becoming self-confirming. Some observations look treatment-state dependent; others remain plainly compatible with local mechanical pathology.

2023: the old disagreement becomes explicit

In 2023 the record no longer merely contains parallel foot and Parkinsonian phenomena. It expressly records a “misalignment” about how the feet should be understood. Severe bilateral foot pain was a major problem. Possible OFF dystonia was considered. The history also recorded that the very first symptoms had been in the feet.

A separate urgent GP referral described severe bilateral burning pain in both feet and lower legs and asked for urgent review. Parkinson nursing input considered OFF-related reduced mobility and shuffling, and Stalevo was introduced.

By this stage the question first left “Not clear” in 2017 had re-entered the record in a more explicit form: the patient and clinician were no longer simply describing symptoms; they were disagreeing about the hierarchy of explanation.

The same year contains evidence against a simple dopamine story

The record also preserves an important counterpoint. During one ten-day period off Parkinson medication, the patient reported no difference “from a Parkinson’s perspective” while severe foot and leg pain persisted.

That evidence matters because it prevents the later history from being rewritten as a clean, continuous demonstration that all foot pain tracked dopamine state. It supports a mixed or state-dependent syndrome in which mechanical, neuropathic, dystonic and Parkinson-related effects may coexist.

2024: local pain treatment helps while movement phenomena persist

Lyrica substantially reduced pain by 2024, particularly on the right, while some left-sided pain and toe curling remained. Evening dopamine timing continued to matter. The record therefore continues to resist a single-cause explanation.

The question is not whether non-dopaminergic benefit disproves neurological involvement. It is whether the persistence of mixed evidence should have triggered a fresh synthesis rather than continued inheritance of the earlier classification.

The path-dependency question

Once the 2017 record had consolidated diagnosis, treatment rationale, LTI, Podiatry and apparent patient understanding into a completed documentary object, later clinicians did not begin with an empty sheet. They inherited a history that already looked organised.

That matters because reopening is not the same cognitive task as making the first diagnosis. New facts have to overcome an existing explanatory structure. A later foot phenomenon can therefore be recorded, treated and even recognised as unusual without necessarily disturbing the classification already in place.

Longitudinal test. What new evidence entered at each stage, and did it cause the original 2017 “Not clear → Podiatry” relationship to be reconsidered as a live diagnostic question?

Why 2025 is different

By March 2025 the issue changed character. The feet were again the dominant clinical problem, but this time the record expressly linked relief to Parkinson medication and then deliberately introduced Sinemet CR into the severe morning-foot problem. The later reported response was profound enough to alter the patient’s own understanding of the preceding years.

That is why March 2025 is not merely another entry in the chronology. It is the point at which the inherited classification meets a therapeutic event with enough force to produce a new historical interpretation.
