# Patient-Centred Causal Ruling — Feet and Levodopa, 2021–2025

**Date:** 13 August 2026

## Core ruling

The feet were the principal functional and experiential interface with Liam's condition. This does **not** mean every corn, callus, pain sensation, curl, posture or gait abnormality had one neurological cause. It means the feet were where structural pathology, pain, abnormal movement, medication state, standing and walking repeatedly converged into lived disability.

The strongest fair causal conclusion is:

> **Operational partition and insufficient use of Liam's longitudinal foot-state information probably contributed to delayed recognition and testing of an effective levodopa formulation. Systematic foot-centred monitoring would plausibly, and moderately probably, have exposed a medication-timing mismatch materially earlier. The documents alone do not prove that nighttime CR was clinically required in 2021, that it would have produced the eventual result then, or that the delay was negligent.**

## The original problem was integrated

The GP referral did not present two settled diagnoses. It presented feet, gait initiation, tone, clawing, dexterity and threatened function under a neurological question.

The hospital strongly identified Parkinsonism and also identified genuine structural foot pathology. But the relationship was left `Not clear`, while Podiatry became an operative parallel pathway.

Podiatry itself was appropriate. The problem is the absence, in the visible record, of:

- explicit neurological ownership of the unresolved relationship;
- an integrated synthesis plan;
- defined reopening triggers;
- structured neurological response measures for the feet;
- demonstrated retrospective re-evaluation when neurological evidence accumulated.

## The Procyclidine warning

The Procyclidine withdrawal/restoration episode is important because the disputed foot phenomenon, gait/movement state and bodily tension changed together under neurological pharmacological perturbation.

That does not prove one neurological cause for every foot feature and does not equal a levodopa trial. It does establish material neurological modifiability within the originally unified presentation.

Locally, Procyclidine was restored and adjusted. Globally, no documented reopening of the original foot/Podiatry partition has been identified.

## What 2021 does and does not prove

Ordinary Sinemet entered treatment in September 2021 in the context of perceived undertreatment and withdrawal of Kemadrin because of ocular-pressure/visual concerns. The record shows cautious titration, dyskinesia/chorea concern and general Parkinsonian management.

The visible 2021 record does **not** show the feet functioning as an explicit organising response domain. It does not identify a structured pre/post-dose framework for:

- foot pain;
- clawing/curling;
- foot throw;
- gait initiation;
- morning first steps;
- onset/wearing-off;
- the overnight medication gap.

Accordingly:

> **The 2021 regimen was not shown to have been adequately designed or evaluated around the foot phenotype. It was plausibly mismatched, but it is not demonstrably clinically wrong on the documentary record alone.**

## Correct chronology of phenotype treatment

Avoid saying absolutely that "the feet entered the levodopa model only in 2025."

The more accurate proposition is:

> **Levodopa entered the medication list in 2021; foot-relevant dopaminergic treatment became explicit by 2023; but the first visible fully articulated prescription organised around the overnight/first-step foot phenotype occurred in 2025.**

This distinction is important because it strengthens the argument rather than overstating it.

## The 2023 warning threshold

By 2023 ordinary uncertainty was no longer sufficient as a complete explanation for lack of systematic integration.

The record contained:

- severe bilateral burning feet/lower legs;
- reported major quality-of-life loss;
- suspected OFF dystonia;
- morning/later-day worsening without Sinemet;
- nurse assessment of undertreatment/OFF symptoms;
- Stalevo-facilitated movement and pain relief;
- inability to sustain later activity/walks;
- explicit clinician-patient `misalignment` over causal organisation.

There was important counterevidence: a reported ten-day medication cessation without Parkinsonian difference, genuine structural lesions, Lyrica benefit, variable patient descriptions, alcohol/cannabis confounding and dyskinesia constraints.

Those facts justified uncertainty. They did not eliminate the need for a structured test of the competing temporal model.

Thus:

> **By 2023 the feet were plainly neurological in at least part, and failure to use them systematically as a primary response domain could no longer be explained simply by absence of neurological evidence.**

Whether the professional standard required a particular intervention remains an expert question.

## What changed in 2025

The 2025 note did something qualitatively clearer.

It mapped:

- the 8 p.m. last Sinemet dose;
- the 1 a.m. bedtime;
- the 8:45 a.m. rising time;
- `murder in the feet`;
- the first 150 footsteps as `torture`;
- hobbling and difficulty tying runners;
- relief after Parkinson medication reached the system;
- the expectation that additional Sinemet would provide additional relief;
- dyskinesia as the limiting counterweight.

Nighttime controlled-release Sinemet was then prescribed around that temporal account, with an early-morning dosing option also discussed. Later `profound relief` was acknowledged.

The key change was therefore both **pharmacokinetic and epistemic**:

> **A different formulation/timing was selected because the patient's lived temporal account acquired direct authority in treatment design.**

## The patient as diagnostic instrument

The strongest supportable formulation is:

> **The principal underused diagnostic instrument was the patient himself—not because his causal interpretation had to be accepted, but because his longitudinal observations of dose onset, wearing-off, overnight absence, pain/tension/curling distinctions, first steps and functional loss were the most direct available means of designing and evaluating a medication-state test.**

The record shows extensive documentation and genuine local listening. The criticism is therefore not that clinicians never heard Liam.

It is that his information was **insufficiently allowed to organise the longitudinal clinical model**.

## Human consequence

The record does not show an uninterrupted eight-year period of identical severe pain. There were periods of improvement, work return, cycling, darts, long walks, toe-support benefit, Lyrica benefit and a 2024 statement that medication was `spot on`.

But the feet repeatedly governed work, standing, walking, recreation and autonomy. By 2023–25 the record includes debilitating bilateral burning, reported absence of quality of life, inability to sustain activity, major reduction in darts, life becoming `a lot smaller`, first steps described as `torture`, and feet as `murder`.

The death-related phrases should remain exactly source-bounded and should not be converted into a psychiatric diagnosis or an unsupported conclusion about sustained suicidal intent.

## Causal chain

The strongest documentary chain is:

> **Unified GP presentation → operational partition → no visible integration/reopening architecture → neurological medication sensitivity → accumulating parkinsonian/dystonic/OFF evidence → 2021 Sinemet without demonstrated foot-centred response design → 2023 warning threshold → 2025 patient-centred temporal formulation → targeted CR experiment → reported profound relief.**

The weakest necessary link for a strict but-for case is the counterfactual:

> Would earlier integration probably have selected, tolerated and successfully implemented an effective strategy materially sooner?

The documents make that a substantial and moderately supported possibility, but expert evidence is required to carry it across a clinical/legal causation threshold.

## Relationship to the July 2017 architecture

This causal audit complements, but does not depend upon, the organising-motive ruling.

The original failure can be explained by genuine mixed pathology, cautious prescribing, component care, anchoring and fragmentation without proving subjective self-protection.

However, the causal history also demonstrates the architecture-preserving effect:

> **facts repeatedly changed local treatment without acquiring equivalent power to reopen the inherited causal history.**

By 2023, `misalignment` proves conscious awareness of the competing foot model. Thereafter, continued historical non-integration was no longer merely unconscious lack of notice.

## Expert-evidence target

An independent movement-disorders expert should be asked to determine:

1. whether IR Sinemet with cautious titration was a reasonable 2021 strategy;
2. what foot/gait/OFF outcomes should reasonably have been measured;
3. when the accumulated evidence required structured OFF-dystonia/timing assessment;
4. what formulation/timing alternatives were reasonably available and when;
5. how dyskinesia, impulse-control, sleep and intolerance risks affected those options;
6. what the 2025 CR response says about mechanism;
7. whether earlier integrated monitoring would probably have produced meaningful durable relief and by approximately when;
8. what injury is attributable to delayed formulation rather than progression, structural disease or other factors;
9. whether care fell below the applicable professional standard.

## Current evidential ruling

Strongly supported:

- feet were a principal lived/functional interface;
- GP presented an integrated foot-gait-tone problem;
- hospital operationally partitioned the unresolved relationship;
- Procyclidine demonstrated coupled neurological modifiability;
- 2021 documentation does not demonstrate foot-centred Sinemet design/monitoring;
- by 2023 foot-relevant OFF/dystonic dopaminergic reasoning was explicit;
- 2025 produced a clearly articulated overnight/first-step temporal experiment;
- later profound relief was reported.

Moderately supported / causal inference:

- partition and insufficient integration contributed to delayed effective formulation;
- systematic patient-centred monitoring would probably have identified the timing mismatch earlier;
- meaningful relief could plausibly have occurred materially earlier.

Not yet proved:

- every foot symptom was neurological or dystonic;
- Podiatry referral was wrong;
- IR Sinemet was incompetent in 2021;
- CR was mandatory in 2021;
- the 2025 response excluded structural/neuropathic components;
- earlier CR certainly would have worked;
- all intervening suffering was preventable;
- negligent formulation;
- a particular but-for date of relief;
- deliberate disregard.

## Contracted proposition

For the case file, use:

> **The hospital prescribed levodopa from 2021, but the record does not demonstrate that it formulated and tested levodopa around the disabling foot-centred condition as Liam actually experienced it. By 2023 the accumulated OFF/dystonic and medication-state evidence required the competing temporal model to be tested systematically. In 2025, when the overnight and first-step history was finally allowed to organise prescribing, a targeted controlled-release strategy was followed by reported profound relief. The sequence supports a contributory case that inadequate integration delayed effective formulation; the clinical-standard and strict but-for conclusions require independent movement-disorders evidence.**
