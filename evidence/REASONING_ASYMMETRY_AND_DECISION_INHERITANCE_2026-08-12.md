# Reasoning Asymmetry and Decision Inheritance — 12 August 2026

## Status

This is an analytical control document. It is not a primary source, pleading, expert conclusion or allegation of motive. It records a pattern now visible across the source set and independently detected in a separate documentary audit.

The governing distinction is:

> **Open questions receive extensive analysis; several inherited or already-operational answers receive comparatively little visible re-analysis.**

The significance of that pattern is for experts and counsel to test. Lack of documentation does not prove lack of thought. But the disparity matters because the July 2017 author repeatedly demonstrates that he is capable of unusually detailed clinical reasoning when he considers a question genuinely open.

---

## 1. Demonstrated reasoning capacity

The July 2017 extended consultation/discharge record is reasoning-rich. It does not read like a terse clinic note.

The author:

- reconstructs a possible symptom history over many years;
- separates historical foot pain from generalized leg/body tension;
- documents severe, markedly symmetrical akinetic-rigid Parkinsonism;
- distinguishes a footdrop-like appearance from true foot drop and describes possible dystonic posture;
- considers structural foot abnormalities, peripheral/neuropathic possibilities and neurological explanations;
- constructs a broad differential including idiopathic, genetic, synucleinopathic, Parkinson-plus, metabolic and toxic possibilities;
- reasons specifically toward PRKN/PARK2 using age, symmetry, akinetic-rigid phenotype, cognition and family-history structure;
- discusses MRI uncertainty and the danger of over-calling subtle findings;
- discusses limitations and possible utility of genetic testing;
- supplies detailed treatment-risk reasoning concerning dyskinesia, impulse-control disorder and dopamine dysregulation;
- creates a dose/titration architecture for procyclidine and other treatment;
- expressly states that he is not a movement-disorders neurologist and seeks outside specialist advice.

This is important because later terseness cannot simply be attributed to a general inability or unwillingness to document reasoning.

## 2. The foot/Podiatry allocation contains a reasoning bridge that is not presently visible

The record analyses the lower-limb/foot presentation extensively:

- historical pain and burning;
- corns/calluses;
- pes planus/hammertoes;
- abnormal gait;
- footdrop-like but not true foot drop;
- dystonic-looking posture;
- severe Parkinsonism;
- possible peripheral/neuropathic contribution;
- patient distinction between foot pain and leg tension.

The relationship is expressly left:

> **`Not clear.`**

Yet Podiatry becomes an operative pathway.

The record presently available does not contain a comparably explicit statement answering:

- which component Podiatry was being asked to treat;
- whether Podiatry was adjunctive to continuing neurological ownership of the unresolved relationship;
- whether the whole painful-foot complaint was being displaced into a structural pathway;
- what findings would be expected to return to Neurology;
- what would cause neurological reconsideration if local treatment failed.

The 6 July GP entry that the case would be prioritised **`as Parkinson's`** sharpens rather than resolves this question.

The correct proposition is not that Podiatry was wrong. Genuine structural pathology made Podiatry potentially appropriate. The problem is that the division of responsibility is largely inferential rather than expressly reasoned in the available record.

## 3. The levodopa decision is reasoned as a risk decision but under-specified as a reopening architecture

The original caution is not unexplained. The record supplies substantial rationale:

- very young age;
- dyskinesia risk;
- impulse-control and dopamine-dysregulation concerns;
- absence of disease-modifying effect;
- desire to use other symptomatic measures first;
- recognition that levodopa might work very well.

But once the strategy becomes operative, the record does not presently identify a prospective reopening rule.

No visible threshold specifies whether reconsideration would be triggered by:

- failure of rasagiline/procyclidine;
- inability to work;
- worsening gait;
- dystonia/cramps;
- pain;
- loss of function;
- specialist opinion;
- genetic clarification;
- or a structured low-dose response trial.

This contrasts with the precision used elsewhere in the same record for titration and withdrawal testing.

The key issue is therefore not lack of an original rationale. It is lack of a visible rule for when that rationale would cease to control.

## 4. Collective/senior decision inheritance is a live structural question

The record contains both individual reasoning and collective authority.

Monaghan later describes Counihan advice at the Weekly Complex Case Meeting, while the contemporaneous correspondence to Healy states:

> `Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?`

The patient separately recalls a post-meeting communication referring to `my boss`; that remains patient-source evidence and is not used here to prove meeting content.

The structural questions are:

- what part of the final strategy was Monaghan's own independent judgment;
- what part was inherited from senior/collective advice;
- who retained responsibility for reopening it;
- whether later contrary or qualifying information had a defined route back into that decision.

The source set presently shows adoption and continuity more clearly than it shows ownership of reconsideration.

## 5. Healy creates a specialist-advice reconciliation problem

Before specialist review, Monaghan records:

- he is `not a movement disorders neurologist`;
- he suspects genetic testing may well be negative;
- PRKN/PARK2 is nevertheless a live hypothesis;
- Galway is keen to hold levodopa back unless Healy advises otherwise.

After personally examining Liam, Healy writes:

> `I think probably has Park II and I would suggest testing that initially.`

He also describes PARK II patients as managing for long periods with relatively low doses of dopamine replacement therapy, while becoming dyskinetic if over-used.

The available record does not presently show a direct comparison between:

- Galway's prolonged-deferral model; and
- Healy's low-dose-replacement / avoid-over-use model.

It does not show whether they were treated as equivalent, whether Galway regarded Healy as supporting the existing plan, or whether Galway preferred its own timing model for stated reasons.

This is a **missing reconciliation**, not proof that Healy was ignored or that he ordered immediate levodopa.

## 6. April 2018 makes diagnostic-management decoupling visible

The April 2018 note contains a striking combination:

- `Obviously I'd be particularly interested to know`;
- PARK2/PRKN is what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause`;
- specific PARK2 testing or a panel would be reasonable;
- Healy would be happy with that route;
- the answer `doesn't particularly change current management`;
- there is therefore `no great loss in 'not knowing' currently right now`;
- the patient could `obviously ... be very much better` with levodopa or another dopamine agonist;
- but `we do not want to do that - at least for quite some time`.

The note supplies some reasoning for delaying genetic testing: future sequencing may be broader and the result is said not to alter current management.

What is comparatively thin is the patient-specific derivation of that management-neutrality claim.

The note does not visibly compare:

- Healy's probable-PARK2 judgment;
- Healy's low-dose dopamine-replacement model;
- the department's own PARK2 knowledge environment;
- the unresolved dystonic/foot phenotype;
- and the existing prolonged-deferral policy.

This creates the strongest current structural inference:

> **The diagnostic answer may have been judged by whether it would alter a management plan that had already been fixed, rather than the stronger diagnostic information being used to ask whether management itself should change.**

That is a hypothesis to test, not a concluded breach.

## 7. DaTSCAN supports syndrome certainty but not later etiological closure

The DaTSCAN was:

> **`abnormal as was expected`**

and the radiology differential included Parkinson's disease or Lewy body disease. The clinician records:

> **`this is obviously not LBD`**

This is compatible with concise clinical exclusion of LBD. It is not itself a major reasoning gap.

The important distinction is what follows:

- the scan confirms the expected dopaminergic/parkinsonian abnormality;
- it does not distinguish idiopathic PD from PRKN disease;
- the record already contains a live autosomal-recessive/PARK2 branch.

Therefore LBD exclusion does not supply the missing bridge to the later idiopathic-PD formulation.

Thus the later record is **reason-rich about treatment but comparatively reasoning-poor about the etiological transition itself**.

## 9. Selective closure: current working model

The pattern now worth testing is:

> **When the case is open, the clinician reasons expansively. When a practical answer has already been supplied by service pathway, collective decision, treatment policy or inherited label, the record more often shifts from exploration to implementation or justification.**

The clearest examples are:

1. `Not clear` foot relationship → Podiatry, without a visible allocation/reopening bridge;
2. reasoned levodopa caution → no visible prospective reopening threshold;
3. Healy probable PARK2 / low-dose replacement → no visible comparative treatment reconciliation;
4. PARK2 strongly suspected → confirmation treated as management-neutral;
5. later IPD belief → no visible etiological bridge from probable/suspected PARK2.

The correct label is **selective reasoning asymmetry / possible decision inheritance**, not dishonesty or incapacity.

## 10. Strongest benign explanation

A benign explanation remains substantial:

- exploratory first assessments are naturally longer than later implementation notes;
- Podiatry may obviously have been intended only for structural pathology;
- collective review is ordinarily a strength;
- Healy did not explicitly direct immediate levodopa;
- his caution about over-use may have been considered compatible with Galway's broader caution;
- genetic confirmation may genuinely have been thought unlikely to alter immediate symptomatic treatment;
- `idiopathic PD` may later have been used loosely as a clinical label while genetics remained unconfirmed.

On this view, much of the problem may be documentary rather than substantive: reasoning may have occurred but was not preserved in a way that allows later reconstruction.

---

## 11. Strongest critical explanation

The critical explanation is path dependence.

An early treatment and pathway architecture forms while important questions remain unresolved. Later information is then evaluated within that architecture rather than forcing the architecture back into an open state.

Under this model:

- Podiatry supplies a practical destination for structural feet without expressly resolving neurological ownership;
- levodopa caution becomes a durable policy without a defined reopening trigger;
- Healy's stronger PARK2 opinion is absorbed without visible comparative re-analysis;
- confirming PARK2 is declared management-neutral because management is already assumed to remain unchanged;
- later IPD language inherits operational force without a documented etiological transition.

The central concern is therefore not lack of reasoning capacity. It is that **the decisions most capable of becoming path-dependent are precisely those for which the available record supplies the least visible mechanism of reappraisal.**

The resulting expert question is whether this merely reflects ordinary clinical documentation, or whether it reveals a decision architecture in which inherited answers were insufficiently returned to an open state when new information arrived.**
