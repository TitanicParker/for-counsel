# 30 controlling facts

1. **First Neurology presentation at 39.** Monaghan's contemporaneous correspondence to Healy records that Liam was 39 and that this was his first time being seen by Neurology.

2. **Possible onset in the mid-20s.** The 2017 record describes a possible symptom history of about 13 years and expressly reasons from disease onset at approximately age `24/26`.

3. **Severe Parkinsonism was already clinically recognised during the July 2017 admission.** The record documents marked symmetrical tone, lead-pipe rigidity, gross bradykinesia and a strikingly abnormal gait.

4. **The lower-limb phenotype was partly dystonic-looking.** The inpatient record describes a footdrop-like appearance as `somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture - NOT FOOT DROP`; the later DaTSCAN indication describes the walk as `almost dystonically unusual`.

5. **Structural foot pathology was also real.** Corns, calluses, pes planus, hammertoes, mallet toes/pressure areas and foot pain were documented in primary-care and hospital records.

6. **Primary care was already reconsidering the gait/feet/muscle-tension problem neurologically before admission.** The GP record expressly considered Neurology for Liam's `gait, feet and "muscle tension" issues` and proceeded to referral after Occupational Health shared the concern.

7. **The neurological relationship of the feet/lower limbs was unresolved.** The hospital reasoning records the relationship as `Not clear`.

8. **The case was reviewed at a Neurology Complex Case Meeting.** Monaghan later stated that this meeting would have been on a Tuesday morning and likely during Liam's first admission. Given admission on Monday 3 July 2017, Tuesday 4 July is the obvious chronology inference, but the native meeting record is still required to fix the date and content conclusively.

9. **The contemporaneous plan links Counihan and Neurology colleagues directly to future dopaminergic-treatment caution.** It records that `The greatest of care has been advised with Prof Counihan and Neurology Colleagues` regarding future consideration of dopamine agonists and levodopa preparations such as Sinemet or Madopar.

10. **The stated rationale expressly included the exceptionally young estimated onset.** The treatment section says the caution was `based on a concern that disease onset was at c 24/26 years of age`.

11. **The operative policy was open-ended.** The same July 2017 treatment architecture records that `We were likely try and avoid these for as long as possible.` This exact formulation is a controlling fact and must not be diluted into merely `cautious use` or `not yet`.

12. **The position was also presented as jointly held with Counihan.** Monaghan wrote to Healy: `Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?`

13. **Monaghan simultaneously recognised the countervailing possibility of major benefit and later regret.** The 2017 reasoning records that Liam might do very well on levodopa, potentially sustainably, and includes the concern that `we may regret` not having started it earlier. This does not prove Monaghan believed the policy was wrong; it proves the downside of deferral was contemporaneously foreseeable.

14. **Monaghan expressly disclaimed movement-disorders specialization.** The 2017 record says he was not a movement-disorders neurologist while nevertheless undertaking detailed young-onset/PRKN reasoning and seeking specialist input.

15. **Counihan was not merely an anonymous member of a collective `we`.** The record identifies him by name in the treatment architecture; the surrounding evidence establishes him as the locally relevant Galway neurologist with Parkinson's/movement-disorder expertise and prior PRKN/genetics publication experience. The precise administrative hierarchy is not required to establish epistemic authority.

16. **No levodopa challenge was performed during the 2017 admission.** Whatever the medical merits of that choice, levodopa did not generate diagnostic-response information during the admission.

17. **By 6 July the GP/Podiatry pathway was already being prioritised `as Parkinson's`.** The GP entry states: `Phoned podiatry to discuss case - will mark as priority case and as Parkinson's - should be called mid September`.

18. **By 9 July Parkinson's disease was already an operative administrative category.** The Long Term Illness Scheme form used Parkinson's disease as the qualifying condition before the 13 July DaTSCAN and before later etiological resolution.

19. **The 13 July DaTSCAN was abnormal `as was expected`.** It confirmed an anticipated dopaminergic abnormality; the referral already described severe bilateral Parkinsonism and suspected autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism. The scan did not itself distinguish idiopathic PD from PRKN/PARK2.

20. **PRKN/PARK2 was specifically considered in 2017.** Galway identified PRKN/PARK2 because of the young, symmetrical, akinetic-rigid phenotype and related features.

21. **Galway's own explanatory material placed PRKN, dystonia, symmetry, juvenile onset and levodopa responsiveness in the knowledge environment.** The Parkinson's-genetics article selected/enclosed with the discharge material described PRKN disease using features including juvenile/early onset, dystonia, symmetrical signs, excellent levodopa response, and genetic testing in juvenile or atypical early-onset disease. Its proper significance is knowledge environment, not proof of what any clinician must have done.

22. **Healy personally reviewed Liam and strengthened the PRKN hypothesis.** His 12 September 2017 letter states: `I think probably has Park II and I would suggest testing that initially. If it is negative I would go on to test a panel.`

23. **Healy described cautious dopamine replacement rather than indefinite non-use.** He wrote that PARK II patients `tend to manage quite well for long periods of time with relatively low doses of Dopamine replacement therapy` but `can become quite dyskinetic if over-used.` He did not expressly order immediate levodopa commencement.

24. **A later-2017 procyclidine withdrawal/restoration episode supplied a neurological-modifiability signal in the disputed lower-limb/foot syndrome.** The project source reconstruction records rapid symptomatic return/change after procyclidine withdrawal and marked improvement after restoration. This does not prove levodopa responsiveness, but it is evidence that at least part of the disputed syndrome was pharmacologically neurologically modifiable and therefore relevant to whether the `feet` could safely be treated as a purely peripheral category.

25. **By April 2018 Galway still recorded PARK2/PRKN as the suspected cause.** The treating record states that the specific PARK2/PRKN condition was what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause`.

26. **The 2018 record treated confirmation of suspected PARK2 as not particularly management-changing.** Monaghan writes `Obviously I'd be particularly interested to know`, but also: `It doesn't particularly change current management - so there is no great loss in "not knowing" currently right now.`

27. **The same 2018 record preserved anticipated major benefit while continuing deferral.** Liam could `obviously ... be very much better` with levodopa or another dopamine agonist, but `we do not want to do that - at least for quite some time`.

28. **The `for as long as possible` period ended in practice in 2021.** By September/October 2021 Liam reported feeling undertreated and was started on Sinemet 62.5 mg twice daily (`12.5/50 mg BD`) as his first levodopa drug. The 27 October 2021 clinic letter then records that because of raised ocular pressures Paddy Browne suggested removing Kemadrin/procyclidine and replacing it with Sinemet; Sinemet was increased to four times daily and Liam hoped this would more than compensate for Kemadrin removal. The contemporaneous letter therefore documents an explicit therapeutic replacement relationship between procyclidine/Kemadrin and Sinemet, while not proving that the two drugs are mechanistically interchangeable.

29. **The later foot/lower-limb record repeatedly resisted a purely structural partition.** It includes a 2020 statement that the footdrop-like lower-limb `throw` was Parkinsonian despite normal strength; 2023 OFF/dystonia reasoning and Stalevo use for movement and pain; 2024 pain improvement with Lyrica without resolution of toe curling; and 2025 severe morning foot pain improving as Parkinson medication reached the system.

30. **In 2025 nocturnal/early-morning dopaminergic coverage became directly relevant to the foot-pain problem, and 2026 genetics confirmed the earlier etiological branch.** The 2025 record documents that the feet improved when Parkinson medication took effect, further Sinemet was expected to provide additional relief subject to dyskinesia limits, and controlled-release Sinemet was used around the overnight/morning state with major relief reported. In February 2026 St Vincent's confirmed two pathogenic Parkin variants and concluded that Liam's Parkinsonism and dystonia are due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations. The 2026 result does not itself establish what treatment should have occurred in 2017.

# 16 current expert / counsel questions

1. **Initial proportionality:** Was the early decision to defer levodopa/dopamine replacement clinically proportionate to the severity, age and phenotype visible at the time it was made?

2. **Meaning of `for as long as possible`:** Clinically, what findings should have made further deferral no longer possible? Was there a review date, disability threshold, treatment-failure trigger, phenotype trigger, specialist-opinion trigger or other defined reopening condition?

3. **Incomplete symptom-set risk:** Was the lower-limb/foot syndrome adequately included among the symptoms capable of ending the deferral period, or did its partial allocation to Podiatry/mechanical/neuropathic explanations prevent it from counting toward the levodopa threshold?

4. **Diagnostic-learning consequence:** Once levodopa had been deliberately excluded during the admission, did that materially remove a clinically useful source of information about gait, lower-limb posturing, tension and foot symptoms?

5. **Procyclidine signal:** What should reasonably have been inferred, if anything, from a withdrawal/restoration episode indicating that at least part of the disputed foot/lower-limb phenomenon was neurologically pharmacologically modifiable?

6. **2021 replacement relationship:** What is the clinical significance of the October 2021 record expressly describing Kemadrin/procyclidine removal and replacement by Sinemet, with escalation to QDS dosing? Does this bear on how the earlier neurological symptom architecture should be understood, while remaining distinct from proof that procyclidine response predicted levodopa response?

7. **PARK2-specific management:** Was it clinically reasonable in 2018 to say that confirming suspected PARK2 would not particularly change current management and that there was `no great loss` in not knowing at that time?

8. **Healy-versus-Galway framing:** Did Healy's description of long-term relatively low-dose dopamine replacement with avoidance of over-use materially differ from Galway's `for as long as possible` / `insofar as maybe possible` / `for quite some time` policy?

9. **Second-opinion reconciliation:** If those framings materially differed, what should the record reasonably show by way of comparison, reconciliation or reconsideration after Healy's review?

10. **Counihan hinge:** Did Counihan actually endorse prolonged avoidance, or did Monaghan operationalise a narrower specialist warning to use exceptional care into the stronger rule of avoiding treatment for as long as possible?

11. **Epistemic authority:** Given Monaghan's express disclaimer of movement-disorders specialization and Counihan's directly relevant Parkinson's/PRKN expertise, what decisional weight would Counihan's apparent support naturally carry even without formal hierarchical command?

12. **Podiatry partition:** Given the dystonic-looking lower-limb phenotype and unresolved `Not clear` relationship, was Podiatry appropriately adjunctive for structural pathology, or did the pathway risk becoming substitutive for unresolved neurological reassessment?

13. **Longitudinal rebalancing:** Did the later medication-sensitive, Parkinsonian, OFF/dystonic and mixed foot/lower-limb evidence require formal reconsideration of the original treatment/partition decision before 2025?

14. **2025 retrospective significance:** What can properly be inferred from the later fact that severe morning foot pain improved when Parkinson medication reached the system and that controlled-release levodopa around the overnight period produced major relief? What cannot be inferred retrospectively about 2017?

15. **Etiological transition:** What evidence justified moving from probable/suspected PARK2 in 2017–2018 to an affirmative idiopathic Parkinson's disease position by March 2025?

16. **Decision governance:** Did an early collective/specialist-attributed treatment decision become more stable than the evolving evidential state, such that later information was assessed for whether it changed an already-fixed management plan rather than first being used to reconsider that plan?

# Controlling pre-proposition formulation

The strongest presently supportable structure is:

> **Recognition succeeded; reassessment may have failed. A legitimate early concern about treatment complications became an open-ended policy to avoid dopaminergic treatment `for as long as possible` without a documented reopening rule. The feet/lower limbs remained etiologically mixed and `Not clear`; a later-2017 medication response showed neurological modifiability; Healy strengthened PRKN and described cautious low-dose dopamine replacement; yet the existing policy visibly persisted and PRKN confirmation was treated as management-neutral. In 2021 Sinemet finally entered and explicitly replaced procyclidine/Kemadrin as the neurological treatment architecture changed. By 2025 the severe morning foot problem itself proved materially responsive to dopaminergic timing/coverage. The 2026 genetics then confirmed that the earlier PRKN branch was etiologically correct.**

This formulation does **not** assert that all foot pain was dystonia, that Counihan ordered prolonged avoidance, that Healy ordered immediate levodopa, or that later treatment response proves what the 2017 standard of care required. Those remain matters for expert evidence.
