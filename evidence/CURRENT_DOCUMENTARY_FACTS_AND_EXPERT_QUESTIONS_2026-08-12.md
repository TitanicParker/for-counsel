# 21 controlling documentary facts

1. **First Neurology presentation at 39.** Monaghan's contemporaneous correspondence to Healy records that Liam was 39 and that this was his first time being seen by Neurology.

2. **Possible long symptom history.** The same correspondence records possible Parkinsonian symptoms extending for approximately 13 years.

3. **Severe Parkinsonism was already clinically recognised during the July 2017 admission.** The record documents marked symmetrical tone, lead-pipe rigidity, gross bradykinesia and a strikingly abnormal gait.

4. **The lower-limb phenotype was partly dystonic-looking.** The inpatient record describes a footdrop-like appearance as `somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture - NOT FOOT DROP`; the later DaTSCAN indication describes the walk as `almost dystonically unusual`.

5. **Structural foot pathology was also real.** Corns, calluses, pes planus, hammertoes, mallet toes/pressure areas and foot pain were documented in primary-care and hospital records.

6. **Primary care was already reconsidering the gait/feet/muscle-tension problem neurologically before admission.** The GP record expressly considered Neurology for the patient's `gait, feet and "muscle tension" issues` and proceeded to referral after Occupational Health shared the concern.

7. **A deliberate levodopa/dopamine-agonist deferral strategy became operative very early.** The 2017 record documents concern about dyskinesia, impulse-control disorder and dopamine dysregulation in a very young patient.

8. **The treatment position was collective/consultative rather than merely private.** Monaghan wrote to Healy: `Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?`

9. **No levodopa challenge was performed during the admission.** Whatever the medical merits of that choice, levodopa did not generate diagnostic-response information during the admission.

10. **By 6 July the GP/Podiatry pathway was already being prioritised `as Parkinson's`.** The GP entry states: `Phoned podiatry to discuss case - will mark as priority case and as Parkinson's - should be called mid September`.

11. **By 9 July Parkinson's disease was already an operative administrative category.** The Long Term Illness Scheme form used Parkinson's disease as the qualifying condition before the 13 July DaTSCAN and before later etiological resolution.

12. **The foot/lower-limb relationship remained unresolved in the hospital reasoning.** The hospital record records the relationship as `Not clear` while Podiatry became an operative pathway.

13. **The 13 July DaTSCAN was abnormal `as was expected`.** The scan confirmed an anticipated dopaminergic abnormality; the referral already described suspected autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism.

14. **PRKN/PARK2 was specifically considered in 2017.** The Galway record identifies PRKN/PARK2 among the etiological possibilities because of the young, symmetrical, akinetic-rigid phenotype and related features.

15. **Prof Healy personally reviewed Liam and recorded that he probably had PARK II.** His 12 September 2017 letter states: `I think probably has Park II and I would suggest testing that initially. If it is negative I would go on to test a panel.`

16. **Healy's treatment description distinguished cautious replacement from over-use.** He wrote that PARK II patients `tend to manage quite well for long periods of time with relatively low doses of Dopamine replacement therapy` but `can become quite dyskinetic if over-used.` He did not expressly order immediate levodopa commencement.

17. **By April 2018 Galway still recorded PARK2/PRKN as the suspected cause.** The treating record states that the specific PARK2/PRKN condition was what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause`.

18. **The 2018 record expressly treated confirmation of the suspected PARK2 diagnosis as not particularly management-changing.** The note begins `Obviously I'd be particularly interested to know` but then says `It doesn't particularly change current management - so there is no great loss in "not knowing" currently right now`, while discussing specific PARK2 testing/panel/WGS options and recording that `we/Prof Healy suspect` PARK2 is the cause.

19. **The same 2018 treatment record maintained levodopa deferral despite anticipated benefit.** It states that Liam could `obviously ... be very much better` if levodopa or another dopamine agonist were given, but `we do not want to do that - at least for quite some time`.

20. **By 25 March 2025 the retrospective etiological position was expressly idiopathic Parkinson's disease.** The complaint response states that `it is our belief and understanding that [Patient] has idiopathic Parkinson's disease.` No presently identified source explains the transition from probable/suspected PARK2 to asserted IPD.

21. **In February 2026 specialist genetics confirmed the earlier PARK2 branch.** St Vincent's reported two pathogenic Parkin variants and concluded that Liam's Parkinsonism and dystonia are due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations.

# 12 current expert questions

1. **Initial proportionality:** Was the early decision to defer levodopa/dopamine replacement clinically proportionate to the severity, age and phenotype visible at the time it was made?

2. **Diagnostic-learning consequence:** Once levodopa had been deliberately excluded during the admission, did that materially remove a clinically useful source of information about gait, lower-limb posturing, tension and foot symptoms?

3. **Reopening trigger:** What findings should ordinarily have caused the original levodopa-deferral strategy to return to an open state, and are any such triggers documented here?

4. **PARK2-specific management:** In this particular patient, was it clinically reasonable in 2018 to say that confirming suspected PARK2 would not particularly change current management and that there was `no great loss` in not knowing at that time?

5. **Healy-versus-Galway treatment framing:** Did Healy's statement that PARK II patients can do well for long periods on relatively low-dose dopamine replacement, while becoming dyskinetic if over-used, materially differ from a policy of holding dopamine replacement back `insofar as maybe possible` / `for quite some time`?

6. **Response to second opinion:** If those treatment framings materially differed, what would good neurological practice require Galway to do with Healy's September 2017 opinion?

7. **Podiatry partition:** Given the dystonic-looking lower-limb phenotype and unresolved `Not clear` relationship, was Podiatry appropriately adjunctive for structural pathology, or did the pathway risk becoming substitutive for unresolved neurological assessment?

8. **Meaning of `as Parkinson's`:** What would a reasonable clinician understand by prioritising a Podiatry case `as Parkinson's` on 6 July while the patient was still undergoing first neurological assessment and before DaTSCAN/genetic confirmation?

9. **Etiological transition:** What clinical evidence would justify moving from probable/suspected PARK2 in 2017–2018 to an affirmative idiopathic Parkinson's disease position by March 2025?

10. **Longitudinal rebalancing:** Did later medication-sensitive, Parkinsonian, OFF/dystonic and mixed foot/lower-limb evidence require formal reconsideration of the original treatment/partition decision before 2025?

11. **Causation/harm:** If the treatment strategy was not adequately reopened, what functional impairment, pain, treatment burden or diagnostic delay can medically be attributed to that pathway rather than to unavoidable disease progression and genuine structural foot pathology?

12. **Polarity question:** Does the documentary record disclose a clinically meaningful tension between (a) the treating neurologist's continuing suspicion that PARK2 was the cause and personal interest in confirming it, and (b) a management architecture already insulated from that answer because levodopa/dopamine replacement had been deliberately deferred? If so, is that tension medically coherent, insufficiently reconciled, or genuinely contradictory?

**etiological pull:** `probably PARK II` / `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause` / `Obviously I'd be particularly interested to know`

versus

**management lock:** `hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible` / `we do not want to do that - at least for quite some time` / `It doesn't particularly change current management`.

The existence and significance of that polarity should be tested independently. It should not be converted into an allegation of dishonesty, motive or bad faith merely because the wording is uncomfortable.
