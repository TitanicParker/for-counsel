# 36 controlling facts

1. **First Neurology presentation at 39.** Monaghan's contemporaneous correspondence to Healy records that Liam was 39 and that this was his first time being seen by Neurology.

2. **Possible onset in the mid-20s.** The 2017 record describes a possible symptom history of about 13 years and expressly reasons from disease onset at approximately age `24/26`.

3. **The patient's longitudinal anchor is anatomically specific.** Liam says the first persistent pain was on the outside of the left foot in his mid-20s and remained the most stable focal pain site across the following two decades. This is patient-source evidence about lived chronology and anatomy, not by itself proof of mechanism.

4. **Primary care entered through gait, feet and tension.** Before admission, the GP record expressly considered Neurology for Liam's `gait, feet and "muscle tension" issues` and proceeded to referral after Occupational Health shared concern.

5. **Severe Parkinsonism was already clinically recognised during the July 2017 admission.** The record documents marked symmetrical tone, lead-pipe rigidity, gross bradykinesia and a strikingly abnormal gait.

6. **The lower-limb phenotype was partly dystonic-looking.** The inpatient record describes a footdrop-like appearance as `somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture - NOT FOOT DROP`; the later DaTSCAN indication describes the walk as `almost dystonically unusual`.

7. **Structural foot pathology was also real.** Corns, calluses, pes planus, hammertoes, mallet toes/pressure areas and foot pain were documented.

8. **The neurological relationship of the feet/lower limbs was unresolved.** The hospital reasoning records the relationship as `Not clear`.

9. **The case was reviewed at a Neurology Complex Case Meeting.** Monaghan later stated that this meeting would have been on a Tuesday morning and likely during Liam's first admission. Given admission on Monday 3 July 2017, Tuesday 4 July is the obvious chronology inference, but the native meeting record is still required to fix the date and content conclusively.

10. **The contemporaneous plan links Counihan and Neurology colleagues directly to future dopaminergic-treatment caution.** It records that `The greatest of care has been advised with Prof Counihan and Neurology Colleagues` regarding future consideration of dopamine agonists and levodopa preparations such as Sinemet or Madopar.

11. **The stated rationale expressly included the exceptionally young estimated onset.** The treatment section says the caution was `based on a concern that disease onset was at c 24/26 years of age`.

12. **The operative policy was open-ended.** The same July 2017 treatment architecture records that `We were likely try and avoid these for as long as possible.` This exact formulation is a controlling fact and must not be diluted into merely `cautious use` or `not yet`.

13. **The position was also presented as jointly held with Counihan.** Monaghan wrote to Healy: `Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?`

14. **Monaghan simultaneously recognised the countervailing possibility of major benefit and later regret.** The 2017 reasoning records that Liam might do very well on levodopa, potentially sustainably, and includes the concern that `we may regret` not having started it earlier.

15. **Monaghan expressly disclaimed movement-disorders specialization.** The 2017 record says he was not a movement-disorders neurologist while nevertheless undertaking detailed young-onset/PRKN reasoning and seeking specialist input.

16. **No levodopa challenge was performed during the 2017 admission.** Whatever the medical merits of that choice, levodopa did not generate diagnostic-response information during the admission.

17. **By 6 July the GP/Podiatry pathway was already being prioritised `as Parkinson's`.** The GP entry states: `Phoned podiatry to discuss case - will mark as priority case and as Parkinson's - should be called mid September`.

18. **By 9 July Parkinson's disease was already an operative administrative category.** The Long Term Illness Scheme form used Parkinson's disease as the qualifying condition before the 13 July DaTSCAN and before later etiological resolution.

19. **The 13 July DaTSCAN was abnormal `as was expected`.** It confirmed an anticipated presynaptic dopaminergic abnormality but did not itself distinguish idiopathic PD from PRKN/PARK2.

20. **PRKN/PARK2 was specifically considered in 2017.** Galway identified PRKN/PARK2 because of the young, symmetrical, akinetic-rigid phenotype and related features.

21. **Healy personally reviewed Liam and strengthened the PRKN hypothesis.** His 12 September 2017 letter states: `I think probably has Park II and I would suggest testing that initially. If it is negative I would go on to test a panel.`

22. **Healy described cautious dopamine replacement rather than indefinite non-use.** He wrote that PARK II patients `tend to manage quite well for long periods of time with relatively low doses of Dopamine replacement therapy` but `can become quite dyskinetic if over-used.` The operative meaning of `relatively low doses` was not defined in the record as a patient-specific total exposure, escalation rule, treatment window or response threshold.

23. **The autumn-2017 procyclidine period generated a strong patient-specific treatment-response signal.** Contemporaneous patient emails describe major restoration of gait and function, `loose and limber` legs, and the experience as `living in a miracle`; during a planned treatment break Liam contemporaneously recorded rebuilding tension, restless feet and gait falling apart.

24. **The patient's present recollection is materially convergent with those contemporaneous emails.** Liam now recalls that the profound relaxation included marked reduction of the longstanding outside-left-foot pain and that this focal pain was the first familiar pain to return when procyclidine was withdrawn. The precise `first symptom` sequence remains patient recollection unless independently corroborated.

25. **The 2017 treatment response matters because it mirrors the original referral architecture.** The GP had referred gait, feet and muscle tension together; Neurology's first effective medication altered gait, lower-limb tension and the feet together. Whatever the exact mechanism, the referred complex was shown to move together under neurological medication.

26. **By April 2018 Galway still recorded PARK2/PRKN as the suspected cause.** The treating record states that the specific PARK2/PRKN condition was what `we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause`.

27. **The 2018 record treated confirmation of suspected PARK2 as not particularly management-changing.** Monaghan writes `Obviously I'd be particularly interested to know`, but also: `It doesn't particularly change current management - so there is no great loss in "not knowing" currently right now.`

28. **The same 2018 record preserved anticipated major benefit while continuing deferral.** Liam could `obviously ... be very much better` with levodopa or another dopamine agonist, but `we do not want to do that - at least for quite some time`.

29. **The `for as long as possible` period ended in practice in 2021.** By September/October 2021 Liam reported feeling undertreated and Sinemet 12.5/50 mg was introduced. The contemporaneous records show 12.5/50 mg BD on 14 October 2021 and escalation to QDS by 26 October 2021.

30. **Sinemet entered as a direct clinical replacement for Kemadrin/procyclidine.** The October 2021 record states that because of raised ocular pressure Kemadrin would be removed and replaced with Sinemet, with escalation intended to compensate for Kemadrin withdrawal.

31. **The nominally small tablet did not remain a static `low-dose` strategy.** Within roughly two months, the regimen moved from Sinemet 12.5/50 BD to QDS and then to Stalevo 75/18.75/200 QDS, with the December 2021 prescription citing `re-emerging dystonia and altered gait` and a `small increase in levodopa`. This does not itself prove over-treatment; it does show that `low dose` requires analysis as a longitudinal strategy rather than tablet strength alone.

32. **By March 2023 the pain syndrome had expanded beyond the old focal lateral-left-foot problem.** The urgent GP referral describes severe bilateral `burning` pain affecting both feet and lower legs, extreme at times, with significant psychological impact and an urgent request for Neurology help `before we get into trouble here`.

33. **By March 2023 the neuropathic-pain treatment pathway was failing at high treatment intensity.** Neurology's internal correspondence two days later records pregabalin/Lyrica at 600 mg/day, `debilitating pain`, `no quality of life`, and worsening over months despite treatment.

34. **At the same time, Liam remained on regular immediate-release levodopa.** The 2023 medication record places him on regular Sinemet while receiving high-dose pregabalin. Severe pain therefore persisted despite active treatment under both the neuropathic-pain and Parkinsonian medication pathways.

35. **By September 2023 the pain/mobility syndrome showed explicit dopaminergic-state dependence.** Folan recorded severe foot pain, reduced mobility and shuffling, worse in the morning and when Sinemet had not been taken, and concluded: `It sounds like his Parkinson's is being undertreated and the leg pain and shuffling is off-related.` Monaghan agreed that a Stalevo trial was reasonable.

36. **By 2025 the evidential asymmetry was explicit, and 2026 genetics confirmed the earlier etiological branch.** In March 2025 Monaghan still wrote conditionally: `If there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here, the alcohol being gone will hopefully slow the deterioration of that`; in the same clinical period he documented that Parkinson medication relieved the feet, that Liam knew Sinemet improved the pain, and that more Sinemet would probably give further relief. Controlled-release Sinemet then produced major relief reported by the patient. In February 2026 St Vincent's confirmed two pathogenic Parkin variants and concluded that Liam's Parkinsonism and dystonia were due to compound heterozygous Parkin mutations. The 2026 result does not itself establish what treatment should have occurred in 2017.

# Patient-source layer that must remain visible

The patient voice is not merely retrospective colour. In important places it converges with contemporaneous self-report.

Liam's current anatomical account is unusually stable: the outside of the left foot is the place he says the condition first `grabbed` him, the pain site around which later compensation was organised, and the signal he learned to read first when the wider motor system deteriorated. That is patient evidence about lived bodily chronology. It should neither be dismissed because it is subjective nor promoted into a neurological diagnosis without expert support.

The autumn-2017 emails materially corroborate the broader patient account of extraordinary procyclidine benefit. The trust consequence is also direct patient evidence: Liam says the successful early treatment convinced him that he was in good hands and that he continued to trust the neurological team until 14 March 2025.

That trust chronology matters. The absence of repeated protest between 2017 and 2025 should not automatically be treated as evidence that Liam understood or accepted every etiological assumption. His operating position was reliance on the clinicians.

By 2023–2025, Liam says the repeated feet/neuropathy/alcohol framing narrowed his expectation that reporting the feet to Neurology would produce useful reconsideration, although he continued reporting them to his GP. The March 2023 urgent GP referral is therefore important not only clinically but communicatively: the GP became the route through which the severity of the feet was again forced back into Neurology.

18. **Patient communication:** Could repeated framing of severe foot pain through alcohol/neuropathy reasonably affect what a trusting patient believes is worth reporting to Neurology, and should that possibility matter in longitudinal reassessment?

19. **Decision governance:** Did an early collective/specialist-attributed treatment decision become more stable than the evolving evidential state, such that later information was assessed for whether it changed an already-fixed management plan rather than first being used to reconsider that plan?

# Controlling formulation

> **The evidence moved; the frame appears not to have moved with it. On or around 4 July 2017, an open-ended policy of avoiding conventional dopaminergic treatment `for as long as possible` crystallised while the feet/lower limbs remained etiologically unresolved. Within months, Neurology's own procyclidine treatment showed that gait, tension and feet could move together. Healy then strengthened PARK2 and advised a cautious, relatively-low-dose dopamine-replacement model. Yet the pre-existing management architecture persisted. When levodopa finally entered in 2021, it did so as a replacement for Kemadrin and escalated rapidly. By March 2023, the once-focal foot problem had expanded into severe bilateral feet/lower-leg pain despite pregabalin 600 mg/day and regular levodopa; by September a movement-disorders clinician explicitly called the pain/shuffling OFF-related. By 2025 neuropathy remained conditional while dopaminergic pain relief was observed. The question is therefore no longer simply whether clinicians failed to integrate the feet. It is whether the clinical architecture repeatedly preserved their separation from the neurological disease model despite treatment-response evidence capable of reopening it.**

This formulation does **not** assert that all foot pain was dystonia, that Counihan ordered prolonged avoidance, that Healy ordered immediate levodopa, that pregabalin failure disproves neuropathy, or that later treatment response retrospectively proves what the 2017 standard of care required. Those remain matters for expert evidence.
