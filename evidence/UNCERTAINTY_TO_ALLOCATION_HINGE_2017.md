# Uncertainty-to-Allocation Hinge — July 2017

**Status:** controlling forensic formulation for counsel/expert development.  
**Source discipline:** this note separates documentary fact, patient-source evidence and inference. It does not allege deliberate suppression, concealment or bad faith.

## Governing formulation

> **`Not clear` was never resolved; it was operationally managed.**

More fully:

> **The relationship between the feet and the neurological syndrome remained epistemically unresolved while the case became operationally settled around their separation.**

The discharge architecture therefore appears to have done something more specific than simply `miss` the feet. The uncertainty was preserved in language, but the partition was preserved with it.

A useful compression is:

> **Epistemically unresolved; operationally settled.**

## Why the bedside protest matters

Liam reports that on Monday 10 July 2017, shortly before discharge and in front of four medical students, he challenged the neurologist by asking in substance whether he intended to give him a `full NCT` and send him out on `bald tyres`.

The event remains patient-source evidence unless independently corroborated.

Its significance is not principally the missing conversation itself. Its significance is where it sits in the decision architecture:

> **`Not clear` is epistemic language. `Please refer to Podiatry` is an operational decision. The protest sits between the two.**

The patient was not necessarily disputing Parkinsonism, asking for levodopa or offering a neurological theory. He was challenging the completeness of discharge: the disabling feet problem that had helped bring him into care remained unresolved.

That challenge therefore appears to have been directed at the exact point at which causal uncertainty was being converted into practical allocation.

## Causal question versus service-allocation answer

This distinction must remain visible:

**Patient's underlying question:**

> Are the feet part of what is wrong neurologically?

**Possible institutional answer:**

> Someone will look after the feet through Podiatry.

Those are not the same answer.

Podiatry may have been entirely appropriate for real corns, calluses, deformity, loading and other structural pathology. The forensic issue is whether a management destination came to function as a causal classification.

The proposition to test is therefore not `Podiatry was wrong`.

It is:

> **Did `Podiatry can treat the feet` become operationally equivalent to `the feet are not part of the neurological causal problem`, even though the record itself continued to say the relationship was `Not clear`?**

## Why this matters longitudinally

The later record repeatedly returns to the unresolved relationship:

- the original GP pathway linked gait, feet and muscle tension;
- the inpatient lower-limb phenotype was partly dystonic-looking;
- the relationship was recorded as `Not clear`;
- procyclidine later changed gait, tension and feet together;
- withdrawal produced deterioration involving the same system;
- Healy strengthened the PARK2 hypothesis;
- later records documented re-emerging dystonia and altered gait;
- by 2023 severe foot/leg pain persisted despite intensive neuropathic-pain treatment;
- the same year pain and shuffling were explicitly described as OFF-related;
- by 2025 Sinemet relief of the feet was directly observed while neuropathy remained conditional;
- 2026 genetics confirmed PRKN-related Parkinsonism and dystonia.

None of those later facts proves what the July 2017 standard of care required. Their significance is different: they show that the causal question the patient pressed at discharge remained clinically alive for years.

## The protest as an early stress test

The strongest present inference is not that the protest was deliberately suppressed.

It is:

> **The bedside protest is the earliest presently identified event at which the patient directly challenged the practical separation of the unresolved foot problem from the neurological discharge pathway. The surviving record shows no corresponding reopening of that relationship.**

That makes the protest a plausible early stress test of the governing frame.

The evidential question is what happened next:

1. Was the protest dismissed as already answered by Podiatry?
2. Was it reconsidered and the existing plan reaffirmed?
3. Did it trigger further internal discussion?
4. Did it trigger or accelerate Podiatry activity without reopening the causal question?
5. Did somebody raise the possibility that the frame itself needed reconsideration?
6. Did nothing record-generating happen at all?

Each possibility has a materially different forensic meaning.

## What can presently be said safely

> **The surviving documents do not corroborate the bedside protest directly, but they substantially corroborate the clinical situation the protest describes.**

Or more tightly:

> **The documents agree with the protest's logic, though not yet with its occurrence.**

The record itself preserves an unresolved causal relationship and a practical Podiatry allocation. The patient account describes an objection to being discharged while that unresolved problem remained functionally central to him.

## Controlling question

> **What happened inside the hospital after the patient challenged discharge and before discharge was allowed to stand?**

If the answer is `nothing`, that is significant.

If the answer is `the issue was reconsidered and the plan reaffirmed`, the reasoning matters.

If the answer is `the concern was answered by allocating the feet more firmly to Podiatry`, that may identify the mechanism by which unresolved causal uncertainty became stable operational separation.

If evidence shows that somebody recognised the contradiction and proposed reopening the frame, but closure nevertheless prevailed, the protest may become a case-defining decision-governance event.

> **The uncertainty was preserved in the record, but stripped of the power to reorganise the pathway.**

That formulation should now be tested against the whole chronology rather than assumed.
