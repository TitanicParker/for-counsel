# Controlled Analytical Glossary

**Status:** CONTROLLING — terminology and classification control  
**Date:** 13 August 2026

## Purpose

This glossary controls the specialist vocabulary used across the repository. Its purpose is not to convert analytical language into fact. It is to prevent descriptive, causal, state-of-mind and methodological terms from being mistaken for propositions already proved by the sources.

> **A defined term is an analytical tool. Whether it applies in any particular instance must still be established source-by-source and proposition-by-proposition.**

## Classification key

- **DOCUMENTARY** — describes the form, wording or relationship of records.
- **DESCRIPTIVE** — describes an observed pattern without itself assigning legal fault or subjective motive.
- **CLINICAL** — organises clinical phenomena or treatment-response evidence; expert evidence may be required for stronger conclusions.
- **CAUSAL** — expresses a proposed causal relationship or counterfactual and must not be treated as established merely because the term is defined.
- **STATE-OF-MIND** — concerns knowledge, awareness, recklessness, intention or interpretation by an actor; requires actor-specific proof.
- **GOVERNANCE** — concerns institutional decision-making, review, authority or responsibility structures.
- **EVIDENTIAL / METHODOLOGICAL** — controls how propositions are sourced, tested and expressed.

## Controlled terms

| Term | Class | Controlled meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Governing frame / management architecture** | DESCRIPTIVE / GOVERNANCE | The early combination of treatment policy, causal allocation, role allocation and assumptions capable of organising later care. Use does not itself prove rigidity or improper motive. |
| **Maximum-deferral rule** | DOCUMENTARY / DESCRIPTIVE | The recorded intention to avoid levodopa and dopamine agonists `for as long as possible`. Whether this was clinically reasonable is separate. |
| **Reopening rule** | GOVERNANCE / METHODOLOGICAL | A predefined event or criterion requiring a governing decision or classification to be reconsidered. An apparent absence must be stated as an absence in the identified record, not proof that no reconsideration occurred. |
| **Operational partition** | DESCRIPTIVE | An unresolved clinical relationship becoming practically separated through different services, classifications or treatment pathways. |
| **Peripheral/Podiatry partition** | DESCRIPTIVE / CLINICAL | The practical allocation of structural/peripheral foot problems to Podiatry while the relationship to the neurological condition remained unresolved. It does not mean Podiatry was inappropriate. |
| **Adjunct versus substitute** | CLINICAL / GOVERNANCE | The question whether specialty care supplemented integrated neurological management or effectively displaced responsibility for the unresolved whole. |
| **Feet as interface** | DESCRIPTIVE / CLINICAL | The feet as the domain where pain, movement, posture, medication state, gait and lived disability repeatedly converged. It does not assert a single cause for all foot symptoms. |
| **Patient as diagnostic instrument** | CLINICAL / METHODOLOGICAL | Use of the patient's longitudinal observations of timing, dose response, wearing-off and function as evidence for designing and evaluating clinical tests. It does not require accepting the patient's causal interpretation as fact. |
| **Diagnostic perturbation / natural experiment** | CLINICAL / CAUSAL | A medication withdrawal, restoration, dose or timing change that provides information about causal relationships. The strength of the inference depends on confounding and expert interpretation. |
| **Coupled neurological modifiability** | CLINICAL / DESCRIPTIVE | Multiple disputed domains changing together under neurological pharmacological control, without proving one exclusive neurological generator. |
| **Local accommodation** | DESCRIPTIVE | New evidence changing a drug, dose, referral or immediate intervention without a visible corresponding change to the broader historical explanation. |
| **Global integration** | CLINICAL / GOVERNANCE | Explicitly allowing new evidence to revise diagnosis, causal classification, specialty ownership, treatment architecture or historical interpretation where warranted. |
| **Historical force** | EVIDENTIAL / METHODOLOGICAL | The capacity of later evidence to alter the interpretation of earlier events rather than merely current treatment. |
| **Treatment authority** | DESCRIPTIVE / CLINICAL | Information being permitted to influence prescribing or immediate management. |
| **Interpretive authority** | DESCRIPTIVE / EVIDENTIAL | Information being permitted to reorganise the explanatory or historical model. |
| **Proximity without synthesis** | DOCUMENTARY / DESCRIPTIVE | Relevant facts appearing close together in a record without an identified explicit adjudication of their relationship. |
| **Documentation inertia** | DOCUMENTARY / DESCRIPTIVE | Earlier formulations persisting through copied, inherited or repeatedly relied-upon records. It does not itself establish conscious preservation. |
| **Decision inheritance** | GOVERNANCE / DESCRIPTIVE | Later actors or processes operating within assumptions established by an earlier decision. |
| **Architecture-preserving interpretation** | DESCRIPTIVE / CAUSAL | An interpretation of new evidence that allows it to coexist with the inherited frame rather than requiring the frame to change. Application in a specific instance is an inference, not an atomic fact. |
| **Conscious interpretive accommodation** | STATE-OF-MIND | Actual awareness of potentially destabilising evidence combined with a deliberate interpretation allowing it to coexist with an inherited model. Requires actor-specific evidence of awareness and interpretation. |
| **Self-stabilising architecture** | DESCRIPTIVE / CAUSAL | A model capable of absorbing contrary evidence without a visible falsification or reopening condition. It is an analytical property, not proof of intention. |
| **Reasoning asymmetry** | EVIDENTIAL / DESCRIPTIVE | Unequal classificatory weight being given to evidence supporting and challenging an inherited model. The comparison must be demonstrated, not assumed. |
| **Evidential asymmetry** | EVIDENTIAL / DESCRIPTIVE | A pattern in which one class of causal evidence retains explanatory authority while contrary observed responses are confined to a narrower meaning such as modulation. |
| **Narrative authority** | EVIDENTIAL / METHODOLOGICAL | Credibility granted to an official or clinician-authored account because of its status or coherence rather than independent substantiation. |
| **Presumptive narrative reliability** | EVIDENTIAL / METHODOLOGICAL | The default assumption that an official retrospective account accurately and completely represents disputed history. |
| **Withdrawal of presumptive reliability** | EVIDENTIAL / METHODOLOGICAL | Requiring a disputed narrative proposition to be reconstructed independently from primary sources, chronology and source genealogy. This is targeted, not a declaration that the whole chart is false. |
| **Protective documentation** | DOCUMENTARY / DESCRIPTIVE | Wording capable of strengthening the defensibility of a decision, such as recording understanding, without by itself proving agreement, complete disclosure or absence of dissent. The term does not establish protective intent. |
| **Scope enlargement** | DOCUMENTARY / EVIDENTIAL | A narrow earlier statement later being used to support a broader proposition than its source naturally establishes. |
| **Responsibility displacement** | DOCUMENTARY / STATE-OF-MIND | A change in framing that moves ownership of uncertainty, consequence or regret from a decision-maker toward another actor. The documentary change may be shown without proving why it occurred. |
| **Temporal severance** | DOCUMENTARY / DESCRIPTIVE | A later diagnosis or finding being treated as new or subsequent without an identified reconciliation of its earlier documentary lineage. |
| **Causal hardening** | DOCUMENTARY / DESCRIPTIVE | A conditional hypothesis such as `may`, `perhaps` or `Not clear` later appearing as a more settled explanation without an identified evidential bridge. |
| **Selective invocation** | DOCUMENTARY / EVIDENTIAL | Reliance on the existence or authority of a source or second opinion without equivalent treatment of material content that may qualify the proposition for which it is invoked. |
| **Directionality** | EVIDENTIAL / METHODOLOGICAL | Whether multiple documentary transformations tend in a common evidential direction, such as improving the defensibility of an earlier decision. Directionality alone does not prove motive. |
| **Knowledge sequence** | STATE-OF-MIND / METHODOLOGICAL | A dated reconstruction of what an actor personally knew, what opportunity or contradiction arose and what the actor then did. |
| **Continuity actor** | DESCRIPTIVE / STATE-OF-MIND | A clinician with repeated personal contact whose accumulated knowledge may reduce the explanatory force of institutional fragmentation. |
| **Constructive notice** | STATE-OF-MIND / EVIDENTIAL | Information sufficient to make reconsideration reasonably necessary as a question without proving subjective recognition. Professional-standard implications may require expert evidence. |
| **Actual awareness of the competing model** | STATE-OF-MIND | Express or otherwise sufficiently evidenced recognition by a particular actor of facts or interpretations inconsistent with the inherited explanation. |
| **Failure to reconsider** | GOVERNANCE / CAUSAL | Continued reliance on a prior frame after evidence arguably required reassessment. Whether reconsideration was professionally required is expert-dependent where clinical standards are involved. |
| **Reckless reconstruction** | STATE-OF-MIND | A material retrospective assertion made with inadequate concern for whether available sources support it. This is a hypothesis requiring statement-specific evidence and should not be inferred from inaccuracy alone. |
| **Knowing reconstruction** | STATE-OF-MIND | Constructing or using an account while appreciating that it materially misstates, enlarges or omits the historical source proposition. Requires strong statement-specific evidence. |
| **Series-level risk** | STATE-OF-MIND / METHODOLOGICAL | A pattern across statements sufficient to justify investigation while preserving the rule that knowledge must ultimately be assessed statement by statement. |
| **State-of-mind threshold** | STATE-OF-MIND / METHODOLOGICAL | The evidential point at which accumulated actor-specific knowledge supports investigation beyond ordinary error, inheritance or anchoring. |
| **Source genealogy** | EVIDENTIAL / METHODOLOGICAL | Tracing a later assertion to its genuinely independent evidential origin. |
| **Authority recursion** | GOVERNANCE / EVIDENTIAL | Later institutional decisions appearing independently authoritative while materially depending on the same originating account. Application requires source tracing. |
| **Narrative capture** | GOVERNANCE / EVIDENTIAL | A review process inheriting a clinician-controlled account without demonstrated proposition-level reconstruction. It is not established merely because a later reviewer agrees. |
| **Independent-origin count** | EVIDENTIAL / METHODOLOGICAL | The number of genuinely separate evidential origins supporting a proposition rather than repetitions of one source. |
| **Atomic fact layer** | EVIDENTIAL / METHODOLOGICAL | The smallest proposition that a source directly supports, kept separate from interpretation and inference. |
| **Source-to-prose chain** | EVIDENTIAL / METHODOLOGICAL | `Source → documentary fact → tested proposition → analytical prose`. The missing v0.9 fact register means the intended atomic layer is not presently fully exposed in the repository. |
| **Falsification condition** | EVIDENTIAL / METHODOLOGICAL | Evidence that would materially weaken or defeat a proposition. |
| **Counterfactual integrated-care model** | CAUSAL / CLINICAL | The minimum alternative process that would have retained appropriate structural care while systematically testing neurological relationships. It is not proof of the professional standard or outcome. |
| **Drug commencement versus phenotype treatment** | CLINICAL / DESCRIPTIVE | The distinction between prescribing levodopa and designing/evaluating its use around the disabling foot phenotype and temporal pattern. |
| **Pharmacokinetic and epistemic change** | CLINICAL / DESCRIPTIVE | A change in both medication timing/formulation and in which information is permitted to organise treatment. Used particularly for the 2025 temporal account, subject to source verification. |
| **Informative brain** | GOVERNANCE / METHODOLOGICAL | The controlling, revisable synthesis that organises the repository without replacing its evidential substrate. |
| **Working memory** | GOVERNANCE / METHODOLOGICAL | Specialist and developmental analyses preserving how propositions were generated, tested and revised; not automatically controlling evidence. |

## Usage rules

1. Do not convert vocabulary into fact.
2. Keep state-of-mind terms actor-specific.
3. Keep clinical conclusions bounded and expert-dependent where appropriate.
4. Use neutral labels in counsel/expert-facing filenames where practical.
5. Preserve contrary evidence and falsifiers.
6. Prefer source IDs and atomic propositions; this glossary supplements the source–proposition matrix and does not replace it.

## Provenance

This glossary consolidates terminology developed during the case-analysis process and formalised after the repository reliability review of 13 August 2026. It should be revised whenever a term proves ambiguous, overstates the source record or is superseded by a more precise formulation.
