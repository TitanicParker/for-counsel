# Reader Architecture — Story, Proof, and Patient Voice

**Status:** governing architecture for the second-generation counsel repository  
**Purpose:** ensure that a reader can fully understand the case, inspect the documentation beneath it, and hear Liam's own account without being forced to choose between narrative and evidence.

## The governing principle

This repository is not merely an archive and it is not merely an argument.

It is a place where a reader who knows nothing about the case should be able to enter at the level of human meaning and descend, without losing the thread, all the way to the primary documentary sources.

The repository should therefore make four things intelligible at the same time:

1. **What happened.**
2. **Why it happened.**
3. **What the documents actually support.**
4. **What Liam was trying to communicate while it was happening.**

The governing reader rule is:

> **The reader should never have to choose between hearing the patient and checking the evidence. The repository must make it possible to do both at the same time.**

This is the stitch layer joining the entire project.

---

# 1. The repository has three inseparable layers

## Layer A — Story

The reader needs a coherent causal account before the evidence catalogue can mean anything.

The story is not `a man was misdiagnosed`.

It is:

- an open foot-centred and gait-centred problem enters hospital;
- severe Parkinsonism is rapidly recognised;
- an early senior/departmental levodopa position becomes controlling while the painful-foot/lower-limb phenotype is still developing;
- uncertainty about the feet remains clinically recorded as `Not clear`;
- Podiatry, LTI, diagnosis, work advice and discharge nevertheless create an operative administrative state;
- later PRKN reasoning and post-discharge DaTSCAN material are consolidated into the completed record;
- a reported contemporaneous protest is not preserved while apparent understanding and collective authority are;
- the resulting history is inherited for years;
- the feet keep returning through medication-sensitive, dystonic, OFF, painful, mechanical and mixed phenomena;
- in 2025 the patient's own causal hypothesis is used to alter dopaminergic treatment and produces a major therapeutic/knowledge rupture;
- in 2026 Parkin genetics supplies later etiological coherence to Parkinsonism and dystonia;
- later institutional review must then be examined for whether it independently reconstructed the hinge or inherited the same completed source state.

The story gives the evidence a direction.

Without it, the repository becomes a pile of facts.

---

## Layer B — Proof

Every material narrative proposition must have a route downward.

A reader should be able to disagree with a sentence and immediately find:

- the source record;
- the relevant atomic facts;
- the proposition built from those facts;
- the inference boundary;
- contrary evidence;
- missing sources capable of changing the conclusion.

The architecture remains:

**SOURCE → FACT → PROPOSITION → PROSE**

But the reader should be able to travel in both directions:

**PROSE → PROPOSITION → FACT → SOURCE**

The repository succeeds when the narrative is easy to read but difficult to detach from its evidential roots.

Proof is not an appendix to the story.

It is the underside of the story.

---

## Layer C — Patient voice

Liam must not appear merely as the object described by the medical record.

He is also an evidential observer of:

- his symptoms;
- distinctions between those symptoms;
- what changed over time;
- what medications altered;
- what he understood clinicians to be telling him;
- what he disputed;
- what questions he asked;
- what he was attempting to get investigated;
- what later treatment taught him about the earlier history.

Once the 2017 hinge record is materially impeached as a complete account, Liam's voice has an additional forensic function.

It becomes one of the sources required to reconstruct the clinical and interpersonal state that the institutional record may not have preserved.

The patient is not made infallible.

The institutional record is no longer allowed to be treated as infallible either.

The correct operation is reconstruction from multiple evidential origins.

---

# 2. The patient voice must be presented beside the institutional voice

The repository should not create a sentimental, evidence-detached `Liam's Story` silo.

Instead, at every major hinge the reader should be able to understand five things:

### What the record says
The institutional documentary formulation.

### What Liam was saying
His own description, distinction, question, protest or causal explanation.

### What the institution appears to have understood him to mean
Where the record converts his words into a clinical summary or causal category.

### What remained unresolved
The actual evidential or clinical ambiguity at that point.

### What happened next
The treatment, referral, administrative or documentary consequence.

This five-part structure should become a recurring design principle across the counsel site.

---

# 3. The 2017 hinge is the model example

At the central 2017 transition, the repository should allow the reader to see the following simultaneously.

## What Liam described

He distinguished local foot pain associated with corns/calluses and mechanical exposure from persistent leg/body tension that did not behave identically.

## What that distinction meant at minimum

The symptoms were not phenomenologically identical and did not change together.

## What the clinical record then considered

The leg/foot symptoms, burning, corns and calluses might be completely separate or peripheral.

## What the record actually concluded

`Not clear.`

## What the examination also contained

Dystonic-looking lower-limb posture, abnormal gait, young-onset Parkinsonism, rigidity, bradykinesia, structural foot abnormalities and later dopaminergic imaging abnormality.

## What happened administratively

The feet moved into a Podiatry pathway while Parkinson's disease became the operative neurological and administrative framework.

## What Liam says happened interpersonally

He protested at the bedside against the emerging handling of the feet; four medical students were present.

## What the completed record preserves

Collective clinical authority, treatment rationale and `good understanding` language, but not the reported protest.

That is the case in miniature.

The reader should not have to assemble this comparison manually from separate pages.

---

# 4. The repository must distinguish Liam's words from meanings later assigned to them

A recurrent risk in the record is semantic escalation.

The current working chain is:

**different experiences**  
→ **different problems**  
→ **possibly different causes**  
→ **different pathways**  
→ **pathway distinction inherited as though causal distinction were settled.**

The repository must therefore preserve interrogative and phenomenological language accurately.

For example:

`Liam wondered whether the pain was neuralgic`

must not silently become:

`Liam believed the pain was neuropathic.`

Likewise:

`the foot pain and leg tension were not the same problem`

must not silently become:

`Liam believed the feet were unrelated to Parkinsonism.`

Where institutional wording broadens Liam's meaning, that broadening is itself evidence to be examined.

---

# 5. Liam's voice changes over time and the repository should let the reader hear that development

The patient should not be presented as possessing a finished retrospective theory from 2017 onward.

His evidential value partly lies in the fact that he learns from his own body over time.

The repository should show that development.

## 2017
He distinguishes pain from tension and reports an unresolved problem.

## 2018
He describes major relief from tension and medication-sensitive changes involving the outside-left-foot phenomenon.

## 2020
The clinical record itself calls a lower-limb/foot-throw phenomenon Parkinsonian while separately recognising a local corn.

## 2022
He describes morning feet needing time to `warm up` and asks whether the coronal pain might be neuralgic.

## 2023
He distinguishes pain, clawing, movement architecture, attention required to walk, OFF-like evening failure and Stalevo effects. The clinician records a `misalignment` between their explanatory models.

## 2024
Lyrica improves pain but not toe curling, further separating components by treatment response.

## 2025
He proposes that the morning foot state may reflect overnight absence of Parkinson's medication. That hypothesis is operationalised through altered dopaminergic treatment.

## March 2025 knowledge rupture
The resulting experience changes how he interprets the preceding history and leads directly into formal complaint and demand for reconstruction.

The reader should be able to experience this progression as a sequence of observation and learning rather than as a retrospective patient theory imposed on the past.

---

# 6. The clinicians must remain real actors

The repository is strongest when it explains how the case could happen without flattening clinicians into villains.

The authority map should therefore remain visible:

- Monaghan held the evolving bedside information;
- Counihan's position carried senior local treatment weight;
- Healy occupied an external specialist / neurogenetic role and was expressly invited to advise whether levodopa should remain withheld.

This produces a system question:

> **Did changed patient information travel back through the authority structure after the first answer became controlling?**

The narrative should show what each person knew, what each role allowed them to control, what information travelled between them, and what remains missing.

Mechanism is more persuasive than caricature.

---

# 7. Missing evidence must remain visible inside the story

A reader should not need to leave the narrative to discover that a proposition depends on an absent source.

High-value gaps include:

- complete Complex Case Meeting material;
- native Healy reply and surrounding correspondence;
- Podiatry referral and treatment record;
- teaching/placement material capable of identifying the four student witnesses;
- native EHR version history around the cumulative discharge artefact;
- complete PAR / SIMT source schedule and deliberative material.

Where a source could materially change the reconstruction, the reader should see that fact at the point where the question arises.

The repository should communicate confidence and openness simultaneously.

---

# 8. Contrary evidence belongs inside the same reader experience

The reader should encounter adverse evidence naturally, not in a distant disclaimer page after being persuaded.

The case includes:

- genuine severe Parkinsonism;
- abnormal DaTSCAN;
- real treatment-risk reasoning;
- structural/mechanical foot pathology;
- benefit from local foot measures;
- benefit from Lyrica;
- dyskinesia and impulse-control concerns;
- periods of good function;
- observations that do not fit a simple dopamine-only pain model.

These facts narrow the theory.

They do not destroy the mechanism.

The repository should make the strongest version of the case the one that survives all of them.

---

# 9. Every major counsel page should answer the same reader questions

Each major page should be checked against this reader contract:

1. **What happened here?**
2. **What did Liam experience or say here?**
3. **What did the institutional record make of that?**
4. **What evidence supports the account?**
5. **What remains uncertain or missing?**
6. **How did this change the next decision state?**

If a page answers only the documentary question, it is incomplete.

If it answers only the patient question, it is incomplete.

If it tells a compelling story without source descent, it is incomplete.

If it contains perfect sourcing but no causal understanding, it is incomplete.

---

# 10. The repository's final form

The finished repository should work for several readers at once.

### A new reader
Should understand the case without prior briefing.

### Counsel
Should be able to identify propositions, proof, source gaps, expert questions and legal issues quickly.

### A clinician
Should be able to reconstruct decision states and see which information existed at which time.

### An institutional reviewer
Should be able to distinguish original evidence from inherited summaries and later authority.

### Liam
Should be able to recognise his own experience and meaning rather than see himself reduced to the institutional interpretation of what he said.

The repository succeeds only if these functions coexist.

---

# Governing formulation

> **This repository should allow a reader to enter through the human story, understand the mechanism, hear Liam's own evolving explanation, challenge any proposition, descend to the underlying evidence, see the contrary material and missing sources, and then return to the narrative without losing the case.**

That is the connective architecture for the entire second-generation build.

It is not a separate page theme.

It is the rule by which every future page, proposition, evidence audit and source presentation should be judged.
